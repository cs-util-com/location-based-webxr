      W4 - Radar and list

      The idea. A small radar in the corner answers "where": every station around you, heading-up, the next one in orange. A sheet at the bottom answers "what is left, and what do I choose": its peek is one progress bar and the next station; pulled up it is a row per station, each with its own Skip and Bring back. The same four glyphs mean the same thing in both places.

      2the next station (the nearest, or the one you chose) 3still to find 1found 4skipped (Bring back stays offered)

      Precision, shown not told

      • Code: you are a sharp pin with a ring - "Placed by a code", no number.
      • GPS: you are a soft blue blur as wide as the accuracy on the radar's scale - "GPS - about 8 m".
      • GPS too weak (above 25 m): the blur turns amber and swallows the nearby stations, which fade; nothing is found until it shrinks.
      • No trusted position: the radar freezes and goes grey where it last was, with a "?" for you, no orange station, no distances.
      • No direction yet: stations become rings at their distance - how far, not which way.

      The skip, when nothing vouches for the position

      Any-order tour: "Can't get there?" names no station. It opens the list with a line saying the guide cannot tell which station is nearest, and every remaining row carries its own Skip. The visitor names the station; the untrusted position never does. Fixed-order tour: the order names the station, so the two-tap skip stays exactly as today ("Skip The well - I can't get there") and needs no position.

      Why: skipping "the nearest" by a position nobody vouches for can skip the very station the visitor is standing at, and the radar has just said it does not know where they are. A list makes the choice the visitor's at the cost of one more look.

      What it trades away

      • An instrument is always on screen: the radar takes a corner of the camera and asks the visitor to read a map, if a tiny one.
      • Two places to look (radar up, sheet down) instead of one; the world itself carries little (only the HUD's markers).
      • For a fixed-order tour most of the list is "Later": it is simple, but it says less than a single "Next" line would.

      Text proposals (changed or new)

      • The station line keeps its words but is split: "3 stations to find - nearest" over "The well" and "46 m"; the full sentence stays for screen readers.
      • New: "Guide me here" (tap a row in an any-order tour to choose the next station; today the guide always takes the nearest), shown as "your choice".
      • New: per-row "Skip" and "Bring back"; Bring back stays offered after the 6 s undo, also after the tour is complete (today only within 6 s).
      • New: "Your position is unknown, so the guide can't tell which station is nearest. Which one can't you reach?"
      • New precision labels: "Placed by a code", "GPS - about 8 m", "GPS - about 40 m, too weak", "Position unknown", "Finding your position...", "Paused - tracking lost".
      • "End this story?" is always on the story card (today only when no other station is offered), with "Keep listening" beside its confirm.
      • On the completion card: "3 of 4 found. A skipped station can still be brought back." or "Thank you for walking with us." under the real line.
      • Outside AR: "You left AR. Your progress stays on this phone.", "Back to the tour"; on return: "Welcome back - your progress is kept."
      • Row words: "Found", "Skipped", "Later", "You are here", "Distance unknown", "ahead / to your left / to your right / behind you".
      • The framework's "Tracking lost - slowly look around to let it recover." keeps its words with a hyphen in place of the dash.
