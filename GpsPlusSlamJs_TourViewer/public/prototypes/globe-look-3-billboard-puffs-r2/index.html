Notes: billboard puff clouds, round 2

Controls. Drag: orbit around the point ahead (left and right) and tilt (up and down). Right-drag or two fingers: pan. Wheel or pinch: fly in and out. W A S D: fly, Q and E: down and up. The camera stays between 100 m and 60 km.

Technique. Each cumulus is a cluster of camera-facing puff sprites in three levels of detail, placed once by a tileable coverage field. Every puff's light is baked at build time: a ray toward the sun through the puffs of its own cloud, then over a map of cloud-top heights for the clouds in its way, gives the sunlight it receives (self-shadowing and cloud-on-cloud shadows), a ray upward the sky light it receives, and the optical path toward the sun drives a forward-scattered silver term for thin, backlit puffs. The puff layout lives in a static float texture; per frame only a list of puff numbers is sorted back to front (in a worker) and uploaded. The sea, sky and haze are the glitter-and-haze variant's shading, unchanged, with the puffs' coverage casting the cloud shadows; everything is drawn in linear light and tone-mapped once.

What changed from round 1, and why.

  • The once-a-second change. Cause: each puff's edge noise was seeded by its slot in the instance buffer (gl_InstanceID). The buffer was rebuilt once a second, and as the clouds drift every rebuild culls a few puffs in or out, so every later puff moved to another slot, took another seed and changed its outline: all clouds redrew at once, once a second. Fix: the seed is the puff's own number, the layout never changes after the build, and the sort runs every frame (so the drawing order is always the current one, never a second old). Puffs that shrink below a pixel now fade instead of switching off, and a sprite's circle no longer shows as a thin arc on big near puffs. Checked: with time frozen, two frames 1.5 s apart are bit-identical, and orbiting 20 degrees away and back gives a bit-identical frame (so nothing depends on history); a 0.4 s timeline of screenshots of round 1 shows the cloud pixels jumping to about 2.5 times their drift level once a second, round 2 shows no such spikes.
  • Water. The sea, sky, sun disc, halo and horizon haze are the glitter-and-haze variant's shader, ported as it is (glitter split by pixel footprint, Cox-Munk lobe, Fresnel sky reflection, Chapman haze with the bright horizon band). The puffs and the far cloud layer now use the same haze and the same tone mapping, so far clouds dissolve into the same band. Gone: round 1's hashed glint cells, which flickered and repeated at 3 km.
  • Light inside clouds. Baked per-puff sun and sky visibility (round 1 had none), so the far sides and crevices of a cloud turn blue-grey and clouds shade each other at a low sun. Small backlit clouds get light through them in proportion to how thin they are. The sun colour on clouds is softened toward white, as in round 1, because the haze variant's physical colour made the shadowed parts brown.
  • Free camera and an honest frame meter (fps, median and p95, display cap, GPU timer where the browser has one, and a Measure button).

Tiers. Low (default on a coarse pointer or small screen): 0.75 drawing pixels per CSS pixel, 5 wave octaves, middle puff level to 24 km and smallest to 13 km, puffs to 40 km, 256 px coverage, no lens ghost. High: up to 1.5 pixels per CSS pixel, 8 wave octaves, puff levels to 40 / 22 km, puffs to 54 km, 512 px coverage, faint lens ghost. ?t= freezes time (for still comparisons), ?orbit= orbits slowly at that many degrees a second.

Measured (laptop integrated GPU, Intel Iris Xe, headless Chromium on Direct3D 11, GPU timer per frame; the machine's CPU was fully loaded by other work, so frame-time slopes from the Measure button were noisy and the GPU timer is the number to trust). High tier at 1440x900: about 5 ms (10 km), 8 ms (3 km, densest), 4 ms (30 km). Low tier: 0.6 ms at 390x844 (10 km), 1.1 ms at 844x390 (3 km), 2.5 ms at 1440x900 (10 km). Measure at low tier, 390x844: about 1.1 ms a frame (GPU about 0.5 ms), 15 times inside 16.7 ms. A low-end phone GPU is very roughly 15-25 times slower than this one, which puts low tier at roughly 10-25 ms: not measured on a phone. Software WebGL (headless, relative only): about 500 ms a frame at 390x844 low. The sort takes 4-6 ms a frame in the worker, off the main thread, and lands one or two frames later. The cloud build with the light bake took 1-2.4 s on the loaded CPU (about 5 million sphere tests and 4 million map steps), once at load.

Self-check against the reference and the review.

  • Clouds still read as clusters of soft balls, most of all the small ones made of a few detail puffs. The baked light is one value per puff, so a sunlit puff can sit next to a shadowed one with a visible step; a light gradient across each puff toward its lit side would soften it (hours).
  • Crevices and shadow sides are blue-grey now but much shallower than in the reference: stronger contrast made the clouds read as dirty balls, so it was kept mild. The 3 km view looks hazier and greyer than round 1, because the haze variant's air is denser near the sea and the view looks toward a low sun at the shadow sides.
  • Small backlit clouds in the glitter path are lighter than in round 1 but still not silvery: the light through them is a thickness guess, not scattering.
  • The far flat cloud layer (beyond about 35-54 km) is still flat and cream-coloured toward the sun, and its hand-over band is visible from 30 km. Cloud coverage repeats every 120 km.
  • Stability: a still camera is exact, and the drawing order follows the camera every frame. While orbiting, two puffs at almost the same depth can still swap order between frames; I could not see it in the orbit pairs, but it is the one remaining source of a jump. Order-independent blending would remove it and lose the opaque cores.
  • The build freezes the page for about a second at load on a laptop, and likely several seconds on a phone (not measured); building in a worker behind a short "building clouds" message is the fix (hours).
  • The free camera orbits left and right around the point ahead and tilts in place; below the cloud base, puffs are cut flat and the far layer is a plain underside. The linear-light target needs half-float colour buffers; without them the page falls back to 8 bits, untested.
