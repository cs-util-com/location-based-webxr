Preparing cloud noise...
Notes

Technique. The same raymarched cumulus shell as the stable tiered variant (half-resolution cloud pass, fixed blue-noise ray offsets, no history), with a new weather field. A 2048 km region map (512x512, 4 km texels) stands in for a real weather map, as the globe's cloud map does: it sets coverage, cloud size, base height and a lifecycle phase that vary over tens of km. Inside it a 46 km dome tile carries three generations of cumulus domes, a third of a cycle apart; each grows from its core, holds and shrinks again over a 15-minute cycle, so single clouds are born and dissolve while the field stays the same on average. The shape noise rises slowly as the billowing.

Why this field technique. The globe's own tiling breaks the repeat with three lookups per sample (hex tiles blended), and live noise would need several octaves per sample. Here the repeat is broken by one extra lookup per step: the region map is read once per primary step and shared by the empty-space skip and the density, and it warps the dome tile by up to 3 km, switches between two scales of it (46 km and 74 km, which never line up) and thins it into clusters, so the 46 km period does not survive. Measured over a 240 km window: the tiled field correlates with itself shifted by 46 km at 0.998; the random field at 0.07-0.19, the same as at any other shift.

What changed from the stable tiered variant, and why.

  • The weather field (above). The bases now vary between 1.1 and 1.4 km and the tops between 2.3 and 3.3 km, with the cloud depth following the cloud size, so big clusters are taller. Clouds stay separate with flat bases: the dome threshold and the height gradient are unchanged.
  • The shape change. The stable variant's only motion is wind drift and a slow rise of the noise (1.5 and 4 m/s); its clouds never change their outline. Here each generation's weight is sin2 of its phase, and a dome is cut at a level that follows the weight, so a young cloud is its dome's core and a grown one the whole dome. The billowing rises at 3 and 6 m/s. The phase and the offsets are computed in double precision in JavaScript and wrapped there, so there is no precision drift over hours.
  • Denser clouds. Extinction 55 to 75 per km, and the density is shaped as d(2-d), so a thin 0.2 becomes 0.36 while cores stay at 1: edges read less see-through, the backlit rim (thin edges) and the sky-lit shadow sides are kept. The far stand-in's opacity 0.92 to 0.97.
  • The cheap tier's far sea. Six wave octaves from 32 m to 1024 m instead of five from 16 m: the long waves are kept, the short ones (which only alias at 30 km) dropped. Cost below.
  • The preset views are picked by mirroring the shader's field in JavaScript: random-field candidates are ranked by region coverage, the best 60 mirrored in full (about 20 ms), and the one nearest a target fill is chosen, kept out of clouds at 3 km.

Toggles (buttons in the bar, or in the URL).

  • ?field=random|tiled - the region-sized field (default) or the 46 km tile of the stable variant. Tiled with the shape change off is the stable variant's cloud field (with the denser clouds).
  • ?evolve=1|0 - shape change on (default) or off. With the tiled field it runs on the dome tile alone, without the region map.
  • ?timescale=1|10|60 - how fast the shape change runs (0 holds it). The wind drift and the sea keep real time, so a time-lapse shows the change, not a gale.
  • ?steps=N - the primary step limit, for the sweep; ?sea=old - the earlier cheap-tier waves; ?tier=low|high, ?view=10km|30km|3km.

Tiers, as in the stable variant. low: canvas at 0.75 of the CSS pixels, clouds at half of that, up to 24 primary steps, one light sample, no erosion detail, six wave octaves. high: canvas at device pixels (at most 1.5x), clouds at half, up to 96 steps, five light samples, erosion detail near the camera, eight wave octaves. Each light sample reuses its primary step's region lookup.

Measured (GPU timer, laptop integrated GPU, D3D11, at a phone's pixel count: 412x915 CSS pixels at 2.625, so low draws 309x686 and high 618x1372; each value is the mean over four camera headings, the three modes interleaved, two repeats; the floor is the half range of the repeats). "Free" here means within the floor or under 10 %.

  • Random field against the tile, same steps, shape change off. Low: +4 % at 10 km, +9 % at 30 km, +10 % at 3 km (floors 4-7 %); compared at the same cloud cover (the random presets show more cloud), +0 %, +8 %, +8 %: free. High (two runs): +17 to +30 %, and +4 to +26 % at the same cover (floors 3-19 %): not free.
  • Shape change on top of the random field. Low: +15 %, +17 %, +4 % at 10, 30 and 3 km (0.38 to 0.43 ms at 10 km); high: +22 to +25 %, +14 to +16 %, +2 to +6 % (3.3 to 4.2 ms at 10 km). Not free at 10 and 30 km, free at 3 km.
  • Step sweep (10 km, against the tile at the same steps). Low 16 / 24 / 40 steps: random +23 % / +8 % / -8 %, change +8 % / +19 % / +14 % (floors 3-16 %). High 48 / 96 / 160: random +35 % / +33 % / +23 %, change +51 % / +41 % / +71 % (floors 6-15 %). At 30 km, low: random +8 % / -11 % / +2 %, change +33 % / +31 % / +15 %. The low tier's verdict for the random field flips only at 16 steps at 10 km, where its view held 28 % cloud against the tile's 18 %.
  • The cheap tier's sea, six long octaves against the earlier five short ones: +12 % at 30 km (floor 13-16 %), -0.5 % at 10 km: within the floor.
  • Memory. The tile is 512x512 RGBA8, 1 MB (1.33 MB with mipmaps); the random field reads the dome tile and the region map of the same size instead, 2.7 MB with mipmaps against 1.33 MB, on top of the shared 3D noises. The laptop GPU has a large cache and hides the cost of a second lookup; on a phone the extra texture traffic per sample is memory bandwidth, so expect the random field's cost at high to be larger there, not smaller.
  • The machine was shared with other work: repeated runs of the same mode drift by up to 20 % between sessions, which is why only the interleaved differences are quoted.
  • Bakes at load: about 1.3 s together on a desktop.

Stability (Playwright, laptop GPU, 844x390, frames rendered from the clock hooks; "changed" is a pixel off by more than 8 of 255, a "pop" one that changes and changes back within three frames).

  • Clock frozen, still camera, two frames 1.5 s apart: identical, 0 pixels, in all three modes and both tiers.
  • One frame of running time at x1 (wind, sea and shape change): high at 10 km 0.05 % tiled, 0.10 % random, 0.30 % random with the shape change; low at 30 km 0.14 %, 0.32 %, 0.45 %. The shape change alone (wind and sea held) at x60 changes 0.9 % (high) and 1.9 % (low) of the pixels per frame, of which 0.08 % and 0.25 % are pops; at x1 that is 60 times less.
  • One frame of a slow orbit (2 deg/s), clock frozen: high at 10 km 1.1 %, 1.5 %, 3.4 %; low at 30 km 3.9 %, 6.1 %, 8.0 %, mostly single cloud pixels at the small far clouds (see the self-check).

Controls. Drag to orbit around the point in the middle of the view; right-drag or two fingers to pan; wheel or pinch to fly in and out; W A S D to move, Q and E to fly down and up. The camera stays between about 100 m and 60 km.
