Narration: more particle looks

The second round of the narration circle's look. The first round compared three looks, and the plume was the prettiest and drew the most attention, so this page keeps the plume and adds four more particle looks of the same family. Everything else (the circle, the card, the ambient sound, the walk-through) is as before. Everything here is a throwaway draft; the looks live in kind.js, which a real app would import as it is.

The five looks

  • Plume (from the first round, unchanged): a column of soft light rises in the middle and widens a little as it goes, under a name plate above head height.
  • Embers: sparks rise from the middle, slow down and drift away with a gusting wind, flickering and cooling from cream to deep orange, drawn as short streaks. It reads like a small fire. Weakness: the drift makes the column lean, so from one side it looks off centre; the streaks are thin from far away.
  • Helix: two strands of motes wind slowly up around the middle, narrowing toward the top, with now and then a mote that lets go. The calmest and the most ordered. Weakness: it can read as a technical symbol rather than as "something to listen to", and from straight above the strands collapse to a ring.
  • Fireflies: a loose swarm over the whole circle, each blinking on its own. As you come near they gather into a slow ball above the middle and blink together. It is the only look that answers your approach. Weakness: from far away a loose swarm is faint, and the gathering is the slowest change of the five (about 2 s).
  • Rings: like sonar, rings of light leave the ground in the middle, widen and rise. While the voice plays their pace follows its loudness: quick while it is loud, slow in its pauses. Weakness: the most "interface-like" of the five; rings seen edge-on at eye height are thin lines.

What to try on a phone

  • Under More, Stand this far from the middle puts you at 3 m (inside the circle), 8 m and 15 m. Switch the five looks at each distance: a look that reads at one distance can fail at another.
  • Fireflies: walk in slowly from 15 m and watch them gather. Rings: tap play inside the circle and watch the pace follow the voice.
  • The dark backdrop, and the name plate off: which look still says "walk into this" without words?
  • Walk through works as before: in, play, out, back, stop, and on to the ambient sound.

What it does (unchanged)

  • The narration is one circle on the ground, 6 m in radius, with a dashed rim and a glow in the middle. Outside it nothing is on the screen; inside, a card opens with the title, play or pause, a scrub bar and the sentence being spoken.
  • It plays only on a tap. Leaving pauses it and closes the card; coming back carries on. Once stopped with a tap, it stays off until tapped again.
  • The warm glow nearby is an ambient sound, in the editor's own colour for it. It plays by itself within earshot (louder toward its middle) and is lowered to 30 % while the narration plays; its glow turns quieter with it.
  • Edit shows the same objects, still and silent, with their fields as an inspector.

Cost

  • Every look stays inside the first round's limits: three draw calls for the narration (ground, particles, name plate; two with the plate off), the particles in one instanced draw of camera-facing squares, no post-processing, no shadows, no render target of its own, no texture except the plate's.
  • Particles per look: plume 200, embers 240, helix 240, fireflies 90 (larger, softer squares), rings 288 (six rings of 48).
  • All motion is computed on the graphics card from a clock and a few numbers; the per-frame work in JavaScript is the same for every look (the distance, the state, five numbers handed to the shader).
  • More, Measure all looks shows each look from 8 m: its draws, its particles, the narration's per-frame update time (mean and worst of 120 frames) and the frame rate. The readout at the top right shows the same for the current look.
  • What it does not measure: the graphics card's time. Soft squares cost by the screen area they cover, so the embers (stretched) and fireflies (large) cost most when you stand inside them. None of this was measured on a phone yet.

The values it rests on

  • Circle radius 6 m; the card opens at the rim and closes 1 m past it.
  • Fireflies gather by distance from the middle: not at all from 5 m past the rim, half at 2 m past it, fully from 1 m inside it, eased over about a second.
  • Rings: pace 0.55 in the voice's pauses up to about 3 at its loudest (1 is idle), following it within about 0.2 s.
  • Embers: wind toward one side of the item, gusting between about 0.3 and 1, up to 1.7 m of drift at the top.
  • Ambient: heard up to 14 m, full volume within 4 m, volume 0.6.

Blunt self-check

  • The looks were judged over a plain grey or dark ground, not over a camera image in sunlight, where the soft parts (the fireflies' halos, the helix's thin top) will be weaker.
  • The embers' wind is in the item's own frame, not the real wind, and it always blows the same way.
  • The voice is a generated speech-like tone pattern, never real speech; the rings' pace follows its loudness, which real speech will vary less evenly.
  • A browser lets sound start only after a tap, so the ambient sound starts with your first tap on the page.
