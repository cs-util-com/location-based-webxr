Make your own tour, in three steps.

No code, no sign-up, no account. The route below takes you through it, one short step at a time.

  1. 1

    Walk and place

    Take your phone to the place, walk the route you have in mind and tap to put each station where it belongs.

  2. 2

    Add text and pictures

    Write what visitors should know at each station and add your photos. Change the words later, on a computer if you like.

  3. 3

    Share a code

    Print the tour's code and hang it where the walk begins. Visitors scan it, and the tour opens in their browser.

Walking and placing need an Android phone with Chrome (AR support). Choosing storage, printing and editing work on any computer. This phone can do every step. This device cannot show AR: do the first steps here and the walk on the phone.

    Notes on this prototype
    
    
      
    Direction
    
      
    The current setup page in its light paper look, extended rather than replaced: the intro picture moves again, a looping strip shows what a visitor sees, "See an example" opens the made-up Mill Park tour, and a calm three-step "Make your own tour" path leads into the unchanged six-step route.
    
      
    Why it should work for this audience
    
      
    A guide, a teacher or a town office never has to leave the page they would build on: first what it is (the intro and the strip), then a finished example to look at, then three plain steps in their own words, then the route that does it. Nothing here asks the reader to know what AR or a QR code is before they see one.
    
      
    Load-bearing details (check a build against these)
    
      
      
        
    • Intro art: the L5 keyframes hPin, hLabel, hPhoto, hDots, one 7 s loop; transform-box fill-box, origin 50% 100%. The current app has the classes but no rules for them, so its picture stands still.
      • 
        
    • Reduced motion: no autoplay anywhere. The intro shows its final frame (pin, label, photo tilted -6 degrees, route drawn); the strip shows all four frames side by side, unlit, with no progress bar; the preview's walk button is hidden.
      • 
        
    • Strip: 2 x 2 frames on a phone, 4 in a row from 720 px or in landscape; one active frame at a time, 3.2 s each (?frame= changes it), the others at 60% opacity; it pauses off screen and when the tab is hidden, and has a Pause button.
      • 
        
    • Spacing set here, not inherited: lede 10 px, requirement line 16 px, reasons 20 px below; sections 46 px on top, 18 px side gutter; step cards 16 px apart with a dotted route behind them.
      • 
        
    • Copy: the Android line sits directly under the lede, before the reasons; the four reasons are the current page's honest ones.
      • 
        
    • The example map is the stage map itself, cloned, so the two can never drift apart.
      • 
      
    
      
    Measured cost (desktop, a loaded machine; not phone numbers)
    
      
      
        
    • JS: all inline, 38.6 kB, 11.8 kB gzip; no library, no CDN, no WebGL. The whole page is 114 kB, 30.2 kB gzip.
      • 
        
    • Frames at 390 x 844 in headless Chromium on a loaded desktop: 5 s idle and 5 s scroll, worst frame 17 ms. With the CPU slowed 4 times, 50 to 67 ms for single frames; 6 times, 50 to 100 ms, with one run at 150 ms. One long frame right after load (170 to 650 ms) also appears with the unchanged L5 page. Not phone numbers.
      • 
      
    
      
    Known weaknesses
    
      
      
        
    • The page gets long: on a phone the route starts about three and a half screens down. The hero button skips straight to it.
      • 
        
    • The three plain steps are not the setup order (the poster is printed before the walk in the route); they describe what a maker does, not the order the route does it in.
      • 
        
    • No 3D: AR is explained by drawings only. If a first-time visitor needs to see depth to "get" AR, this variant cannot show it.
      • 
        
    • On a phone in portrait, the moving intro picture sits below the first screen, as in L5.
      • 
      
    
      
    Parameters it rests on
    
      
      
        
    • Strip frame length 3.2 s. At 2.4 s the label and the card finish only about half a second before the next frame lights, too quick to read; at 4.5 s it reads calmly, but one round takes 18 s.
      • 
        
    • Intro loop 7 s, as in L5.
      • 
        
    • First screen: at 390 x 844 the Android line ends at 335 px and the first button at 515 px; at 360 x 740, 358 and 539 px. In landscape (844 x 390) the button ends at 383 px of 390: a landscape screen under about 385 px tall pushes it below the first screen.
