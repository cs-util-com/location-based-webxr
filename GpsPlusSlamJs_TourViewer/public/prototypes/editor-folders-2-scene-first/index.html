Folders, scene first

You group items where you see them: in the 3D view. Select a few items in place, press Group these, and they become a folder: a soft frame on the ground with a name tag. The station list on the left only mirrors what happened.

A folder behaves like a group in a drawing tool. The first click on any of its items selects the whole folder, which moves, turns and scales as one. A second click (or a double-click) goes inside the folder: everything else fades, the frame turns solid amber and a bar at the top says where you are. Now you edit one item alone. Esc, a click outside the frame, or Leave folder takes you out again.

Why this direction is good

  • Things that belong together usually stand together (a text and a picture beside a model). Selecting them where they stand is the shortest way, and you see what you pick.
  • It is the grouping everyone already knows from slide and drawing tools: box-select, group, click to select the group, double-click to go in.
  • The frame shows before any drag what will move together, so a folder can never move a hidden item by surprise.
  • The list stays the precise tool (order, drag in and out, items far apart), but nothing has to start there.

The strongest case against it

  • Selecting in 3D is fragile: a box in a tilted view catches items behind others, items far apart need a huge box, and overlapping frames get cluttered.
  • "First click selects the folder" adds one click to every item edit, and a tester who misses it thinks the item cannot be picked.
  • On a phone, long-press is invisible until someone explains it, and it competes with turning the view with one finger.
  • On a desktop the select tool takes the left mouse button, so turning the view moves to the right button or Space: a habit change for anyone used to the current 3D view.

What to try

  1. Drag a box on the ground around the stone, the text card and the picture (or Shift-click them).Long-press the stone until the ring pulses, then tap the text card and the picture. Press Group these.
  2. Drag the folder's middle disc to move it, the knob on its stalk to turn it, a corner dot to scale it. Watch the list: the folder is there too.
  3. Click the picture again (second click) to go inside the folder. Move the picture alone; the frame follows its items. Press Esc to leave.
  4. In the list, drag the text card's grip out of the folder (onto the station row or between free items). Try dropping it on the story: "Not possible here".
  5. Select the folder and press Duplicate, then Ungroup the copy, then Undo a few times.

One station, nothing saved. Right-drag (or Space + drag) turns the view, the wheel zooms, middle-drag pans.
