      Design note - W3

      The guide speaks

      The tour's guide walks with you, and every status is something the guide says, at the moment it matters; your answers are reply chips under it. Between those moments the guide falls quiet, and the strip under its face keeps the essentials for a glance: where to go, how far, and how much the guide trusts your position. Each station is a chapter: arriving shows its title, and the guide hands over to the station's own speaker until the story ends.

      Reading it at a glance

      • The ring round the guide is the trust. Teal and solid with a code mark: a code placed you, good to a step, so no number. White: GPS, and the strip says "about 8 m". Amber and broken: GPS too weak to guide. Grey dots and a question mark: no trusted position.
      • The distance follows the trust. Plain metres with a code, "about" with GPS, no number at all when the GPS is too weak or the position is unknown - and no arrow when there is no position to point from.
      • The lamps in the scene are the guide's: one over the next station (fixed order), or over the nearest five still to find (any order). They flicker dimly when the GPS is too weak and go out when the guide has lost you. Off screen, a lamp waits at the screen's edge.
      • The chapter strip at the top: found, next, skipped and left; a tap lists the chapters.

      When the guide speaks

      Only at moments that change what you do: AR starting and the camera's tracking, the code seen, a change of trust, the way to a new chapter, arriving, a story's end, the skip suggestion, leaving and coming back, the end. Never because a distance changed. After about 8 s the line folds away into the strip; a tap on the guide or "Tell me again" brings it back.

      What it trades away

      • While the guide speaks, the bubble covers the lower part of the camera.
      • More words than a bare display: a visitor who knows the tour reads a sentence where a number would do. The strip is the shortcut.
      • A character has a tone. Each tour needs a guide (or a neutral default like this one), and a cheerful voice may not suit every subject.
      • The replies are a small fixed menu; anything outside it needs another way in.
      • More text to write and translate.

      The skip when nothing vouches for the position (any order)

      The guide never names a "nearest" it cannot measure, and the skip stays offered. "I can't get there" answers: "I can't tell where you are, so I won't guess which one is closest. Which can't you reach?", with one "Skip <title>" reply per station still to find, in the tour's order, and "Show me a code instead". Still two taps; the visitor, who can see where they stand, makes the choice the guide cannot. In a fixed-order tour the order names the station, so the skip names it as usual. The two-minute suggestion is not made without a distance, which sizes it. The same rule holds while the GPS is too weak (a proposal: today the skip follows the nearest station by that position).

      Texts - today's, and the guide's (proposals)

      The reply labels for the skip, the undo and ending a story are today's texts unchanged. The guide's lines replace the station line and the status sentence; the strip carries the short form. A station title is used as written, except that a leading "The" is lower-cased inside the guide's sentences.

      Not covered here

      • The station's real story content (photos, audio, choices) is faked as a figure, a sign and a few lines.
      • Branch-order tours, quizzes and a tour that is not finished yet.
      • Walking is sped up in "Play the walk"; the two-minute skip clock is fast-forwarded.
