station-blocks-6-primitives - notes

3D primitives rebuilding what is gone: the castle keep over the ruined wall in the north-west corner of the market square.

Page: index.html (one file, three.js r185 from unpkg via the prompt's import map, nothing else loaded). Screenshots: shots/. Check script: tools/shoot.mjs (drives the page only through window.__stationBlock).

1. The model as built, and what I would change

One record, the same in every view:

  • label: "The castle keep"
  • era: "c. 1340 - 1560"
  • parts: ten entries of { shape, dims, pose, material }
    • box {w, h, d}, cylinder {r, h}, cone {r, h}, roof {kind: "hip" | "gable", w, d, h}, wall {points: [[x, z], ...], h, t} (an open polyline, mitred corners), arch {w, h, t, openingW, springH} (a wall piece with a round-headed opening)
    • pose {x, y, z, yaw}, block-local metres and degrees; y is the part's bottom
    • material: "stone" | "timber" | "plaster" | "#rrggbb"
    • the example: the four walls as one polyline open at the gate, the arch in the gap, a stone panel over it, three slit windows (dark colour boxes), a timber gallery, a hip roof, a plaster stair turret with a slate cone
  • The block's own pose (place, turn, size) is the envelope every block has, not a field of this model. exportModel() returns { model, view, pose, label, era, parts }, 1.3 KB of JSON and no media file.

Fields I would add or drop:

  • Add certainty per part ("known" | "likely" | "guess"). The one I would fight for. A reconstruction is only honest if the guessed roof can look different from the surviving footprint; every view here could use it (ghost fainter, outline dashed, footprint unfilled, solid desaturated).
  • Add name per part (optional, short). The editor's list reads "box" six times; a creator needs "south wall", "gate", "gallery".
  • Add remainsHeightM on the block. The solid view fades its base and the rise view starts above the remains; here both use a fixed 1.2 m, which is right for this ruin only.
  • Keep roof as one shape with kind; the pitch follows from h.
  • Drop nothing. cone could be a cylinder with a top radius of 0, but a creator looks for "cone".
  • Not added on purpose: openings cut into walls (booleans), per-face materials, image textures. Each makes the creator's job and the app's code much bigger. The cost is that a door in a wall must be built as "wall, arch, wall", which the creator does by hand.
2. The views
  • Solid (required). The keep in stone, timber, plaster and slate, standing on the real stumps. Its lowest 1.3 m fades out, so the real remains carry the base and a placement error shows as a soft overlap instead of a wall hanging in the air or sunk into the paving. A soft contact shadow grounds it.
    • Fields: all six (parts[].shape/dims/pose/material, label, era).
    • Why a visitor likes it: the building, at full height, where it stood. The most "wow" of the six.
    • Weakest: 1 m of error is still visible at the gate, where the real gap and the virtual arch disagree. It hides the real ruin and, in a crowded square, the people behind it (no occlusion: a 17 m wall draws over a person standing in front of it). Generated textures read as "model" more than "stone" in hard sun.
  • Ghost (required). Smoked glass: a dark translucent body (measured: a near face renders at about 142 of 255 against a sky of about 215), light rims where surfaces turn away, faint stone courses and timber boards, front surface only (a depth pre-pass hides the back walls), and a slow band of light rising through it.
    • Fields: shape, dims, pose; material only as a faint pattern (a colour is ignored); label, era.
    • Why: the visitor sees both the real ruin through it and the size of what is gone. It forgives error best after the outline.
    • Weakest: in sun against a bright sky the rims wash out; the dark body is what keeps it visible. It reads as a screen effect more than a building.
  • Outline (required). Feature edges only: a warm white core over a dark halo, hidden edges dashed and faint. Cylinders and cones get their rims plus two silhouette lines recomputed each frame, instead of sixteen facet lines.
    • Fields: shape, dims, pose; label, era. Material is not used.
    • Why: the lightest and calmest; the camera image stays almost untouched, and a 1 m offset looks like a drawing slightly off, not like a broken model.
    • Weakest: from inside the footprint it is a cage of lines. The halo is what makes it legible outdoors; without it the lines vanish on a pale facade.
  • Rise (suggested, built). On entering the circle the keep grows out of the remains in 7.5 s, a warm seam marking the growing edge and the finished outline faint ahead of it; the label arrives at the end. "Rise again" replays it.
    • Fields: all; pose.y and the heights decide what appears first.
    • Why: it explains without words that the ruin is the bottom of this.
    • Weakest: it plays once per entry, and a visitor who walks in looking at the map misses it. On the tenth station the seam is a gimmick.
  • Footprint (suggested, built). The plan inlaid on the paving: walls and turret as hatched dark bands with a light edge, raised parts (gallery, roof, caps) as dashed outlines, the gate threshold dashed, dashed height marks up each wall corner, and a low fading curtain of light along the walls so the plan reads at a grazing angle.
    • Fields: shape, dims, pose, label, era; pose.y decides filled (on the ground) or dashed (raised). Material is not used: stone, timber and plaster draw the same plan.
    • Why: it never hides anything, survives any error, and in the editor's bird's-eye view it is a plan drawing.
    • Weakest: from eye height at 20 m a plan on the ground is a sliver whatever its design; the curtain and the height marks are what make it findable, and the visitor has to walk up to it to read it (its screenshot stands about 10 m from the nearest wall, the others about 24 m from the keep's centre). On busy cobbles it competes with the real joints.
  • Engraving (my own, unlisted). The keep drawn as a page from an old town chronicle: cream paper faces, sepia hatching by the light (one diagonal in half-shade, cross-hatching in shade, horizontal strokes in deep shadow), stone courses and staggered joints, timber boards, ink edges and silhouettes.
    • Fields: all; material picks the pattern and a colour's darkness sets the hatch density (the slate roof comes out cross-hatched).
    • Why: it matches the story (a building known only from drawings), it is calm, and it stays legible in sun because it is dark ink on light paper.
    • Weakest: at 25 m the hatching approaches the pixel size and fades to a flat tone by design, so far away it is a cream model with ink edges. It is a style choice, and next to a photo block it may look twee.
    • Inspiration: the general look of woodcut and copper-plate town views; the technique (world-space line masks with screen-space antialiasing, tone by light) is generic.
3. What each view costs

Frame times below are from this page's readout in headless Chromium with SwiftShader, i.e. SOFTWARE rendering on the CPU at 1440x900. They are not phone numbers and say only how the views compare to each other. "cpu" is the JavaScript time of one frame (update plus issuing the draw calls), which is the part that does transfer to a phone roughly.

  • Solid: frame 198 ms mean / 354 ms max, cpu 1.1 / 2.9 ms
  • Ghost: frame 211 ms mean / 546 ms max, cpu 1.0 / 2.4 ms
  • Outline: frame 156 ms mean / 331 ms max, cpu 0.9 / 1.3 ms
  • Rise: frame 258 ms mean / 594 ms max, cpu 1.3 / 2.9 ms
  • Footprint: frame 291 ms mean / 721 ms max, cpu 0.9 / 1.3 ms
  • Engraving: frame 161 ms mean / 475 ms max, cpu 7.9 / 84.7 ms
  • Creator: frame 302 ms mean / 492 ms max, cpu 1.3 / 2.5 ms
  • Editor: frame 146 ms mean / 288 ms max, cpu 1.0 / 1.5 ms

The machine was shared with other browser runs during the run, so the frame column is noise on top of software rendering; the cpu column is the one to compare. Every view's cpu mean is around 1 ms; the single maxima above about 3 ms are one-off shader compiles and texture uploads on the first frames after a switch.

  • Media size: none for any view. The record is about 1.3 KB of JSON. The three material textures are generated by the app at start (three 512x512 canvases, about 4 MB of GPU memory with mipmaps, shared by every block of this model in the tour).
  • Draw calls (the phone cost that matters): Solid 10 meshes + 1 shadow; Ghost 10 depth pre-pass + 10 glass + 2 line sets; Outline 10 pre-pass + 5 line sets; Rise 10 pre-pass + 10 solid + 2 line sets; Footprint about 8 fills + 1 curtain + 3 line sets; Engraving 10 + 3 line sets. Triangles are under 3 000 for the whole keep. None of this is near a phone's 50 ms budget; the risk is overdraw of a 17 m translucent wall filling the screen (Ghost), which a phone handles but a cheap one may not at full resolution.
  • Creator's minutes: I wrote the ten parts of this keep as data in about 15 minutes with the sizes decided; I did not time an editor session, and my estimate for a creator in the editor is 20-40 minutes for ten parts (unmeasured). Without a survey of the remains most of that time goes into measuring, not into the tool. The phone is for checking and nudging, not for assembling ten parts. Choosing a view costs nothing once the parts exist: every view is drawn from the same parts with no extra input.
4. The creator's controls
  • Phone (creator mode, at the anchor in AR). Six large buttons along the bottom:
    • View: the six views as chips.
    • Parts: a shape palette (tap to add one in front of the gate); tap a part to pick it, drag it, and on release it snaps to a 0.1 m grid, to the sides of its neighbours (within 0.35 m) and falls onto the first part below its centre (or the ground). Material swatches, turn 15 degrees, delete.
    • Turn: the whole block, a slider and 5 degree steps.
    • Size: 0.5 to 1.5, with 1.00 marked as the true size (a reconstruction should stay at 1.00).
    • Text: label and era.
    • Done: closes the sheets and confirms with a toast.
    • Without the Parts sheet, dragging the keep moves the whole block on the ground.
  • Desktop (editor mode). The station's blocks on the left with this block's parts as a list and a shape palette. The inspector on the right: label, era, view, the block's place (x, z, turn, size); for the picked part its shape, its dims as numbers (a wall's points as "x, z" lines), pose, material (stone, timber, plaster, a colour picker), Snap down, Duplicate, Delete, and the size of the stored JSON. In the view: Move handles (x and z, 0.1 m steps; on release the same snapping as on the phone), Turn (yaw only, 15 degree steps) and Whole block. The footprint plan is always drawn on the ground in the editor, so the plan reads from above whichever view is picked.
  • Per view: nothing to set. A view is chosen, not tuned. The fade height should become the block field remainsHeightM.
5. Blunt self-check
  • Rules and context points the page breaks or bends:
    • The footprint screenshots stand about 10 m from the nearest wall, the other views about 24 m from the keep's centre; from 24 m the footprint is a sliver. That is a finding about the view, not a fair comparison.
    • The label is a screen-space chip anchored to a world point, not world-scaled text; it stays readable at any distance but does not shrink with distance.
    • There is no occlusion of real people or objects (the page has no depth from the scene); Solid and Engraving draw over anyone standing in front of the keep. In the app that needs depth sensing or a rule to fade near people.
    • Solid's faded base is a transparent pass; where two faded faces overlap, their order can flip (visible only in the bottom 1.3 m, and only through the gate).
    • Snapping is simple: side snapping uses bounding boxes (a turned part snaps by its box, not its faces), and support is one ray from the part's centre, so a part overhanging an edge falls to the ground if its centre is past it.
    • A wall polyline cannot be closed (first point equal to last); this keep's ring is open at the gate, which works here only because the gate is where the opening is.
  • Drop first: Rise, as a view. It is an entrance, not a look: it belongs as a block option (entrance: "rise" | "fade") that plays before Solid, Ghost or Engraving. Kept as a view, it doubles the Solid view's code path for a 7 s effect.
  • Proudest of: Engraving. It is the only view that does not look like a game asset, it is the most legible in sunlight (dark ink on light paper), it uses the material field more than any other view, and it suits a building known from drawings. Close second: the faded base of the Solid view, a one-line shader change that makes 1 m of placement error look intended.
