About this prototype

Direction

The whole hero is the view through a phone held up on site: the place behind it stays plain, the phone shows it with the tour in it, and scrolling turns the same view into the maker's side (placing the station) and then the printed code.

Why it should work for this audience

  • A non-developer understands "AR" fastest by seeing it: a picture of a real place with calm cards in it, not a word or a diagram.
  • The backdrop outside the phone has no tour in it; inside it does. When the phone moves, the station stays on its spot: the one property of AR worth explaining, shown rather than told.
  • The maker's side is the same spot, the same phone: "you make it by standing there" needs no further explanation.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • First screen at 390x844: kicker, headline, the phone, the "Make one yourself, without code" line, the Android line and both buttons, without scrolling.
  • The phone dock is sticky and full-bleed on a phone; captions scroll in the space under it; the scene switches when a caption's top passes the middle of that space.
  • Magic window: the phone moves (translate and roll), the screen content counter-moves by the inverse transform, so the world inside lines up with the plain world outside. Status bar, top pill and sheets move with the phone; cards, picture, marker and arrow stay with the world.
  • A framing rectangle (world x 290 to 730, y 250 to 760) is always fully on the screen; the viewBox is fitted to it for every screen shape. The marker, card and picture sit inside it.
  • The phone turns sideways when the page is a phone held sideways (landscape, height up to 560 px); then the sheets become side panels.
  • Each scene plays one short entry (about 2 to 3 s) and a gentle 5 s sway, then holds still; nothing loops. Reduced motion: the final state of every scene, no sway, no glide.
  • AR content looks like the app's: light cards (rgba 251,249,243), serif titles, red only for the station and arrows. Screen chrome is dark and translucent like the AR overlay.
  • Paragraph and list margins are zeroed here too, and every gap is set explicitly, so a port into the design system keeps them.

Technique

Layered SVG and HTML with CSS transforms, no WebGL and no library. A camera-feed look needs soft light, haze and grain, which flat gradients do well and low-poly 3D does not; the AR content needs crisp type, which HTML gives for free. The only motion is transforms on four composited layers. The no-WebGL floor is the page itself.

Measured cost (desktop machine under other load, not a phone)

  • JS: inline only, about 19 kB raw, 6.5 kB gzip; no CDN script, no WebGL, no canvas. The whole page is about 21 kB gzip.
  • Frames at 390x844 over a 5 s idle (hero entry plus sway) and a 5 s scroll, four runs each at 1x, 4x and 6x CPU throttling: p99 16.8 to 50 ms; worst single frames 66.7 ms (scattered, also in the unthrottled runs, so machine load) and one stall of 0.4 to 1.2 s that occurs only in the first page of a freshly started browser, about 1.3 s after loading, and never in later pages (browser warm-up, not reproduced). Desktop Chromium on a loaded machine, not a phone.
  • Alignment check: the same scene element inside the screen and in the backdrop stays within 0.5 px of each other at rest and at full sway.

Known weaknesses

  • The drawn phone at 390 px wide is about 65 % of a real phone, so the card text inside it is small (about 11 px).
  • The scene is an illustration that wants to feel like a photo; a real photo of a made-up place would read faster but costs bytes and licensing.
  • The scroll-switched scenes are easy to scroll past on a phone; the captions carry the meaning on their own.
  • The fake QR codes are patterns, not real codes.

Parameters it rests on

  • Phone height on a portrait phone: 54 % of the viewport, capped at the viewport minus 366 px so the buttons stay on the first screen (?ph=50 / 54 / 58 to compare). At 390x844 all three fit; at 360x740 only the cap saves 54 and 58.
  • Backdrop paleness: 50 % paper over the plain place.
  • Entry length: about 2.4 s; sway: 5 s, about 14 px and 0.9 degrees.
  • Sideways phone below a viewport height of 560 px.
