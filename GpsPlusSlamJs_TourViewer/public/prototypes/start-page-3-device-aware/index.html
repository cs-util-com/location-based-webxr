How it goes

Your story, told in one walk.

Here is the whole of it, start to finish. Each picture shows what your phone, and later your visitor's phone, would show at that moment.

  1. A story you know

    It starts with something you know.

    The old mill at the edge of the park turned for a hundred years. You know when it was built and what the stream drove, and somewhere you have a photo of it from 1910.

    People walk past it every day and know none of this. You could tell them, right there, at the mill.

  2. On site

    Walk there with your phone.

    Open the page on your phone and go to the mill. Stand where the story happened and hold the phone up. A ring on the ground shows the spot. Tap Place a pin, and a numbered station drops onto it.

    You do not need anything printed for this. Placing works anywhere along your walk.

  3. Words and pictures

    Add what you know.

    Type a title and a few lines. Take a photo there. They stay on that spot, standing in the air above the station, the way a sign would.

    Then walk on to the next place that has a story, and do the same there. The words can be changed later, at a computer.

  4. When it is ready

    Hang a code where the walk begins.

    At the end of your walk the tour is saved. Print its code on a sheet of paper, whenever the tour is ready, and hang it where the walk starts: on a gate, a post, a noticeboard.

    Visitors scan it there to open the tour.

  5. Your visitors

    They scan it, and your stations appear.

    Someone stops at the noticeboard and points their camera at the code. No app to install: the tour opens in their phone's browser.

    They hold the phone up, and your station appears on the spot where you stood, with your words and your photo. Then the next one, as they walk.

And at the mill they read what you wrote, beside the photo you kept.

    Notes on this prototype

    V3, "device-aware front door": the real start page, with one change. The hero's main button, and one line under it, depend on the device. Everything else on the page is the real page's markup, styles and moving parts (film, cards, use cases, the setup map). The editor entry, the storage steps, the link check and AR are faked; nothing is saved or sent.

    The doors

    • Computer (wide screen with a mouse or trackpad, decided before the first paint): "Build or edit at your computer" opens the editor entry. The line under it sends the page to a phone (a code to scan) or shows the setup steps.
    • Phone with AR: "Make your tour here" goes to the real setup, storage first. "Open it on this phone" goes to "You already have a tour?".
    • Phone without AR: "Send this page to a computer" (the share sheet) and a line saying where tours are made and where they play.
    • While a phone's AR check runs, the door reads "Make your tour", in the same space as the final door: the swap changes the words, not the height.

    Measured

    Headless Chromium, the device set by the switch, reduced motion on (scrolls are instant), at 390x844, 844x390, 1000x620, 1280x720 and 1440x900. "Scroll" is what the reader scrolls by hand, from the top of the page, to bring each next control into view (16 px margin); scrolls the page does itself (a door that moves to the setup) are not counted. A paste counts apart from the taps.

    Layout shift after the AR check, at a delay of 0, 300 and 2000 ms: the door itself adds none at any delay or width. Its four phone layers are equally tall (112 px at 390x844, 86 px at 844x390, 120 px wider), so the swap from "Make your tour" changes nothing below it. The only shift in the second after the check (0 to 0.019) comes from the story film settling (its first card and play button), and the page shows the same total (0.016 to 0.037 at 1000 px and wider, 0 on the phone sizes) at a computer, where no check runs. Headless timings: the check's mark lands about 0.2 s, 0.6 to 1.6 s and 2.1 to 2.6 s after the start.

    The values this rests on: the computer guess is (min-width: 768px) and (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine); the check's delay is the switch's (the real check on a phone is usually quick, but not bounded); one headless run per case, so the shift numbers carry the film's timing noise. A different width threshold moves only which visitors get which door, not the heights.

    Weaknesses

    • Three pages in effect: the same address shows three different main buttons. A link shared from a computer opens a different door on a phone, and help texts or screenshots must say which one they mean.
    • More tests: every door, at each check outcome, at each width, and the pending state, against one door today.
    • A wrong device guess sends someone the wrong way. The computer guess is width plus a fine pointer: a tablet with a trackpad, or a narrow computer window under 768 px, gets the phone doors (and its AR check says "no AR"). A phone whose AR check is slow shows "Make your tour" for that long.
    • A first-time visitor at a computer who wants to create on site sees the editor first; the way to the phone is the line under it.

    Switches

    The dark button at the bottom right: the device (as if, or ?ar=1 / ?ar=0), a returning device (a phone then starts at the setup), and how long the AR check takes. Each change reloads the page.
