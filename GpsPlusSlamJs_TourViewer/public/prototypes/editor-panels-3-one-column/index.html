One right column: details and files share it

Never more than one panel stands on the right: the details and the files share one column with two tabs. Selecting something shows its details, dropping a file shows the files, and while files are still being made smaller a badge on the Files tab says how many.

Handles under the panels (desktop)

The camera frames the selection in the visible part, not in the window: the picture's centre is moved to the middle of the space between the list, the right column, the bottom panel and the map controls, and the distance is chosen so that the move disc, the turn knob, the size dots and a station's radius dot all fit inside it. Opening or closing a panel re-centres the selection in what is left. With one 300 px column on the right, a 1280 px window keeps 732 px of 3D view instead of about 440 px with the details and the shelf side by side.

A phone held sideways runs out of room

On a phone the screen is the 3D view plus at most one column: the list or the right column, switched with List / Details / Files in the top bar (tap the open one again for the 3D view alone). Sideways, the 3D view keeps 544 x 347 px whichever column is open, and the map controls stand in the 3D view's own corner. Upright, the column becomes a sheet under the view, which keeps the top 54 %. A tap on a list row keeps the list and frames the thing beside it; a tap in the 3D view opens its details.

What it costs

  • The details and the files are never side by side. A file goes into an item with "Use a file" in its details, or by dragging the file from Files onto the item in the 3D view or onto its row in the list, never into the details' own field.
  • The column changes its tab on its own. A selection takes you from Files to Details; a drop takes you from Details to Files, except while you are typing in the details, when only the badge and a short message say so. Both can surprise.
  • On a phone the list and the details never show together, so going through several items means switching, or selecting them in the 3D view.
  • Upright, the "column" is a sheet over the lower half, not a column.
  • The bottom panel (problems, history) is shown on a desktop only; on a phone it would be one more thing the single column shows in turn.
  • The camera moves whenever a panel opens or closes. It keeps the direction you look in and only re-centres, but it can feel busy.
  • A very large station on a sideways phone cannot show its whole circle and a move disc big enough to grab; the disc wins, and the radius dot moves to the visible part of the circle.
