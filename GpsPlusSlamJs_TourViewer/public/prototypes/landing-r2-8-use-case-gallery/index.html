About this prototype

Direction

A use-case gallery: a non-developer recognises themselves in an example tour before they understand the technology, then makes their own.

Why it should work for this audience

A guide, teacher, museum or town office thinks in places and visitors, not in AR. Six makers who look like them, each with a real station, a real effort ("one evening") and the real steps, answer "is this for me?" and "how much work is it?" at once. One visual rule carries the AR idea: black line art is the place, colour is what the tour adds through the phone; the detail view's "With the tour / Without" switch makes that difference tangible without any 3D.

Load-bearing details

  • First screen at 390x844: brand bar, the three facts (AR on site; make it yourself, no code, no sign-up, no account; Android with Chrome), and the first example's picture plus its maker line above the fold.
  • The illustration rule: ink line art, white fills, one spot colour per tour, halftone dots, viewfinder corners. Every example uses only notes, photos, pins and route dots, the content a tour holds today.
  • Every card says "Example" and the gallery head says the makers and places are invented. No testimonials, no quotes from people, no accuracy numbers.
  • Type: condensed bold sans for headlines (system condensed faces), a serif for reading, the system sans for labels. Hairline grid, no rounded corners, no shadows; the only dark block is "Make yours".
  • Phone layout: a swipeable row (default) with the next card peeking at 80 % card width; "List" switches to one column. From 720 px a two-column grid, from 1080 px (and in phone landscape) three columns.
  • Motion: none on load. The detail view fades in (220 ms) and the "Without" switch fades the colour layer (250 ms); both off under reduced motion.
  • The detail view is a modal dialog with its own history entry: the phone's Back closes it; #ex-<id> deep-links an example.

Measured cost (desktop machine, under load; not phone numbers)

JS: one inline script, 15.2 kB raw, 5.4 kB gzip; no CDN JS, no WebGL, no images (the whole page is 64 kB, 18.1 kB gzip). At 390x844 in headless Chromium on a desktop: 5 s idle had no frame over 50 ms at 1x, 4x or 6x CPU throttle; 5 s scroll in the swipe layout, worst 33 ms at 4x and 50 ms at 6x; swiping the row, worst 17 ms. The list layout had one scroll frame of 100 to 150 ms at 4x. The costliest action is opening an example: about 45 to 90 ms at 4x, the first open up to about 230 ms. That cost is the modal itself (about 13 ms for an empty dialog) plus laying out the dialog's text; removing the picture copy, the halftone patterns or the fade made no clear difference. These are desktop numbers, not phone numbers, taken while the machine ran other work.

Swipe or list on a phone (390x844)

Swipe reads better here. The first screen shows the first example whole, its title, and the edge of the next one, and "Make yours" is one short scroll away (page about 2 700 px tall). The list gives each picture about a fifth more size but needs about five screens of examples before "Make yours" (page about 5 300 px). The verdict would reverse if readers do not swipe and only ever see example 1; only use on real phones can tell. Card width: at 76 % the next card shows enough to read its tag, at 90 % it nearly disappears; 80 % is the compromise.

Known weaknesses

  • No moving picture of AR; the "Without" switch is a stand-in for seeing it.
  • The made-up times ("one evening") are estimates for tours of that size, not measurements.
  • Condensed headline faces differ by platform (Bahnschrift, Roboto Condensed, Avenir Next Condensed); without one the headline falls back to a regular sans and runs one line longer.
  • In the swipe row only one example is fully visible at a time, so a reader may never reach the sixth.
  • Opening an example breaks the 50 ms frame budget at 4x throttle (see above); a build would keep the detail laid out instead of creating it on tap.

Parameters it rests on

  • Headline size on a phone (about 45 px at 390 wide; 34, 40 and 46 tried), which decides whether the first picture reaches the fold. With the two-line headline it does at every size tried; the earlier four-line headline at 40 px pushed the picture below it.
  • Card width in the swipe row (80 %; 76, 84 and 90 tried).
  • ?ar=1 / ?ar=0 fake the device's AR support (the toggle does the same; Auto asks the browser). ?view=row / ?view=list pick the phone layout.
