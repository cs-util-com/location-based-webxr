AR tours, on site, on your phone

You have a story to tell. Tell it where it happened.

Or something to show. These are the tools to put it on site: your visitors hold up their phone at the real spots and see your words and pictures there.

About this prototype

Direction

The quietest page of the set: a small model of the tour's route as a still, or drawn once and slowly, with no scroll-driven camera; below it three cards, each with a drawn phone that plays once (place a station; add words and a picture, then hang the code; a visitor sees it); then the whole way in story order as stacked sections; then six ideas.

Why it should work for this audience

  • The model shows "a route with stations in a real place" at a glance; the three phones then show, concretely, what making it and seeing it look like.
  • Only one thing moves at a time: the route draws while it is in view, and the phones play one after another, each once, then hold. Text is never next to something moving for long.
  • The model and the phones share one palette, one ink line and one hard paper shadow, so the two drawing styles read as one page.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • Shared look: paper #f4eedf, ink #2a2824, route red #cf4428 only for the route, pins, badges and the main button; the phone world uses the model's own colours (grass #d3cfa6, path #f6f1e3, walls #efe7d4, roof #a49a86, faceted crowns); AR cards are white with a 1 px ink border and a 3 px hard shadow, like the model's labels; the phone body is 8 px ink with the model's 5/6 px hard shadow.
  • The hero model never follows the scroll; it draws once in s while at least a quarter of it is in view, then stops rendering. "Draw the route again" replays it.
  • Each phone starts when of it is in view and no other picture is moving; the next waits. The visitor's phone ends with a single sideways movement of px in which the station stays on its spot.
  • Text in the phones: base size , smallest readable text at 390 px (the clock in the status bar is decoration).
  • The setup is told in story order (story, walk, words and pictures, the code, visitors), with the setup page's six steps named inside the five parts; printing comes after the walk.
  • Paragraph and list margins are zeroed here and every gap is set explicitly, so a port into the design system keeps them.

Measured cost (desktop machine under other load, not a phone)

  • JS: page script about kB gzip; three.js 0.185.1 minified about 187.5 kB gzip, loaded after the first paint; together about kB of the 250 kB budget.

Known weaknesses

  • three.js costs most of the budget for one picture that draws once; the drawn floor is almost as good and costs nothing.
  • The phones wait their turn: a reader who scrolls past a phone quickly sees its first frame, and it plays when they come back to it. On a wide screen the third phone starts about eight seconds after the first.
  • Card 2 shows two things (words and picture, then the code), so it is the longest picture, about five seconds.
  • On a phone the three cards stack, so the page is long before the setup; the cards are the reason to scroll.
  • The code is a pattern, not a real code. Nothing was measured on a phone.

Parameters it rests on

  • ?hero=still or a drawn route with ?draw= seconds (3, 6, 10 tried); ?hh= hero height on a portrait phone in px (260, 300, 340 tried).
  • ?pt= text size inside the phones in px (12, 13, 14, 15 tried; at 15 the code's sheet starts to cover the notice board); ?sway= the visitor phone's movement in px (0, 4, 6, 8, 12 tried; the station stays on its spot at every value); ?start= how much of a phone must be in view before it plays (0.3, 0.6, 0.85, 0.9 tried).
  • ?ar=1 / ?ar=0 fake the device's AR support; ?gl=0 forces the drawing without 3D.
