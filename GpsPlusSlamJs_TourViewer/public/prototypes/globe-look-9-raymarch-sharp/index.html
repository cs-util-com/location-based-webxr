Preparing cloud noise...
Notes

Technique. The same raymarched cumulus shell as the stable raymarched page (weather-map domes eroded by baked 3D noise, short light march, the glitter-and-haze sea), but the clouds are kept at the full canvas resolution and only one pixel of every 2x2 block is marched per frame, in a fixed order. The other three pixels carry their own last value, reprojected with the camera's motion and the clouds' wind drift (Catmull-Rom lookup, clamped so it cannot ring). Each marched pixel checks whether its new value lies within the range of the history around it; where it does not (something changed or was uncovered), the carried pixels nearby lose their validity and are filled from the marched pixels instead. Nothing is jittered, so a still view converges to the full-resolution render of every pixel, identical to the bit.

Why this technique, and what it trades.

  • The stable page marches a quarter of the canvas pixels (half resolution) and then softens them with a 3x3 tent filter, which is where most of its blur comes from. Here the same number of rays per frame builds a full-resolution picture over four frames, and only a light filter is needed.
  • The earlier temporal attempt wobbled because it jittered the rays, so its accumulated history was resampled at a shifting position every frame. This one has no jitter: every pixel is re-marched every 4 frames (67 ms at 60 fps). A marched value that disagrees with the history replaces it outright, so a real change lives at most 4 frames; one that agrees keeps half of its history, which smooths the fine grain of the march while the camera turns (a difference below the change threshold fades by half every 4 frames).
  • Traded: a resolve pass at full resolution and two full-resolution history buffers (about 10 bytes per pixel), the change test in the march, and a lower resolution for up to 4 frames where the history is rejected (newly uncovered cloud, the screen edge during a turn, the first frames after a jump).
  • Considered and not taken: a precomputed noise volume (the page already samples baked 3D textures, so there was little per-step cost to cut: the march is less than half of the frame at 10 km), and a full-resolution pass on cloud edges only (edges cover much of the screen in these views, so it would cost more than it saves).

What else changed from the stable raymarched page.

  • Denser clouds: the extinction of view rays is raised from 55 to 90 per unit density (x1.64); the light paths (sun, sea shadows, the powder term) keep 55, so the backlit rim and the blue-grey sky-lit sides are lit as before, only less see-through. ?dense=0 restores the old density.
  • The cheap tier's far sea: it now keeps the long wave octaves (6 octaves from 32 m to 1 km, as far as the high tier reaches) instead of 5 from 16 m to 256 m; the shortest waves are folded into the slope statistics, as they already were wherever a pixel cannot resolve them. ?farsea=0 restores the old waves.

Toggles.

  • Buttons Base clouds / Sharp clouds, or ?method=base|new: base is the stable page's scheme (half resolution, every pixel every frame, tent filter), so both can be compared on one page.
  • ?cell=1|2|3: march 1 of 1, 4 (default) or 9 pixels per frame. ?tap=0..1: the final filter (0.25 at high, 0.4 at low; 0 is sharper but grainy). ?rej=a,b: the change threshold (0.15,0.5). ?box=2: the change test over 2x2 instead of 3x3 history pixels. ?blend=0..0.9: the share of agreeing history kept in a marched pixel (0.5). ?clouds= and ?scale= as before (base cloud and canvas resolution).
  • ?method=ref: a 2x2 supersampled reference (4 rays per pixel, slow), used for the sharpness measurements.

Measured on a laptop's integrated GPU (D3D11) at a phone's pixel count: 360x800 CSS pixels at 3x, so the canvas is 540x1200 at high and 270x600 at low. The clock is frozen wherever a test compares pictures.

  • Sharpness, as the blur that best turns the supersampled reference into the page's cloud opacity (Gaussian sigma in canvas pixels, lower is sharper; error against the reference in brackets), 10 km view. High: base 1.28 px (0.046), sharp 0.48 px (0.020); the base scheme with clouds at 0.75 or at full resolution only reaches 0.84 and 0.72 px. Low: base 1.42 px (0.068), sharp 0.70 px (0.039; 0.54 px with the high tier's lighter filter, but grainier). The same holds at 3 km (high: 1.28 to 0.50) and 30 km (1.37 to 0.49). Against the high tier's reference, the cheap tier improves from 3.9 to 2.5 px at 10 km and from 3.2 to 1.8 px at 30 km (base high: 1.3 and 1.4); at 3 km the cheap tier differs in shape (24 steps, no erosion), not only in blur, so resolution alone does not fix it there.
  • A pose reached by a slow orbit against the same pose rendered fresh (3 degrees at 2 deg/s, 60 fps): high 0.46 % of the pixels differ by more than 8 of 255 (mean 0.18 of 255), low 1.6 % (mean 0.42); at 10 deg/s 1.8 % and 3.6 %. While turning the sharpness stays at 0.51 px (high) and 0.72 px (low) at 2 deg/s and 0.57 and 0.75 px at 10 deg/s, against the base scheme's 1.28 and 1.42 px. With the clock running as well: 0.46 % and 1.7 %. The base scheme passes this test trivially (0 %), since it keeps nothing.
  • Stability (two frames 1.5 s apart, camera still, clock frozen): identical to the bit, at both tiers. One frame of running time: 0.003 % (high) and 0.02 % (low) of the pixels change by more than 8 of 255 (base: 0.01 % and 0.06 %). Frame-to-frame shimmer (the second difference over 12 frames) during a 2 deg/s orbit: high 0.17 % of pixel-steps over 8 of 255, against 0.28 % for base and 0.38 % for an equally sharp full-resolution render without reuse; low 0.50 %, against 0.85 % and 0.91 %.
  • What would reverse that: the settings are swept, not picked. With the change test over 2x2 pixels the shimmer was 0.9 % (high) and 2.8 % (low), worse than base, because the grain of the march was taken for change. Without the history share (blend 0) it is 0.43 % and 1.2 %, above base; with 0.75 it drops to 0.13 % and 0.42 % but the turning picture lags the fresh one twice as much. A tighter threshold (0.02,0.08) raises the orbit difference from 0.40 % to 0.78 %. Marching 1 of 9 pixels instead of 1 of 4 keeps the sharpness (0.54 px while turning) but doubles the shimmer (0.61 % high, 1.5 % low).
  • Cost, the GPU time per frame under steady load (the frame drawn several times so the integrated GPU holds its clock), interleaved A B A B; the spread between repeats of one setting (the noise floor) was 0.05-0.3 ms. The Measure button's slope varied by 1-4 ms between repeats on this machine, too coarse for these differences, so the GPU timer is quoted. High tier: base 1.7-2.1 ms; sharp, 1 of 4 pixels, 2.2-3.4 ms, that is +0.3 ms at 3 km to +1.3 ms at 10 km (+17 % to +67 %); 1 of 9 pixels 1.8-2.5 ms (-1 % to +21 %); every pixel every frame 4.4-8.4 ms. Per pass at 10 km: the march costs the same per ray plus about 20 % for the change test, the full-resolution resolve adds 0.34-0.40 ms, the composite is unchanged. Low tier: base 0.24-0.34 ms, sharp 0.35-0.46 ms. The far-sea change: +0.06-0.07 ms at low (about +10 % of that frame; noise floor 0.01-0.06 ms).
  • At equal cost: the base scheme with clouds at 0.75 costs about what the sharp clouds cost (3.0 against 3.1 ms at 10 km) and reaches 0.84 px against 0.48. The sharp clouds on a 1.2x canvas (?scale=1.2) cost 1.6-1.7 ms, below base high's 1.8-2.0 ms, and are still sharper (0.80-0.83 px on the high grid against 1.28-1.37), but the sea is then drawn on the coarser canvas too.
  • Noise bake at load: 0.5-1.5 s on the main thread. Switching between base and sharp compiles shaders once (a short pause).

Live on this device: press Measure.

Controls. Drag to orbit around the point in the middle of the view; right-drag or two fingers to pan; wheel or pinch to fly in and out; W A S D to move, Q and E to fly down and up. The camera stays between about 100 m and 60 km.
