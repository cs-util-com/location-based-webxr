Your part

Making a tour means making this poster.

Six steps, in a browser, without code. Each one fills in a part of the sheet.

  1. 1

    Choose where your tour lives.

    Your tour is one small file in storage you already have: Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, GitHub or your own server. It stays yours.

    Fills in: what the code points to

  2. 2

    Paste its share link.

    The link becomes the code. Every phone that scans it opens your tour.

    Fills in: the code

  3. 3

    Print the poster.

    Give it a title and a line about the walk; the code and "Point your phone here" are set for you. Print at actual size, on A4 or larger.

    Fills in: the title and the words

  4. 4

    Hang it where the walk begins.

    On a flat surface at chest height, in good light, out of the rain, where people naturally stop.

    Fills in: the pins, and makes "You are here" true

  5. 5

    Walk the route and place your stops, in AR.

    Scan your own poster, walk the way you have in mind, and tap to put notes and photos where they belong. The tour is made by walking it.

    Fills in: the route and its stops

  6. 6

    Put the saved tour back.

    Replace the file in your storage with the one the walk saved. The link and the printed code stay the same: come back and add more, nothing to reprint.

    Keeps: the code you already hung

About this prototype

Direction

The printed poster, the one physical object of a tour, is the hero; scrolling reads the poster part by part, first as a visitor, then as the maker whose six steps each fill in one part of it.

Why it should work for this audience

  • A guide, a teacher or a tourism office already knows what a poster is and how to make one. Starting from a finished object makes "an AR tour" concrete before any word about storage or links.
  • The visitor's story (scan, walk) and the maker's job (make this sheet) are told on the same object, so the six steps stop looking like an abstract setup.
  • "Print an example poster" gives a would-be maker something to hold and show colleagues.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • Every poster length is in cqw of the poster's width (container queries), so hero, crops, the pinned board and the A4 print are one design. A port that uses px or rem breaks the print.
  • Hero poster width on a portrait phone: clamp(240px, min(0.74 x 100vw, (100svh - 352px) / 1.414), 360px). The smallest poster text is 3.6 cqw, about 10 px at 280 px.
  • Wall: deep green-black, poster tilted -1.4 degrees with two orange pins. Two inks only on the poster (green, orange) plus black.
  • Phone portrait: each explanation shows its poster part as a 16:9 crop with an orange ring. 760 px and wider: one sticky poster, parts dim to 26 % and the current part gets an orange frame and the item's tag; crop marks appear in the maker's part.
  • The design system's margin reset is assumed: this page sets every margin it needs explicitly.
  • Poster text must not inherit page rules: a page rule like .item p resized the poster's lines inside the crops and squeezed its map. Scope page rules (.item > p) or the poster drifts from its print.
  • Crops are filled lazily, one per frame, a screen ahead of the reader; the part positions are measured once on an unrotated poster off screen.
  • Motion: the hero poster swings onto the wall once (0.9 s) and the pins drop; nothing loops. Reduced motion removes both.
  • Print: @page A4, margin 0; only the poster prints, 210 x 297 mm, colours forced.

Measured cost

JS: inline only, no CDN, no WebGL: about 4.3 kB gzip (the whole page 16 kB gzip). Desktop Chromium at 390x844 on a loaded machine, not a phone: no frame over 50 ms while scrolling at normal CPU. At 4x CPU slowdown the FIRST scroll has 1 to 2 frames of about 70 to 120 ms, when the first poster crop is painted (hiding the crops removes them; the second scroll is clean). One poster lays out in about 2 ms (6 ms at 4x). On a mid-range phone this first crop may well cost a frame over 50 ms: not measured on a phone.

Known weaknesses

  • On a 360x640 phone the hero needs a short scroll for the buttons; the poster stays legible.
  • The printed example code is a pattern, not a working code. A real example tour to scan would be stronger.
  • No 3D: AR is shown by one flat drawing; a reader who has never seen AR may still need the phone view.
  • The dark wall and the light poster are a strong contrast on the first screen; the sections below are light. The jump is deliberate (the wall, then the proof sheet) but could feel like two pages.

Parameters it rests on

  • Hero poster share of phone width, ?pw= (default 0.74): tried 0.66, 0.74, 0.82.
  • Wide layout from 760 px; crop zoom computed per part so it fills about 84 % of the crop's width or 74 % of its height, clamped 0.9 to 2.6 (0.9 to 2.4 in use); the part being read is the one crossing the middle 10 % of the viewport.
  • Smallest poster text measured in the hero: 10.4 px (fine print) and 11.8 px (how-to line) at 390x844; 9.9 and 11.3 px at 390x740; 8.3 and 9.5 px in phone landscape, the weakest view.
