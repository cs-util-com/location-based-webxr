    Notes on this prototype

    Direction

    Two doors: the first screen asks one question, take a tour or make one, and each answer opens its own short path on the same page while the other door stays one tap away.

    Why it should work for this audience

    • Two kinds of people arrive at the same address: someone standing at a poster who wants the tour now, and someone (a guide, a teacher, a museum) who wants to make one. A single story has to serve both and serves neither first; two doors let each self-select in one tap.
    • The visitor never reads a word about storage or posters; the maker never hunts for "scan".
    • Visual language: flat colour blocks and very big system type, like wayfinding signs. A door is a sign you choose by colour from across a room. Blue means "taking", yellow means "making", and the colour stays with you through the whole path, so you always know which door you are in. High contrast reads in sunlight on a phone, where visitors use it. It is deliberately unlike the light paper look of the setup page.

    Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

    • Both doors fully above the fold on a 390 x 844 phone, together with the headline and the Android line; side by side in landscape and on desktop.
    • Each door is one link the full width of its block (a big touch target), with a one-line promise, a picture and an arrow square.
    • Door colours: blue #1e40e6 with white text, yellow #ffd23a with near-black text; square corners, no shadows, no gradients.
    • Door title weight 900, tight letter spacing (-0.045em), line height 0.92, scale clamp(42px, 12.4vw, 104px).
    • Opened door: the intro hides, the door face shrinks to a title bar with "Both doors", and the other door becomes a 58 px bar pinned to the bottom (portrait) or a 76 px vertical strip (wide). URL hash #take / #make, so the browser back button closes a door and a link can open one directly.
    • The AR line is said three times, each where it matters: under the headline, in the visitor path, in the maker path (with "on any device" vs "on site, Android + Chrome" on each step).
    • Motion: only a one-shot pin drop on the two door pictures (0.7 s, never repeats) and a 0.35 s door widening on wide screens; none under reduced motion.
    • The design system's paragraph and list resets are already applied here (all margins 0), so spacing comes from the blocks' own padding and gaps.

    Measured cost

    JS: about 11 kB inline, about 4 kB gzip; no external scripts, no WebGL, no web fonts; the whole page is about 14 kB gzip. Frames, measured on a desktop under other load, not on a phone: a 5 s scroll at 390 x 844 never over 16.8 ms, also with the CPU slowed 4x and 6x; 5 s idle never over 16.8 ms except the one warm-up frame (83 ms at 4x, 233 ms at 6x). The heaviest moment is the first opening of a door (laying out its path for the first time): 33 to 67 ms with the CPU slowed 4x, thanks to the idle warm-up (150 to 250 ms without it). The warm-up itself is one task of about 120 ms at 4x, 1.4 s after load, when nothing moves.

    Known weaknesses

    • It explains AR with two small pictures, not by showing it; a first-time visitor who has never seen AR may not get it from a pin on a phone.
    • The maker's path ends after step 1 and hands over to the full setup page; the six steps are not redesigned here.
    • Two equal doors make the visitor door as loud as the maker door, even though the maker is this page's main audience. A visitor with a code usually arrives straight in the tour, not here.
    • "Scan a code" in a web page can only start the camera inside the page; most visitors will use their camera app anyway, which the copy says first.

    Parameters it rests on

    • Door title size (try ?type=s, ?type=m, ?type=l): both doors stay above the fold at 390 x 844 with all three (bottom of the second door at 715, 734, 765 px); at 360 x 740 "l" misses by 1 px; on a 1440 x 900 desktop "l" wraps "Make a tour" onto two lines and pushes that door below the fold, so m (104 px there) is the largest that holds. Phone landscape uses its own fixed size and ignores the setting.
    • Door face height 212 px on a phone; the 58 px bottom bar; breakpoint to side by side at 900 px wide or any landscape at least 600 px wide.
    • ?ar=1 / ?ar=0 fake the device's AR support (the toggle at the top does the same; Auto asks the browser).
    • The idle warm-up: each closed path is laid out once, off screen, 1.4 s after load, so the first tap on a door does not pay for it. ?warm=0 switches it off to compare.
