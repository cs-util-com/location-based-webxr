    W1 - Calm card

    The idea

    Everything the visitor needs sits in one card at the bottom, the way a map app's navigation card does: the next station, how far, which way, how sure the guide is, and how far through the tour. The card is a slim bar while walking and grows only when there is something to read or decide: a story, a choice, the end. The camera view stays clear; the only thing drawn over it is one small edge arrow when the station is off screen, and a small ring on it when it is in view.

    How precision reads

    • By code (teal): the distance is exact ("35 m") and the arrow is teal.
    • GPS, about 8 m: the distance is rounded and says "about".
    • GPS weak (amber): no distance and no arrow; the card asks the visitor to step into the open.
    • No position (dashed grey): no distance, no arrow, no ring; the card asks for a code. The dashed outline repeats on the arrow badge.

    What it trades away

    • Nothing in the world marks the path; the visitor reads the card and turns. A visitor who never looks down follows only the edge arrow.
    • Leaving AR takes two taps (open the card, Leave AR). That is on purpose: a pocketed phone should not end the tour, and the phone's back gesture still leaves at once.
    • In an any-order tour the card names one station (the nearest); the others are one tap away in the opened card, not on screen.
    • The card hides a strip of the camera's lower edge (the ground at the visitor's feet), never the stations ahead.

    The skip with no trusted position (the open question)

    In an any-order tour, the skip does not name a station when nothing vouches for the position (no code lined up and no usable GPS, and also while GPS is too weak). "Can't get there?" opens the list instead and asks "Which one can't you get to?"; the visitor taps the station they cannot reach. The undo works as always. The suggested skip (after 2 minutes) is not offered then, since its clock needs a distance.

    Why: the guide cannot know which station is nearest, but the visitor knows which one is behind the locked gate. Naming the "nearest" by a position the card itself calls unknown would contradict the line above it. In a fixed-order tour nothing changes: only one station is offered, so the skip names it without needing a position.

    Texts changed (proposals)

    • The station line is split across the card instead of one sentence: an eyebrow ("Next - 2 of 4", "Nearest - 3 to find"), the title, the distance, a direction ("ahead on your right"). The real sentence ("Next: The well, 30 m") is still announced to screen readers word for word.
    • GPS distances say "about" and are rounded ("about 60 m"); a code's distance is exact.
    • The precision chip: "By code", "GPS, about 8 m", "GPS weak", "No position", "Locating..." (new).
    • Direction words: "straight ahead", "ahead on your left", "on your right", "behind you", "you are here" (new).
    • Story card eyebrow "Story playing" (the live line keeps "Station found - its story is playing."); "Keep listening" to cancel ending a story; "Cancel" to cancel a skip; "Keep looking" to dismiss the suggested skip (new).
    • The tour's end is split: "Tour complete" and "Every station visited - 1 skipped."
    • "Which one can't you get to?" (the any-order skip with no trusted position), "Leave AR", the paused page ("AR tour - paused", "N of 4 stations found", "Your progress stays on this phone.") and "Welcome back - your progress is kept." (new).
    • The framework's tracking hint is shown with a plain hyphen: "Tracking lost - slowly look around to let it recover."
