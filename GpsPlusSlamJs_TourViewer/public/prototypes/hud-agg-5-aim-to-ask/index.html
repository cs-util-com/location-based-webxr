Drag sideways to turn, drag up to walk. Point the ring at things and tap. Use the arrows above for the eight beats.

Aim to ask

The screen stays empty except for a small ring in the middle. Point the ring at something and only that one thing says what it is and what one action starts it. Things you cannot see are a single count at the foot, with the shorter way to turn.

What to try

  • Drag sideways to turn, drag up or down to walk (or the arrow keys).
  • Point the ring at the pin, the film screen, the sculpture, the picture behind you, the glowing listening spot.
  • Tap anywhere to act on what the ring points at, or tap a thing directly.
  • Turn away from an open panel: the ring drains and the panel closes.

The eight beats

Tune it

Measured per beat

Highest value while the beat ran, at this window size. HUD = share of the screen covered by HUD boxes. Items = HUD boxes on screen (the ring and the label count as two). Beyond = items besides Exit and the dial.

Which thing wins the screen

  • A story step, a quiz included, wins over everything. While it shows, the ring names nothing, the turn cue hides, a playing narration pauses and a film plays on silently. The free things only glow in the world.
  • Otherwise, what the ring points at wins. One label, one action.
  • Pointing at nothing, the place you stand in speaks (the listening spot, the film's reach).
  • A panel opens only on your tap or on a start you walked into. A new one replaces the old one, whose thing goes on in the world. It closes after you point away for the set time, or when you leave its place.
  • Things of the station you are in have a teal glow and answer the ring one by one. A station you are not at answers only as itself ("Old well, next stop"), its things are grey and never named, and they never count at the foot.

A seventh and an eighth item

Nothing on screen grows: the count at the foot goes up by one each, and the ring names each only while pointed at. Try "+2 items" under Tune it.

The strongest case against it

  • To read a panel you have to keep pointing near the thing. Holding a phone up in daylight tires the arm, and a phone lowered to read turns the ring onto the ground.
  • Nothing is discoverable by glancing: a thing you never point at never introduces itself, and the count at the foot is easy to ignore.
  • Under a weak position the things drift away from where they are drawn, so the ring points at the drawing, not at the real place.
  • Two things behind each other in the same direction share one ring: the nearer one wins and the other needs a step aside.
