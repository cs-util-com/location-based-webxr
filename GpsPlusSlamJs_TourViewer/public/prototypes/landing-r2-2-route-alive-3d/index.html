About this prototype

Direction

The example route comes alive in a small 3D model of a made-up park: the route draws itself, the stops rise as pins, a walker follows them, and scrolling takes the camera down to eye height at stop 1, where a phone frame and a floating note show what a visitor sees.

Why it should work for a non-developer

  • "Stops placed in a real place, walked in order" is shown, not explained: a model park reads at a glance as a place, the numbered pins as an order.
  • The descent to eye height is the one moment that says "AR": the same pin, now floating in front of the mill wheel inside a phone.
  • The copy never covers the model: text and diorama sit in separate grid cells at every viewport.

Load-bearing details (check a later build against these)

  • Layout: text in its own grid cell (top in portrait, left column in landscape and desktop), the diorama in its own cell; the camera is fitted to that cell's real frustum, so nothing of the park or its pin labels is cut.
  • Framing: azimuth , elevation (portrait) and (wide), field of view for the overview (an almost flat, model-like look), fitted with a 9 % margin.
  • Motion: route drawn in about 3 s, each pin rises when the line reaches it, then the walker loops the route three times (about s a loop) and stops. Pause button, no motion with reduced motion.
  • Scroll: the stage is sticky inside a 230svh section; the camera moves between 12 % and 88 % of it; the text panels swap at the middle.
  • Paper palette and serif headings of the current setup page; route red only for the route, pins and the primary button.
  • three.js must be the minified build (three.module.min.js). The unminified files alone are about 412 kB gzip, over budget.

  • JS: page script about kB gzip, three.js 0.185.1 minified 187.5 kB gzip (86.6 + 100.9), together about kB of the 250 kB budget. Loaded after the first paint.

Known weaknesses

  • three.js still costs most of the budget: there is little room left for anything else on this page.
  • The eye-height view is a model, not a photo: it hints at AR, it does not show a camera image.
  • Reduced motion and no-WebGL get two still drawings instead of the model; the drag-to-turn is lost there.
  • The long sticky section asks for a lot of scrolling before the steps on a phone.
  • Frame times were measured on a desktop under load (Intel integrated graphics), with CPU throttling as a rough stand-in for a phone; nothing was measured on a phone.
  • The fixed prototype label and device switch sit over the page content when scrolling.

Parameters it rests on

  • Overview elevation (portrait , wide ; try ?el=40 to ?el=64: at 40 the park flattens and two labels collide, 50 to 64 all hold), azimuth (?az=; 72 looks along the park's long side so it fills a phone), field of view (?fov=), walker loop length (?walk= seconds), scroll length 230svh, eye height about 1.7 m, pin height about 5.5 m in model scale.
  • URL switches: ?ar=1 / ?ar=0 fake the device's AR support; ?gl=0 forces the drawing without 3D.
