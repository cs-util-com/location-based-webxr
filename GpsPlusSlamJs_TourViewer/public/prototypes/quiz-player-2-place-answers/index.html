The place answers

A quiz step of a station's story, played in the place. The answers are hidden at the things they are about and found like eggs. You choose by standing at one until its ring fills, and you give your answer back at the question stone in the middle. The screen keeps one small pill of dots.

What wins the screen

  • A story line on screen wins: one strip at the foot, nothing else of the quiz.
  • While the question is open, the pill is the quiz's only screen element. Everything the quiz says lives in the world: the question on the stone, each answer and its clue on its thing, the rings on the ground.
  • The guide's short reactions appear once as a toast and fade.
  • A sheet opens only when the pill or the stone is tapped. It is the floor: answer from where you stand, take back, give, skip.
  • Typed answers need a keyboard, so they are the one case that goes to a card.

The other items meanwhile

By default a playing narration or film pauses when the question enters and its mark in the world dims (two voices at once cannot both be heard). Nothing is locked: tapping a dimmed item plays it, and the quiz simply waits. Change it below to compare.

A seventh and an eighth item

More free items in the station change nothing on the screen: they dim in the world like the others. A second quiz in the same station waits for the first; its stone stays dark until the story reaches it.

Answer types

  • Single: in the world, beats 1-11.
  • Multiple: in the world, each ring toggles its answer, beat 16.
  • Text: the answer is found in the place (beat 14), typed in a one-line card (beat 15).
  • Code and number are handed to the same card: a code like text, a number with its unit and tolerance, a decimal comma accepted. Not demonstrated.
  • Typed answers are judged the tour format's way and no other: Unicode NFC, case and spaces folded, umlaut variants (ae, oe, ue, ss); then the salted SHA-256 is compared.

The strongest case against it

It is slow, and it asks for a lot of walking. Find four, stand at one, walk back to the stone: on a busy square, or for a visitor with a child on the hand, that is a chore, and the sheet that rescues them also skips the whole idea. It rests on answers standing at least about 5 m apart and on a position good enough to tell them apart; when the position is rough the rings switch off and the quiz becomes a tap game in 3D, which is only play while people hold a phone. And a question on a stone in the world is easy to lose: the visitor who walks away from it relies on the pill and the radar to remember there is one.
