Notes: billboard puff clouds

Technique. Each cumulus is a cluster of camera-facing puff sprites in three levels of detail, placed by a tileable coverage field so clouds form fields, gaps and small scattered clouds. A puff takes a flattened sphere normal from its sprite coordinates, a wrapped sun term weighted by how exposed it sits inside its cloud, sky-lit ambient, and a forward-scattering rim on thin edges and small puffs when the view looks toward the sun. Bases are flat because any fragment whose sphere point lies below the cloud base fades out. A worker culls, picks the level of detail and sorts every visible puff back to front by view depth once a second; a cheap depth prepass of each puff's opaque core lets the GPU skip hidden fragments.

Sea, haze, far clouds. One full-screen pass ray-casts the sphere: Beckmann sun glitter whose roughness grows with the pixel footprint (wave slopes too small to see become roughness), plus random glint cells about one pixel in size that keep the mean but break it into sparkles near the camera; Fresnel sky reflection; haze from a Chapman-function optical depth (air plus a low haze layer, with ozone) used for sky, sea and every puff. Beyond about 45 km the same clouds continue as a flat layer drawn from a top-down coverage texture, which, projected along the sun, also darkens the sea as cloud shadows. Clouds drift on a wrapping 120 km tile.

Tiers. Low (default on a coarse pointer or small screen): 0.75 drawing pixels per CSS pixel, middle puff level to 24 km and smallest to 13 km, puffs to 40 km, two wave octaves, sea reflection from three sky colours per frame, 256 px coverage, no lens ghosts. High: up to 1.5 pixels per CSS pixel, puff levels to 40 / 22 km, puffs to 54 km, three wave octaves, exact sky reflection, 512 px coverage, faint lens ghosts. Debug: ?sync=1 times each pass, &bench=10 amortises that timing, ?prepass=0 turns the depth prepass off.

Measured. Headless, laptop integrated GPU (Intel Iris Xe), each pass drawn 10 times and averaged. At 844x390 low tier (633x292 pixels, the pixel count of a phone at this tier): sea 0.3-0.8 ms, clouds 0.6-1.4 ms. At 1440x900 high: sea 1.0-1.4 ms, clouds 0.8 ms (30 km) to 5.5 ms (3 km, densest). The depth prepass saves about 30% in the 3 km view and costs about 0.2 ms in the sparse 30 km view. CPU sort in the worker: 5-10 ms once a second, off the main thread. Headless software WebGL (CPU shared with other runs, relative only), median frame: 390x844 low 158-228 ms, 1440x900 low 527-2168 ms, 1440x900 high 976-4735 ms (30 km to 3 km); the cloud pass is 65-90% of it. A low-end phone GPU is roughly 15-25 times slower than this laptop GPU, which puts low tier at very roughly 15-50 ms: not measured on a phone.

Self-check against the reference.

  • Clouds read as clusters of soft balls, most of all up close (3 km view): no light passes between puffs, so there is no self-shadowing inside a cloud and no shadow of one cloud on another, and the reference's deep blue-grey crevices are missing. A per-puff occlusion value baked at build time (a few steps toward the sun through the puff set) would add most of it at no runtime cost: about a day.
  • Small backlit clouds in the glitter path come out grey where the reference shows them silvery; the rim term is a guess, not transmitted light. Tuning per-puff thickness: hours.
  • The far cloud layer is flat: near the horizon it loses the bumpy tops and reads partly as flat patches (worst from 30 km), and the hand-over band (about 35-54 km) is visible as a change of texture. Pushing puffs further costs fill and sort; impostor cards per cloud for the far band would be the next step: days.
  • Glints are a statistical trick (hashed cells flickering in time), not facets moving with the waves; from 30 km they look too coarse.
  • Cloud coverage and shadows repeat every 120 km; hidden by haze at 3 and 10 km, findable from 30 km.
