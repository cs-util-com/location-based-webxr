Notes on this prototype

Direction

The maker's story, told in a soft clay world: one guide has a story about a place, walks there, drops stations, adds words and pictures, hangs a code, and visitors arrive and see it all appear. It ends on "Make your own tour" and step 1.

Why it should work for this audience

  • A guide, teacher or museum person sees someone like them doing the job, in the order they would do it. The steps are shown before they are asked for.
  • The two facts that matter most (it is AR on site, and you make it yourself) are in the hero and are then acted out, not explained.
  • Clay reads as friendly and unfinished on purpose: nothing looks technical.

Load-bearing details (check a later build against these)

  • Seven stops: the hero, five numbered beats, the finale. One short caption per beat (a heading of at most nine words, one sentence under it). Phone portrait: captions on top, the scene below; landscape and desktop: captions on the left, the scene on the right.
  • The camera frames each beat into the space the caption leaves free (a lens shift plus a fit to that space, recomputed on every resize), so the subject is never under the text.
  • Each beat's motion plays once in time when it is reached by scrolling (pins pop with a squash and a wobble, cards rise, the poster bounces up); scrolling back shows the beat's end state, it does not replay backwards.
  • Stations turn into faint ghosts once the guide leaves (only a phone sees them) and pop back one by one when the visitors scan the code.
  • Soft shadows are painted blobs, not shadow maps; materials are matte (Lambert); no post-processing.
  • Scene switching:
  • Reduced motion: the same seven beats as a list of still drawings, nothing moves. No WebGL 2 (three.js 0.185 needs it): the same scroll story with the still drawing of each beat in place of the 3D. In both, three.js is never requested.
  • Spacing that the app's design system would reset: caption gaps (8 to 10 px under headings), 16 px above the buttons, 46 px buttons.

Known weaknesses

  • The visitor's own view (what they see through the camera) is only implied; this page tells the maker's story.
  • About five screens of scrolling before step 1, unless the reader taps "Make your own tour" in the hero.
  • Cartoon scale: the pins and cards are much bigger than real AR content would look.
  • Never measured on a phone.

Parameters it rests on

  • URL switches: ?ar=1 / ?ar=0 fake AR support; ?gl=0 forces the still pictures; ?mode=swap / ?mode=move pick the scene switching; ?fov= and ?per= change the lens and the scroll length per beat.
