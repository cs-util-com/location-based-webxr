Notes: relief cloud deck

Technique. One full-screen pass over an analytic sphere. The cloud layer is a 2.5D height field: cloud thickness above one flat base, baked once into a tiling, world-anchored texture (clustered unions of domes plus a small detail texture, stored with its gradient and mipmaps). Each pixel marches the ray through the cloud slab until it drops below the height field, refines the hit, and shades it from the stored gradient: sun with a short self-shadow march along the sun direction in the same field, sky light on the shadow sides, a forward-scattering rim on edges seen against the sun, and soft fringes from near misses. The sea gets its cloud shadows from the same field, sun glitter from a slope distribution with per-pixel random glint counts (sharp near, smooth far), and everything is fogged by an analytic haze integral.

Tiers.

  • Low: 18 march steps, 3 refinement steps, 3 shadow taps, 1 sea-shadow tap, one glint level, 1024 texel cloud map, render scale 0.6 of CSS pixels (adapts 0.45-0.8 to keep frames near 30 ms).
  • High: 44 march steps, 5 refinement steps, 7 shadow taps, 3 sea-shadow taps, two blended glint levels, 2048 texel cloud map, render scale up to 1.5 device pixels.

Measured (headless Chromium, so only relative; GPU time per frame with ?bench=1, which waits for the GPU after each frame):

  • Integrated laptop GPU, 1440x900: low 4.7-6.6 ms, high 14-15 ms (3 km, 10 km) and 33 ms (30 km, the longest rays through the deck).
  • Same GPU, 390x844: low 3.8-5.3 ms (one noisy 15 ms reading), high 7-17 ms.
  • Software WebGL (CPU), 390x844: low 300-560 ms, high 2.4-3.5 s, so low costs about 1/7 of high. Not measured on a phone; a low-end phone GPU is several times slower than the laptop GPU above, which puts low at roughly 20-40 ms before the render scale adapts down.
  • Startup: the cloud maps bake once in about 0.3-0.9 s.

Self-check against the reference.

  • The clouds read as solid, lit mounds, not as soft vapour: a surface model with a short shadow march has no light through thin parts, no inner glow and no wispy edges. From 3 km they look like pillows or snowy hills. A thickness-based translucency term (2-3 more taps) would soften them; real softness needs a volume.
  • A height field cannot overhang: flanks are steep and the flat dark base is never seen from the side, and there is only one layer, so a partly transparent edge shows the sea behind it rather than the cloud behind it.
  • Silhouettes are jittered single samples: the backlit rim and the soft fringe flicker as a fine white fuzz, and near the horizon the deck turns into crumbly white noise instead of dissolving smoothly into the haze. Temporal accumulation or a stronger far-field blur would fix it (one history buffer).
  • The haze band is thinner and less golden than in the reference, the glitter path is narrower near the horizon, and the small scattered clouds are too uniform (popcorn) at 30 km. These are tuning, not cost.
  • The cloud map tiles every 64 km, hidden by a second mask at another scale; on a globe it would be a per-tile or cube-face map. Sky and haze are hand-fitted, not a physical atmosphere.
