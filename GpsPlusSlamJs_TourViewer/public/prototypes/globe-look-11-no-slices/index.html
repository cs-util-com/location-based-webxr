Notes

What this page is. The living raymarched cumulus (region field, clouds that grow and dissolve, denser clouds), used to study one fault: seen from the side and close, with the sun to one side, the cloud tops look cut into thin horizontal layers, like contour lines. This page finds the cause, tries four ways to hide it that never change from frame to frame with a still camera, and measures what each costs. Time runs at x60 by default.

The cause. Two candidates: the creases of the 643 shape noise texture (one texel is about 46 m, and trilinear filtering has a kink at every texel plane), or the march itself. The test is whether the stripe spacing follows the step length or stays fixed in the world. With twice as many steps (?stepscale=0.5) the stripes come at half the spacing and fainter; with four times as many they are gone; with longer steps (0.67) they widen. Changing the noise's vertical frequency (?shapez=2.6 and 0.65, which moves every texture crease) leaves them where they are. So it is the march: a primary step is 50 to 150 m near the clouds, and at these densities one step is nearly opaque (extinction 75 per km times 0.05 km is 3.75 per unit density). The visible surface is wherever the first sample lands inside the cloud, and those samples sit on spheres of equal distance from the camera, which look level from a low pitch. Each sphere cuts the rounded top along a contour line.

The methods (?deband=, or the De-band button).

  • off - the march of the living variant: fixed blue-noise start offset per pixel, steps that grow with distance.
  • refine (default) - when a step lands in cloud after an empty one, step back and march that step and the next one again in four sub-steps each, every sub-step lit. Only the first cloud entry per ray; the coarse grid continues afterwards, so the far field is unchanged. Knobs: ?refn= sub-steps (2-8), ?refm= coarse steps refined (1-4), ?refmax= entries per ray, ?reflight=1 one light march per coarse step, ?reftau= and ?reffar=a,b refine only steps at least that opaque, ramped in from a to b km.
  • fine - every step half as long, the step limit doubled. The brute-force answer.
  • entry - interpolate where the ray crossed the cloud edge inside the entry step (a secant on the signed density) and add the missing length. It moves the weight, not the sample, so the shading still comes from the grid.
  • strat - one offset per step on a fixed un-offset grid. This fixes the slice positions in camera space as world shells do, and is kept as the control that confirms the cause.

The banding metric. The cloud does not move when the march grid is shifted by half a step; the slices do. So the slice signal is the difference between the two renders, 8-bit luminance RMS over pixels well inside opaque clouds, with the grain under 3 px removed. Lower is better; 4x the steps gives the floor of what the metric still sees (2.6, 3.0 and 1.4 at P1, P2, P3). The render is deterministic, so a repeat reads the same value. A second number is the error against a render with 4x the steps. Poses: P1 a low view over cloud tops at 3.45 km, the sun to one side (the reported view); P2 the same height towards cloud flanks; P3 3 km looking across; and the 10 and 30 km views.

  • Tier high, slice metric (P1 / P2 / P3 / 10 km / 30 km): off 8.5 / 10.0 / 3.4 / 4.9 / 5.0; refine 4.7 / 4.9 / 2.4 / 2.6 / 1.6; fine 5.2 / 6.4 / 2.3 / 2.8 / 2.0; entry 8.6 / 10.1 / 3.4 / 5.0 / 5.1 (no effect); strat 8.6 / 9.8 / 2.7 (no better by the metric, sharper stripes by eye).
  • Error against 4x the steps: P1 off 8.3, refine 3.5, fine 3.1; P3 off 2.7, refine 1.5, fine 1.1.
  • Refine variants at P1: 2 sub-steps 6.1, 8 sub-steps 3.9, one coarse step refined 5.2; lit once per coarse step 5.7 and brighter than the reference (error 8.2): rejected. First entry only reads the same as two entries at the same cost. Refining only opaque entry steps (reftau 0.5 / 1 / 2) gives 5.0 / 5.2 / 5.5 near and 2.5 / 3.3 / 4.1 at 30 km; ramping that in with distance kept the near quality but did not save cost at 10 km.
  • Tier low (24 steps, so the slices are two to three times stronger; P1 / P2 / 10 km): slice metric off 22.0 / 22.4 / 17.8, refine 12.0 / 10.1 / 12.4, fine 15.5 / 15.7 / 14.3; error against 4x the steps off 31.8 / 35.3 / 12.8, refine 8.5 / 7.9 / 7.6, fine 17.3 / 15.2 / 6.3. Here refine is clearly better than fine near the clouds.

Did the denser clouds make it worse? Barely by the extinction: view extinction 55 / 75 / 90 (?sigmav=) gives 8.3 / 8.5 / 8.6 at P1, 9.8 / 10.0 / 10.2 at P2, 3.2 / 3.4 / 3.4 at P3: +3 to 7 % from 55 to 90. Even at 55 one step is nearly opaque. The density curve d(2-d) of the living field matters more: without it (?curve=0) the slices read 6.7 / 8.3 / 2.8, so the curve strengthens them by 20 to 27 %, by making cloud edges step from empty to dense in one sample.

Cost (GPU timer, laptop integrated GPU, D3D11, at a phone's pixel count: 412x915 CSS pixels at 2.625; methods interleaved on one page, three repeats, mean; the floor is the half range of the repeats; separate runs drift by up to 17 %, so only differences within a run are quoted).

  • Tier high, four runs: near the clouds (P1, P2, P3) off 2.8 to 3.5 ms, refine 4.5 to 5.2 ms (+39 to +67 %, floors 0.1-0.5 ms), fine 4.3 to 5.0 ms (+34 to +62 %); entry +2 %, strat +30 %. At 10 km off 3.7 to 4.6 ms, refine 7.0 to 7.6 (+62 to +108 %), fine 6.3 to 6.7 (+41 to +79 %; floors up to 0.8 ms). At 30 km off 3.0 to 3.1, refine 6.2 to 7.5 (about 2x, floor up to 2.4 ms), fine 4.5 to 5.8.
  • Tier low (P1 / P2 / 10 km): off 0.43 / 0.37 / 0.46 ms (floors 0.01-0.03), refine 0.53 / 0.55 / 0.77 (+25 / +49 / +66 %), fine 0.62 / 0.54 / 0.62 (+45 / +46 / +35 %, floors up to 0.11).
  • Refine variants (high, P1, same run as refine at 4.6 ms): 2 sub-steps 4.0, 8 sub-steps 5.8, first entry only 4.5, one coarse step refined 5.1 (more steps run before the ray is opaque).

The meter. The display refresh is now a sliding estimate over the last 1.5 s (the shortest steady frame interval, so dropped frames do not pull it down), shown in the readout with its last change, and Measure probes it again before each step and uses that value to say whether a frame was held at the display rate. The debug hook lookMeterSelfTest() feeds synthetic intervals (120 Hz, then 60 Hz, then 120 Hz again, a 120 Hz display missing most frames, a jittery 60 Hz, and the capped test) and checks the reading follows: all seven cases pass.

Verdict. refine, first entry, four sub-steps over two steps: it halves the slice metric where they were seen (8.5 to 4.7 at the reported view, floor 2.6), and by eye the contour lines are gone there, for about +40 to 60 % GPU time near the clouds at high (+25 to 50 % at low), and up to 2x far away. At low it is clearly better than fine (error 8.5 against 17.3 at the reported view); at high fine is nearly as good near and cheaper far away. Far views barely need either. To add refine to a page like the living variant: about 40 lines of shader (the lighting of one sample moved into a function, a back-step on the first entry and a sub-step loop that reuses the step's region read), no new texture, no history, nothing that changes per frame.

Tiers, as in the living variant. low: canvas at 0.75 of the CSS pixels, clouds at half of that, up to 24 primary steps, one light sample. high: canvas at device pixels (at most 1.5x), clouds at half, up to 96 steps, five light samples. ?tier=low|high, ?view=10km|30km|3km, ?timescale=0|1|10|60, and the field toggles of the living variant (?field=, ?evolve=) are kept.

Controls. Drag to orbit around the point in the middle of the view; right-drag or two fingers to pan; wheel or pinch to fly in and out; W A S D to move, Q and E to fly down and up. The camera stays between about 100 m and 60 km.
