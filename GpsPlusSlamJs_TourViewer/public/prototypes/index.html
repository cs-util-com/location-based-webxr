2026-10-18 The 3D editor on the globe (stations, items, codes) #

Made before the 3D editor is built. Four directions on a faked town (a drawn street map, box buildings, a hill), all with the same sample tour: four stations with their items, two codes, the recorded walk. Each one shows the station tree, moving and turning an item or a whole station (handles and arrow keys), the radius, adding, a view at eye height, undo and redo. Best on a desktop with a real graphics card.

  • 3D-1 - StudioA dark, docked editor like a game engine: the tree on the left, a numeric inspector on the right, tool keys and a gizmo.
  • 3D-2 - Walk itOpens standing at the first station at eye height; you walk the tour and pick things up to move them, with a mini-map and a bird's-eye view one key away.
  • 3D-3 - StoryboardThe tour as a story in order: a filmstrip of station cards, camera flights along the walk, and a "Play walk" preview.
  • 3D-4 - Map that tiltsStarts as a flat street map and tilts continuously into 3D, down to eye height; map icons stand up as it tilts.

2026-10-21 The visitor's walk in AR (from AR start to the tour's end) #

Four directions for being guided from station to station: every state of the walk (tracking, waiting for a position, a code, GPS, GPS too weak, no trusted position, arriving, a story, the skip and its undo, a fixed-order and an any-order tour, the end, leaving AR). A faked camera you turn by dragging (or by the phone, after a tap); "Play the walk" runs a whole tour in about two minutes, and the demo switcher jumps to any state. The "i" button explains each design.

  • W1 - Calm cardOne bottom card, slim while walking, larger only when a moment needs it; the camera stays clear.
  • W2 - Guidance in the worldA trail on the ground and a beacon over the station, fading as the position becomes less certain.
  • W3 - The guide speaksA guide character carries the walk in short lines; the visitor answers with reply chips.
  • W4 - Radar and listA corner radar with every station and a pull-up list, made for tours in any order.

2026-10-22 Station content blocks (one kind of content, several views) #

Eight pages, one kind of station content each, every view drawing the same data on the same plain square: a visitor mode (walk in and switch views live), a creator mode on the phone and a desktop editor mode. Narration is a tone pattern, never speech. The notes panel on each page explains the views and what they cost. The six "ground view" pages are the short-text page with only its arrow on the ground replaced, one direction each, and a button that draws everything 1 m off. Nine more ground views, each laid out once and fixed in the world, are in the fixed ground views section.

  • Narrated textA long text with a voice-over that reads along: a reading sheet, a caption strip, a silent reading and a stale-timing state, plus a lectern; a creator can time it by tapping along.
  • Short textShort labels and instructions sized for their reading distance: pin, sign, arrow on the ground, speech bubble, a callout and an edge hint.
  • Short text, ground view: road markingThe ground arrow as worn road paint: one long arrow toward the target and the caption beside it in stretched road letters. Weakness: from the side and from far away the caption turns into stripes.
  • Short text, ground view: light trailSoft dots on the ground that curve toward the target, with the caption on a small tag at the start. Weakness: it looks like an app rather than part of the place, and it runs straight through walls.
  • Short text, ground view: knee ribbonA dark ribbon floating at knee height with raised chevrons, the caption on a small lectern plate beside it. Weakness: from straight behind it shrinks to a short ladder of chevrons.
  • Short text, ground view: medallionA brass compass inlay with the caption around its rim and a needle toward the target. Weakness: reads at 3 m only.
  • Short text, ground view: spotlightThe caption cast as soft light on the paving, with a light arrow toward the target. Weakness: in bright sun on pale paving it fades out.
  • Short text, ground view: stepping cardsSmall plaques leaning back along the way, each with the caption and an arrow. Weakness: seen from behind, the cards hide each other and the repeated caption stutters.
  • PicturesOne old picture at its spot: framed, cut out, then and now, and several ways of leading the visitor to the photographer’s viewpoint.
  • VideoA captioned clip: a screen in space, a clip inside a real window, a cut-out figure, a porthole and a stand-here view.
  • 3D modelsA 3D model with notes: on a pedestal, at full scale, as a ghost, with numbered hotspots and as a cutaway.
  • 3D primitivesA lost keep rebuilt from simple shapes over its ruin: solid, ghost, outline, rising from the remains, a footprint and an engraving.
  • A recurring characterA cut-out guide at two stations: a speech bubble, tap to speak with read-along, a portrait in the corner and a pop-up stage.
  • Audio aloneA narration and a soundscape, visible with sound off: a listening spot, a sound zone, a beacon, drifting words and a hearing horizon.

2026-10-25 Landing, round 2 (the Tour Viewer's landing and start) #

Ten directions for the Tour Viewer's own page as the landing and the start at once: a would-be maker (a guide, a museum, a teacher, a town office) learns what an AR tour is and how to start, and a visitor with a code gets straight in. Each page has a switch (and ?ar=1 / ?ar=0) that shows it as on an Android phone with Chrome or as on a computer or iPhone, and a folded notes panel at its end with its direction, cost, weaknesses and the values it rests on. R2-2, R2-5 and R2-9 load three.js after the first paint.

  • R2-1 - L5 extendedThe current setup page kept and grown: a moving intro, a looping strip of what a visitor sees, an example tour and three plain steps before the six-step route. Weakness: a long page, and AR is only drawn, never shown in depth.
  • R2-2 - The route comes aliveA small 3D model of a made-up park: the route draws itself, pins rise, and scrolling takes the camera down to eye height inside a phone. Weakness: three.js takes most of the script budget, and the long scroll section delays the steps on a phone.
  • R2-3 - Through the phoneThe hero is the view through a phone held up on site: the tour stays on its spot while the phone sways, then the same view shows placing a station and the printed code. Weakness: the drawn phone is small, so the text inside it is tiny on a phone.
  • R2-4 - Two doorsOne question first, take a tour or make one, as two big sign-like colour blocks, each opening its own short path. Weakness: AR is explained by two small pictures, and the visitor door is as loud as the maker's.
  • R2-5 - Clay storyThe maker's story in a soft 3D clay world, one beat per scroll: walk out, drop stations, hang a code, visitors arrive. Weakness: about five screens before step 1, and the visitor's own view is only implied.
  • R2-6 - Poster firstThe printed poster is the hero; scrolling reads it part by part, as a visitor and then as the maker who fills it in, and it prints as an A4 example. Weakness: no 3D, so AR is shown by one flat drawing.
  • R2-7 - Try it in 30 secondsA playground on the first screen: place three stops on a drawn park, write a line, then watch it as a visitor. Weakness: a drawn map is not the camera, and the phone keyboard hides the map while typing.
  • R2-8 - Use-case gallerySix invented example tours in ink and one spot colour, with a "With the tour / Without" switch, then "Make yours". Weakness: no moving picture of AR, and in the swipe row a reader may never reach the last example.
  • R2-9 - Developer landing, forkedThe developer landing's dusk 3D world and glass panels, every chapter rewritten for makers; the phone frame shows the AR content only through its screen. Weakness: dark, costly on a phone (blurred glass over a moving canvas), and close in idea to R2-5.
  • R2-10 - Calm controlNo 3D and no motion: one plain, readable page like a good public-service page, with questions answered and step 1 on the page itself. Weakness: it may read as dull, and one still picture may not make AR clear.

2026-10-26 Ground views, third set (five styles, placed handles, a bend) #

Two pages with one ground view in five styles a creator picks per block (ribbon, vanes, footprints, lights and the floor arrow, which can lie, hover or stand upright and can carry its caption). 2026-10-29 The story editor (a station's steps, choices and quizzes) #

  • Step cardsA vertical list of cards in visitor order, each showing what the visitor sees and opening in place to edit; jumps as chips with a margin line.
  • Step cards, round twoOn a phone a tapped step fills the screen and Back goes up one level; on a desktop cards open in place; a choice can go back to an earlier step or end the story, with a warning only for a loop no answer can leave.

  • Inline scriptThe story reads like a play script: the guide's lines, media inline, choices as reply buttons, branches as named sections; write to add a line.
  • Quiz: story cardThe question sits in the story card, answers replace Continue, the guide reacts there.
  • Quiz: the guide asksThe figure asks in its bubble, answers grow as signs in the world, a typed answer goes on a slate.
  • Quiz: find it hereA riddle about the place shrinks to a slip while you look around; answering is pick, then confirm.
  • Quiz in 3D: the fanThe card asks, the answers rise as signs in a fan around you; walk around them on the phone.
  • Quiz in 3D: on the thingsEach answer is a marker on a thing in the place, the tower, the fountain, the door; tap the thing.
  • Quiz in 3D: walk to itEach answer is a flag in its own circle; step in, or tap it from afar, and confirm.
  • Quiz in 3D: the card unfoldsThe answer rows lift out of the story card and stand as tablets in the space in front of you.
  • Quiz in 3D: the creator places the answersPlace four answer markers by drag, where you stand or where you aim; a spacing slider warns when two answers stand closer than the phone can tell apart, and the walk shows how often a neighbour is read instead.
  • Quiz in 3D: find the answers firstThe answers hide like eggs by the things they name; a glint grows as you near, a hint points after a while, and the question opens once all are found.
  • Quiz in 3D: answers as small scenesEach answer is a little scene on a plinth (a picture, a model, a few words); after the pick a consequence plays in the place instead of a bare "Correct!".
  • Quiz: find, then stand at the answer (A/B/C) Three ways to choose by standing at the answer, all on the stage's compact HUD: a pill of found-dots, the guide's line as a short toast, and the story card, Skip and the notes behind an "i". A B C at the top left of each page switches between them; add ?hud=card to see the full card instead.
    • A - stand in the ringFind the answers as on the page before, then stand still in one answer's ring on the ground until it closes; it locks in with a pulse, a short seal lets a walk away take it back, then the ring turns teal or breaks. The question lies on the ground.
    • B - answers that listenThe eggs are found as on the find-then-go-back page; then the question flies into them and you answer by standing by one: it wakes, fills with light, rises and opens, and a short countdown lets a step away take it back. No rings.
    • C - stand in the lightFind the answers as on the go-back page; then the question rises as a sign in the middle and each answer stands as a pillar of light. Stand in one until the light has poured down to fill it, and the answer locks in.
  • Quiz in 3D: find them, then go backThe answers hide like eggs as on the find-first page; once all are found, walk back to the one you think is right and tap it within its ring. Found answers stay marked in place and listed with their way and distance.
  • Quiz in 3D: the shared stageThe walkable place the quiz pages run on, with a trivial sample item, edit and play, and a cost readout.
