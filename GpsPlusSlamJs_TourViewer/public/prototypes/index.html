Tour Viewer design prototypes

Design drafts made to choose a direction (polish round M1), kept as an inspiration gallery. They are not connected to the app; buttons only switch states. The Tour Viewer took L5 (the setup page), H1 (the authoring HUD, with an action strip), S1 (the scan overlay), V4 (the visitor's start page) and E1 (the desktop editor); the others are here for anyone who would rather build one of them.

The entry and setup page (for a new creator)

  • L1 - EditorialA calm magazine-like scroll story with large illustrations.
  • L2 - Playful illustratedBright, friendly, a little mascot walks through the steps.
  • L3 - Dark, AR-forwardA dark page whose hero shows AR content appearing over a scene.
  • L4 - App-like wizardOne step per screen, a progress bar, like a native app.
  • L5 - Spatial pathThe steps are stations along a tour route on an illustrated map.

Landing ideas, third set (the Tour Viewer's landing and start)

Made 2026-10-25. Six pages that combine what worked in the second set: the motivation first, then what a visitor sees, what making looks like, the steps in the order of the story, the setup as stacked sections and example uses at the end. Each has the same switch (and ?ar=1 / ?ar=0) and a folded notes panel at its end. R3-2 and R3-5 load three.js after the first paint.

  • R3-0 - Strip or cardsA side-by-side page, not a landing page: the four-phone strip and the three cards, one after the other, each filled with the same animated drawn phones, to choose the layout by seeing both.
  • R3-1 - Drawn phone stripThe drawn style: a calm drawn phone shows what a visitor sees, then making a tour as a numbered strip of animated phones (place, add, hang the code, a visitor sees it). Weakness: on a phone only one strip frame shows at a time.
  • R3-2 - The route comes alive, with the making phoneThe 3D park and its self-drawing route as the hero, joined by a phone that shows the making side, one scroll step at a time; the camera always stops before the phone acts. Weakness: eight steps is a long scroll on a phone; three.js takes most of the script budget.
  • R3-3 - Two phones, one stationThe same station shown twice, side by side: being made and being seen, moving in step through the story. Weakness: the phones are drawn sideways, while a maker probably holds the phone upright.
  • R3-4 - The maker's story, made concreteText first, in the second person ("You know a story about a place"): five story parts, each with a drawn screen of the real step that plays once after its text. Weakness: the longest read, about seven phone screens before the setup.
  • R3-5 - Calm route, cards belowThe quietest page: the 3D park draws its route once and stops, then three cards with drawn phones that play in turn, then the setup in the order of the story. Weakness: what a visitor sees comes last of the three cards.

Landing, round 2 (the Tour Viewer's landing and start)

Made 2026-10-25. Ten directions for the Tour Viewer's own page as the landing and the start at once: a would-be maker (a guide, a museum, a teacher, a town office) learns what an AR tour is and how to start, and a visitor with a code gets straight in. Each page has a switch (and ?ar=1 / ?ar=0) that shows it as on an Android phone with Chrome or as on a computer or iPhone, and a folded notes panel at its end with its direction, cost, weaknesses and the values it rests on. R2-2, R2-5 and R2-9 load three.js after the first paint.

  • R2-1 - L5 extendedThe current setup page kept and grown: a moving intro, a looping strip of what a visitor sees, an example tour and three plain steps before the six-step route. Weakness: a long page, and AR is only drawn, never shown in depth.
  • R2-2 - The route comes aliveA small 3D model of a made-up park: the route draws itself, pins rise, and scrolling takes the camera down to eye height inside a phone. Weakness: three.js takes most of the script budget, and the long scroll section delays the steps on a phone.
  • R2-3 - Through the phoneThe hero is the view through a phone held up on site: the tour stays on its spot while the phone sways, then the same view shows placing a station and the printed code. Weakness: the drawn phone is small, so the text inside it is tiny on a phone.
  • R2-4 - Two doorsOne question first, take a tour or make one, as two big sign-like colour blocks, each opening its own short path. Weakness: AR is explained by two small pictures, and the visitor door is as loud as the maker's.
  • R2-5 - Clay storyThe maker's story in a soft 3D clay world, one beat per scroll: walk out, drop stations, hang a code, visitors arrive. Weakness: about five screens before step 1, and the visitor's own view is only implied.
  • R2-6 - Poster firstThe printed poster is the hero; scrolling reads it part by part, as a visitor and then as the maker who fills it in, and it prints as an A4 example. Weakness: no 3D, so AR is shown by one flat drawing.
  • R2-7 - Try it in 30 secondsA playground on the first screen: place three stops on a drawn park, write a line, then watch it as a visitor. Weakness: a drawn map is not the camera, and the phone keyboard hides the map while typing.
  • R2-8 - Use-case gallerySix invented example tours in ink and one spot colour, with a "With the tour / Without" switch, then "Make yours". Weakness: no moving picture of AR, and in the swipe row a reader may never reach the last example.
  • R2-9 - Developer landing, forkedThe developer landing's dusk 3D world and glass panels, every chapter rewritten for makers; the phone frame shows the AR content only through its screen. Weakness: dark, costly on a phone (blurred glass over a moving canvas), and close in idea to R2-5.
  • R2-10 - Calm controlNo 3D and no motion: one plain, readable page like a good public-service page, with questions answered and step 1 on the page itself. Weakness: it may read as dull, and one still picture may not make AR clear.

The visitor's start page (after scanning a poster)

Made 2026-10-12; the app took V4. A small "Prototype state" control in a corner switches between the page's states: loading the tour, location not yet allowed, location denied, ready, checking AR, AR not supported, and a tour that could not be loaded.

  • V1 - The poster's continuationThe paper look of the printed poster, come alive: the tour's name, "AR tour starts here", one big Start.
  • V2 - Dark, AR-forwardA night scene with the tour's pins already appearing over it, like a game or a camera app.
  • V3 - Three stepsAllow location, start AR, point at the poster - each step ticked off as you go.
  • V4 - Minimal, one tapThe tour's name, one big button in the thumb zone, the details folded away.

The desktop editor (on a computer, after the phone)

Made 2026-10-13; the app takes E1. All three are "full map, floating panels" and differ in where the list of codes and notes and the editing card sit. Open the sample tour; a prototype control fakes a failed save and a browser that cannot write into the file.

  • E1 - Left railThe list as a collapsible rail on the left; the editing card pops up next to the selected marker and follows it.
  • E2 - Right inspectorA compact list in the bottom-left corner; a tall inspector on the right, always in the same place, with the save status.
  • E3 - Bottom stripThe tour in walk order as a strip of cards along the bottom; the editing card top right while something is selected.

The 3D editor on the globe (stations, items, codes)

Made 2026-10-18, before the 3D editor is built. Four directions on a faked town (a drawn street map, box buildings, a hill), all with the same sample tour: four stations with their items, two codes, the recorded walk. Each one shows the station tree, moving and turning an item or a whole station (handles and arrow keys), the radius, adding, a view at eye height, undo and redo. Best on a desktop with a real graphics card.

  • 3D-1 - StudioA dark, docked editor like a game engine: the tree on the left, a numeric inspector on the right, tool keys and a gizmo.
  • 3D-2 - Walk itOpens standing at the first station at eye height; you walk the tour and pick things up to move them, with a mini-map and a bird's-eye view one key away.
  • 3D-3 - StoryboardThe tour as a story in order: a filmstrip of station cards, camera flights along the walk, and a "Play walk" preview.
  • 3D-4 - Map that tiltsStarts as a flat street map and tilts continuously into 3D, down to eye height; map icons stand up as it tilts.

The 3D editor, round 2 (after round 1 was tried)

Made 2026-10-19 from the four pages above. All four now share one frame: the stations as story cards in a panel docked on the left, an inspector docked on the right while something is selected (suggested actions first, the numbers folded away), one set of handles (a ring with four dots for a station's radius or a model's size, a turn knob, drag the middle to move), a top view (key T) where pins and photos lie flat, a dashed ring where loading starts, and two fingers that only ever move the camera. Each keeps its own idea.

  • 3D-1 round 2 - StudioThe dark docked editor, lighter: no add toolbar, no arrow gizmo, the same camera and first-person view.
  • 3D-2 round 2 - Walk itStill opens at eye height at the first station to walk the tour; the panel is docked and the mini-map is gone.
  • 3D-3 round 2 - StoryboardThe story cards move into a vertical strip on the left, reordered by dragging, with the walk between them; Play walk stays.
  • 3D-4 round 2 - Map that tiltsOpens from above for precise placing and tilts to 3D for heights and looks, with ground shadows and drop lines as height cues.

The 3D editor, round 3 (after round 2 was tried)

Made 2026-10-19: two converged pages with the same frame and rules, differing only in where they start. Section headers fold, one click in the tree selects, unfolds and flies there (a second click deselects), the tour title opens its details, map controls sit at the left, pins and photos lie flat by the camera's angle, items move only once selected, two fingers tilt, models size in metres, stations carry numbered name boards, and only the selected and the five nearest stations show their items. No eye-height view.

  • Round 3 - StudioOpens in a tilted 3D view; the dark docked editor.
  • Round 3 - MapOpens from above like a map and tilts into 3D.

The visitor's walk in AR (from AR start to the tour's end)

Made 2026-10-21. Four directions for being guided from station to station: every state of the walk (tracking, waiting for a position, a code, GPS, GPS too weak, no trusted position, arriving, a story, the skip and its undo, a fixed-order and an any-order tour, the end, leaving AR). A faked camera you turn by dragging (or by the phone, after a tap); "Play the walk" runs a whole tour in about two minutes, and the demo switcher jumps to any state. The "i" button explains each design.

  • W1 - Calm cardOne bottom card, slim while walking, larger only when a moment needs it; the camera stays clear.
  • W2 - Guidance in the worldA trail on the ground and a beacon over the station, fading as the position becomes less certain.
  • W3 - The guide speaksA guide character carries the walk in short lines; the visitor answers with reply chips.
  • W4 - Radar and listA corner radar with every station and a pull-up list, made for tours in any order.

Station content blocks (one kind of content, several views)

Made 2026-10-22. Eight pages, one kind of station content each, every view drawing the same data on the same plain square: a visitor mode (walk in and switch views live), a creator mode on the phone and a desktop editor mode. Narration is a tone pattern, never speech. The notes panel on each page explains the views and what they cost. The six "ground view" pages (made 2026-10-25) are the short-text page with only its arrow on the ground replaced, one direction each, and a button that draws everything 1 m off. Nine more ground views, each laid out once and fixed in the world, follow in the next section.

  • Narrated textA long text with a voice-over that reads along: a reading sheet, a caption strip, a silent reading and a stale-timing state, plus a lectern; a creator can time it by tapping along.
  • Short textShort labels and instructions sized for their reading distance: pin, sign, arrow on the ground, speech bubble, a callout and an edge hint.
  • Short text, ground view: road markingThe ground arrow as worn road paint: one long arrow toward the target and the caption beside it in stretched road letters. Weakness: from the side and from far away the caption turns into stripes.
  • Short text, ground view: light trailSoft dots on the ground that curve toward the target, with the caption on a small tag at the start. Weakness: it looks like an app rather than part of the place, and it runs straight through walls.
  • Short text, ground view: knee ribbonA dark ribbon floating at knee height with raised chevrons, the caption on a small lectern plate beside it. Weakness: from straight behind it shrinks to a short ladder of chevrons.
  • Short text, ground view: medallionA brass compass inlay with the caption around its rim and a needle toward the target. Weakness: reads at 3 m only.
  • Short text, ground view: spotlightThe caption cast as soft light on the paving, with a light arrow toward the target. Weakness: in bright sun on pale paving it fades out.
  • Short text, ground view: stepping cardsSmall plaques leaning back along the way, each with the caption and an arrow. Weakness: seen from behind, the cards hide each other and the repeated caption stutters.
  • PicturesOne old picture at its spot: framed, cut out, then and now, a mosaic, and several ways of leading the visitor to the photographer’s viewpoint.
  • VideoA captioned clip: a screen in space, a clip inside a real window, a cut-out figure, a porthole and a stand-here view.
  • 3D modelsA 3D model with notes: on a pedestal, at full scale, as a ghost, with numbered hotspots and as a cutaway.
  • 3D primitivesA lost keep rebuilt from simple shapes over its ruin: solid, ghost, outline, rising from the remains, a footprint and an engraving.
  • A recurring characterA cut-out guide at two stations: a speech bubble, tap to speak with read-along, a portrait in the corner and a pop-up stage.
  • Audio aloneA narration and a soundscape, visible with sound off: a listening spot, a sound zone, a beacon, drifting words and a hearing horizon.

Ground view, fixed in the world (the short-text block)

Made 2026-10-25. Nine more ground views for the short-text block, each a copy of the knee ribbon page with only that view replaced. Here everything on the ground is laid out once from the record and never moves or changes shape while the visitor walks around it; only the small caption plate may turn. Each page adds a Sun button (direct sun on pale paving) to the 1 m error button, and section 0 of its notes says what it costs and where it fails.

  • Short text, ground view: laid ribbonThe knee ribbon, laid out once along the block's turn and never re-shaped, with a calm light that runs along it toward the target. Look first from straight behind at 6 m: there it is a short ladder, and the light carries the direction.
  • Short text, ground view: chevron vanesNo ribbon: a straight row of separate chevrons at knee height, pitched nose-up so they show more of themselves to someone behind them. Look first from the target side: there the undersides stack into chevrons that seem to point away.
  • Short text, ground view: chevron trackThe ribbon pressed onto the paving: a low dark track with raised chevrons and a raised head. Look first with the 1 m error toward the wall: the head runs into the step.
  • Short text, ground view: footprintsBare-foot prints on the paving: a pair to stand on, a walking line, and a last pair facing the target, with a soft light stepping through them. Look first from the walk-in end, then from 12 m behind, where they shrink to a speck.
  • Short text, ground view: guide lightsSmall round lights set into the paving in a line that ends in a V, with a pulse running toward the target. Look first with Sun on: the rings, not the light, carry it on pale stone.
  • Short text, ground view: floor arrowOne short, solid cast arrow at the block's spot that says "that way" and does not draw the way. Look first from straight behind at 12 m, where it is still an arrow.
  • Short text, ground view: stakesTwo rows of short stakes with pennants that close in toward the target like a funnel. Look first from behind, then from the side, where it is a row of little flags all pointing at the target.
  • Short text, ground view: runnerA woven carpet runner on the paving with the caption woven into a panel at its start, plus the small plate that turns. Look first from behind the start, then from the far end, where the woven caption is upside down.
  • Short text, ground view: arcA thin rail in one arc from the block's spot to a ring before the target, with crossed chevrons riding along it. Look first from the side, further than 6 m away (closer, it is a straight incline), then from straight behind, where it is a vertical line.

Ground views, third set (five styles, placed handles, a bend)

Made 2026-10-26. Two pages with one ground view in five styles a creator picks per block (ribbon, vanes, footprints, lights and the floor arrow, which can lie, hover or stand upright and can carry its caption). The arrow runs from the block's spot toward its target and stops 2 m short; one scale sets the letters and the arrow's length together, by default from the target's distance. The handles sit in the world, on a circle round the start: four dots for the size, and a control for the bend. Hovering pieces float a little, never under reduced motion; each style's two colours are free, with a warning when the dark one would vanish in sun. The two pages differ only in how the arrow is bent.

  • Ground styles, bend with a knobA turn knob behind the start bends the arrow: clockwise bends it to the right. Look first in creator mode on the guild block: turn the knob and back, then drag a size dot.
  • Ground styles, bend by dragging the middleA handle in the middle of the arrow is dragged sideways to bend it, so the knob only ever turns. Look first in creator mode on the guild block in portrait: drag the handle right and left, then open the editor at phone width, where the bend spans a few pixels.
