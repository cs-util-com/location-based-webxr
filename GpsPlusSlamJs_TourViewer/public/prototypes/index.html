Tour Viewer design prototypes

Design drafts made to choose a direction (polish round M1), kept as an inspiration gallery. They are not connected to the app; buttons only switch states. The Tour Viewer took L5 (the setup page), H1 (the authoring HUD, with an action strip), S1 (the scan overlay), V4 (the visitor's start page) and E1 (the desktop editor); the others are here for anyone who would rather build one of them.

The entry and setup page (for a new creator)

  • L1 - EditorialA calm magazine-like scroll story with large illustrations.
  • L2 - Playful illustratedBright, friendly, a little mascot walks through the steps.
  • L3 - Dark, AR-forwardA dark page whose hero shows AR content appearing over a scene.
  • L4 - App-like wizardOne step per screen, a progress bar, like a native app.
  • L5 - Spatial pathThe steps are stations along a tour route on an illustrated map.

Creating the tour in AR (the authoring HUD)

  • H1 - Floating chipsMinimal chips at the edge; a chip row next to a selected object.
  • H2 - Bottom sheetLarge thumb-friendly buttons in a sheet that changes with the mode.
  • H3 - Drag along the surfaceTap to select, then drag the object along the surface.

Starting a tour as a visitor (the scan overlay)

  • S1 - Centre cardA small translucent card with a phone moving over the code.
  • S2 - ViewfinderCorner brackets frame the view and snap onto the poster.
  • S3 - Coach markAlmost nothing on screen: a pulsing target and a short hint.

The visitor's start page (after scanning a poster)

Made 2026-10-12; the app took V4. A small "Prototype state" control in a corner switches between the page's states: loading the tour, location not yet allowed, location denied, ready, checking AR, AR not supported, and a tour that could not be loaded.

  • V1 - The poster's continuationThe paper look of the printed poster, come alive: the tour's name, "AR tour starts here", one big Start.
  • V2 - Dark, AR-forwardA night scene with the tour's pins already appearing over it, like a game or a camera app.
  • V3 - Three stepsAllow location, start AR, point at the poster - each step ticked off as you go.
  • V4 - Minimal, one tapThe tour's name, one big button in the thumb zone, the details folded away.

The desktop editor (on a computer, after the phone)

Made 2026-10-13; the app takes E1. All three are "full map, floating panels" and differ in where the list of codes and notes and the editing card sit. Open the sample tour; a prototype control fakes a failed save and a browser that cannot write into the file.

  • E1 - Left railThe list as a collapsible rail on the left; the editing card pops up next to the selected marker and follows it.
  • E2 - Right inspectorA compact list in the bottom-left corner; a tall inspector on the right, always in the same place, with the save status.
  • E3 - Bottom stripThe tour in walk order as a strip of cards along the bottom; the editing card top right while something is selected.

The 3D editor on the globe (stations, items, codes)

Made 2026-10-18, before the 3D editor is built. Four directions on a faked town (a drawn street map, box buildings, a hill), all with the same sample tour: four stations with their items, two codes, the recorded walk. Each one shows the station tree, moving and turning an item or a whole station (handles and arrow keys), the radius, adding, a view at eye height, undo and redo. Best on a desktop with a real graphics card.

  • 3D-1 - StudioA dark, docked editor like a game engine: the tree on the left, a numeric inspector on the right, tool keys and a gizmo.
  • 3D-2 - Walk itOpens standing at the first station at eye height; you walk the tour and pick things up to move them, with a mini-map and a bird's-eye view one key away.
  • 3D-3 - StoryboardThe tour as a story in order: a filmstrip of station cards, camera flights along the walk, and a "Play walk" preview.
  • 3D-4 - Map that tiltsStarts as a flat street map and tilts continuously into 3D, down to eye height; map icons stand up as it tilts.

The 3D editor, round 2 (after the owner tried round 1)

Made 2026-10-19 from the four pages above. All four now share one frame: the stations as story cards in a panel docked on the left, an inspector docked on the right while something is selected (suggested actions first, the numbers folded away), one set of handles (a ring with four dots for a station's radius or a model's size, a turn knob, drag the middle to move), a top view (key T) where pins and photos lie flat, a dashed ring where loading starts, and two fingers that only ever move the camera. Each keeps its own idea.

  • 3D-1 round 2 - StudioThe dark docked editor, lighter: no add toolbar, no arrow gizmo, the same camera and first-person view.
  • 3D-2 round 2 - Walk itStill opens at eye height at the first station to walk the tour; the panel is docked and the mini-map is gone.
  • 3D-3 round 2 - StoryboardThe story cards move into a vertical strip on the left, reordered by dragging, with the walk between them; Play walk stays.
  • 3D-4 round 2 - Map that tiltsOpens from above for precise placing and tilts to 3D for heights and looks, with ground shadows and drop lines as height cues.

The 3D editor, round 3 (after the owner tried round 2)

Made 2026-10-19: two converged pages with the same frame and rules, differing only in where they start. Section headers fold, one click in the tree selects, unfolds and flies there (a second click deselects), the tour title opens its details, map controls sit at the left, pins and photos lie flat by the camera's angle, items move only once selected, two fingers tilt, models size in metres, stations carry numbered name boards, and only the selected and the five nearest stations show their items. No eye-height view.

  • Round 3 - StudioOpens in a tilted 3D view; the dark docked editor.
  • Round 3 - MapOpens from above like a map and tilts into 3D.

The visitor's walk in AR (from AR start to the tour's end)

Made 2026-10-21. Four directions for being guided from station to station: every state of the walk (tracking, waiting for a position, a code, GPS, GPS too weak, no trusted position, arriving, a story, the skip and its undo, a fixed-order and an any-order tour, the end, leaving AR). A faked camera you turn by dragging (or by the phone, after a tap); "Play the walk" runs a whole tour in about two minutes, and the demo switcher jumps to any state. The "i" button explains each design.

  • W1 - Calm cardOne bottom card, slim while walking, larger only when a moment needs it; the camera stays clear.
  • W2 - Guidance in the worldA trail on the ground and a beacon over the station, fading as the position becomes less certain.
  • W3 - The guide speaksA guide character carries the walk in short lines; the visitor answers with reply chips.
  • W4 - Radar and listA corner radar with every station and a pull-up list, made for tours in any order.
