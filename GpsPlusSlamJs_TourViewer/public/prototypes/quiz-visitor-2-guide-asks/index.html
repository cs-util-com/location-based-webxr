Design note - Q2

The guide asks

The figure asks where it stands. The answers grow out of the ground as small signs around it, and you answer by tapping a sign in the place. The question can be about what is in front of you: count the columns, read the stone. The HUD holds only a way out.

What stands where

  • The bubble is the one the character already has: same plate, same name line, its tail on the head. It keeps its reading size and flips to the side with room.
  • The signs look placed in the world, but they are placed for the screen. When the question starts they fan out around the figure to fit the view you hold, so all of them are in view and clear of each other, and they fan again if you turn the phone.
  • Any tap near a sign counts: it snaps to the nearest sign within a thumb's width, because a sign 3 m away is small. While a line is showing, a tap anywhere goes on, so nothing has to be reached.
  • The figure reacts with what every character has: four frames (idle, two talk frames, beckon), a hop for right, a sway for wrong, a beckon to try again, and a turn towards the sign you chose. No special artwork is needed.

What happens when

  • Right: the sign lifts and turns gold with a tick, the points rise out of it in the world and show for two seconds at the top, the other signs sink.
  • Wrong, may try again: that sign tips over and stays tipped, the others stay. The tick and the cross carry the meaning, not the colour alone.
  • Wrong, one try: the signs sink, the right one stays standing, no points.
  • Typed: a slate stands by the figure. Tap it and a field opens with the keyboard; the letters appear on the slate as you type. The keyboard does not open by itself: it would hide the place before you have read the question.
  • Done: the signs stand grey as a whole, yours marked, and a tap goes on. No points twice.
  • Costs too much: a quiz that costs more than you have stands grey as a whole with what it needs, so no single answer can give the right one away.
  • Walking away: within 5 m you can answer. Up to 9 m the question stays readable but the signs fold to small marks: "Come closer to answer". Past 9 m the bubble folds to a name tag, as it does today. Past the station the guide says once that the question stays open, and offers to let it go. Coming back finds everything as it was, tipped signs and half-typed words included.

What it trades away

  • Signs stand in thin air. Nothing here knows about walls, cars or people: on a narrow pavement a sign can stand in a hedge or inside a passer-by. There is no hiding behind real objects.
  • Placement decides whether it works. This scene is hand-tuned so that the bubble leaves the well in view. In a real place the bubble or a sign can cover the very thing the question is about.
  • Long answers fight the idea. Try "Long answers": the signs become small cards. Beyond about 30 characters a list would be easier to read.
  • Holding the phone up. Reading and answering in the world means holding the arm up for the whole question, and tapping high up on the screen one-handed is a stretch.
  • The keyboard covers half the world upright and nearly all of it sideways, and how a keyboard behaves inside a real AR session is unproven here.
  • A hash cannot say "almost". A typed answer is right or it is not, and when it is wrong the page cannot show the right word: it does not know it.

Rules this page reads as proposals

  • Feedback lines and a retry setting are not in the tour file yet; the page assumes both.
  • Feedback text sits in the file in clear, so a typed quiz's feedback must not repeat its answer, or the hash protects nothing. The sample's lines avoid it.
  • A wrong answer with a price would be charged on every try. This page has no price on a wrong try.
  • A later right try earns the full points, because the file has one number per quiz.

Not covered here

  • Quizzes with several right answers, number answers and code answers.
  • Voice lines, and how a story with several figures shares one quiz.
  • Ground finding: the signs stand on a flat plane by construction.
