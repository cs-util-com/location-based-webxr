Drag to walk. Go close, or tap a thing.

The Pond Walk

A film, a piece of music and a person stand in this park. None of them has a button.

Sound on, headphones if you have them: you will hear things before you reach them.

What a visitor sees

One rule for all three: go close and it starts, walk off and it waits, tap it to stop it or go again. These are the live scene. Tap a picture to stand there.

How this is decided

What starts each one

  • The film starts when you stand in front of it, within 10 m. From behind the sheet it stays dark: nobody wants a film from the back. The lamp warms from 16 m, so the start is announced.
  • The music starts at 16 m, far before you arrive, at about a twentieth of full loudness and muffled. It is meant to pull you over by ear. A piece that needs its first words heard should not use this: it would get a shorter distance.
  • The person turns to you at 12 m and lifts a hand, but only speaks at 5 m, a distance where people speak to each other. She writes what she says on her slate.

The same rule after the start

  • Walking off holds the item at 13 m, 19 m and 8 m. Coming back carries on where it stopped. The person repeats the line she was in.
  • A finished thing stays finished: passing it again does not start it. A tap does.
  • A tap on a playing thing stops it, and that stop wins: it stays stopped until tapped again, even when you stand next to it. Tapping works up to 12 m.

The item does not know where it stands

Each of the three only knows four moves: run, hold, carry on, start over. A separate starter watches the visitor and calls those moves. In a story, a step would call the same moves and no starter would be attached; the sheet, the gramophone and the person would not change. How close the visitor is does reach the item, but only for how it looks and sounds: a lamp warming, a head turning.

What is faked

  • The park stands in for the camera image, and dragging stands in for walking at about twice a walking pace.
  • The film is drawn on a canvas, the music and the voice are tones, and the words on the slate replace the speech you would hear. A projection this visible in daylight is not physics, but a screen in AR is drawn, so it can be.
  • The distance model is a plain fall-off with a muffled top end and left-right panning, and it never passes through walls or trees.
  • While the person speaks, the other two sounds drop to a third. A real object would not do that; here, her words matter more than the rule.

Where it is weak

  • Without sound the gramophone has little to say from far away: a turning record is not visible at 16 m.
  • GPS can be several metres off. With a 10 m start and a 13 m hold, an error of 3 m can start and stop the film while the visitor stands still. That needs a wider gap between the two distances, or a position from a code.
  • Nothing tells a visitor that a tap restarts a finished thing, apart from a pulsing pilot light on the projector.
  • The slate is easy to read at 3 m and hard at 6 m. That is the point of it and also its cost for anyone who cannot go close.
  • Two visitors at one sheet each see their own film at their own time. Nothing here is shared.
  • Other values would change the feel: at 20 m the music would start out of sight, and at 8 m it would be just a button you walk into.
