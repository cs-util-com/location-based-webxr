Notes

The look of the living clouds, driven by the real cloud map. Everything of the earlier page stays (the march, the three dome generations, the distance scale, the sea, the air); what changes is where the clouds are. How much of a place is cloud now comes from the globe's NASA cloud map, the full 8,192 x 4,096 map, read per step by latitude and longitude the way the globe's cloud shell reads it. The procedural region map keeps the other jobs: cloud size and top, warp, the phase of the life cycle, the base. The life cycle runs at 30 times real time, 15 minutes.

Places. The Place button goes through a front, clear sky, broken tropical cloud and a high latitude. ?lat=&lon= opens anywhere. The camera starts there; flying and the wind then move it over the map. The sea is the page's sea everywhere: land is not drawn.

Grey to cover. The map's grey says what share of a place is cloud, so a law turns it into the dome field's strength, measured at 60 km with one grey over the whole map, so that the share of the picture under cloud (less than half the light through) equals the grey. Up to about 0.45 the domes do it alone. They cannot go past about 0.65, however strong, so above that a closed deck does it.

Overcast. With every dome field full, the 46 km tile would show. So a deck of the domes' height rises under them with the grey, opened by a relief of unrelated periods: its gaps are real gaps, and at grey 1 it is closed. A closed deck would show the shape noise's 3.8 km tile as a lattice; there, a second copy of the noise, turned and scaled by unrelated amounts, breaks it, at one more 3D read in a deck only.

The meter. The panel at the top left shows frames per second, the median and 95th percentile frame, and on devices with a GPU timer the GPU time. Measure draws the whole frame 1, 2, 4 and 8 times per displayed frame, about 10 s, and works out one frame's cost from how the frame time climbs, so it works on a phone without a GPU timer and above the display's rate. Leave the phone alone until the panel's numbers settle; the result is in the Notes.

Colour. ?grade= or the Grade button: aces is this look's own curve at 0.47 (not three.js's ACES, which is scaled differently), neutral the globe's curve, t-neutral80 the same at 0.8. ?gradeexp= sets the exposure. ?pr=tier uses the tier's own pixel ratio instead of the globe's (the device's, at most 2).
