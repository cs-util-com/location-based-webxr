Make your tour

Six steps, one after another.

Start on any computer. The walk happens on site, with an Android phone with Chrome. You do not print anything while you build: the code comes last, when the tour is ready. Open a step to read more.

  1. 1

    Where your tour's file is stored

    Your tour is one small file, kept in storage you already have.On any computer

    Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, GitHub or your own server. The setup gives you an empty starter tour to put there.

    The file stays yours, and nothing is uploaded until you choose to. Once a visitor's phone has loaded the tour, it keeps working without signal.

  2. 2

    The link that opens your tour

    Paste the file's share link once. It is what your code will open.On any computer

    The page checks the link right away. Keep the file where it is: the code points to it.

    When you change the tour later, you replace the file, and the link and the code stay the same.

  3. 3

    Walk the route and place your stations

    At each spot: stand there, tap to place, add a few lines and a picture.On site, with an Android phone with ChromeOn site: this phone can do it

    This is the part the pictures above show. You can start right away, without a printed code: placing works anywhere along the walk.

    You can change the words later, on a computer.

  4. 4

    Save the tour and put it back

    When you finish, the tour is saved on your phone. It replaces the file in your storage.From your phone or a computer

    The page shows how, for the storage you chose in step 1. The link does not change.

  5. 5

    Print the code When ready

    A PDF with your tour's code, printed at actual size.On any computer, then a printer

    Printing can come after the walk, whenever the tour is ready. Each page has a 10 cm bar to check the size.

  6. 6

    Hang it where the walk begins When ready

    Flat on a wall or a post, at about chest height.On site

    Pick a spot a phone can see squarely from about a metre away. Visitors scan it there to open the tour.

About this prototype

Direction

The drawn style of the earlier drawn-phone page, all the way through: the reason first ("you have a story to tell"), a calm drawn phone with what a visitor sees, then the making as a strip of four numbered drawn phones that play one after another (place a station, add text and a picture, hang the code, a visitor sees it), then the setup as sections one under another, then ideas.

Why it should work for this audience

  • A guide or a teacher understands AR fastest by seeing it: a real-looking place, a phone held up, calm cards fixed to the spot.
  • The four pictures are the whole job in the order it is done, each a real screen of the real step, so "how hard is this?" is answered before any setup text.
  • The setup then repeats the same order in words, with printing and hanging marked "when ready", so nothing teaches the opposite of the pictures.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • Order: way in for visitors (a one-line band), motivation, the visitor's view, the four-phone strip, six setup sections, ideas.
  • First screen at 390x844: kicker, the motivation headline and line, the drawn phone, "Make one yourself, without programming", the Android line and both buttons.
  • The hero phone sways once, small (6 px sideways, 2 px up and down, 0.35 degrees, 4 s, after its 2.5 s entry), and never again by itself; a mouse or a sideways drag moves it gently. The place inside the phone stays lined up with the pale place around it.
  • Strip: one lit frame at a time, 5 s each, the others at 62 % opacity and in their final state; a progress bar under the lit one; it starts only when a quarter of it is on screen, stops off screen, in a hidden tab, on Pause, and when the reader swipes or taps a frame (that frame then plays once).
  • On a phone the strip is a sideways row that shows one frame and the edge of the next, with four numbered tabs above it; from 1000 px all four stand in a row.
  • Text inside the drawn phones has a 13 px base size: at 390 px wide the card text is 12.2 px, the card title 14 px, labels and buttons 11.7 to 12 px; only the clock and the small upper-case tags are smaller (9.4 to 9.8 px). A screen narrower than 160 px (a 360 px phone's hero, a phone held sideways) shows the card's title only and a shorter share sheet, so nothing overlaps.
  • The place and the AR marks are separate layers in every screen; the progress bars animate a transform.
  • Setup sections are plain rows with a rule between them, not boxes; each opens for more. Paragraph and list margins are zeroed and every gap is set explicitly.
  • Reduced motion: no entry, no sway, no autoplay; every phone shows its final picture.

Technique

Layered SVG and HTML with CSS transforms. The place is drawn once and reused in all ten views (five screens and five pale backdrops). No WebGL, no canvas, no library, so the page itself is the no-WebGL floor.

Measured cost (desktop machine under other load, not a phone)

  • JS: inline only, about 22 kB raw, 7 kB gzip; no CDN script, no WebGL and no canvas (a run with canvas switched off asked for 0 contexts). The whole page is about 97 kB raw, 26 kB gzip.
  • Frames at 390x844 over 5 s each (hero entry and sway, strip playing, a scroll through the page), two runs each with the CPU slowed 1, 4 and 6 times: p99 17 ms at 1x, 33 ms at 4x (worst 50 ms), 50 to 67 ms at 6x with 1 to 3 frames over 50 ms per 5 s (worst 117 ms). One stall of 0.6 to 1.1 s happens only in the first page of a freshly started browser and never in later pages (browser warm-up). Desktop Chromium on a loaded machine, not a phone.
  • The progress bars animate a transform, not a width: as a width they cost about 880 ms of main-thread time per 5 s of playback, as a transform about 170 to 370 ms.
  • Drawing match: a point of the place drawn inside the hero screen and in its backdrop lands within 0.7 px at rest.

Known weaknesses

  • The hero phone and the last strip frame show nearly the same picture; that is the point (the story comes full circle), but a reader may find it repetitive.
  • On a phone only one strip frame shows at a time, so the four-in-a-row overview exists only as the numbered tabs.
  • The fake codes are patterns, not real codes.

Parameters it rests on

  • Base text size inside the phones: 13 px (?fs=12 / 13 / 14).
  • Hero sway: 6 px (?sway=0 / 4 / 6 / 9 / 14; 14 was the earlier page).
  • Strip frame length: 5 s (?frame=3.5 / 5 / 6.5).
  • Hero phone height on a portrait phone: the screen height minus 470 px, at most 54 % of it (?cut=430 / 470 / 510).
