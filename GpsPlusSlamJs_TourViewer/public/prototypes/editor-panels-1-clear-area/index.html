Clear area: the camera knows the panels

The panels stay docked where they are today, and the camera knows about them: every selection is framed in the uncovered rectangle of the 3D view (a view offset moves the picture's centre there), with a distance chosen so every handle fits in it. On narrow windows the panels give way by fixed, visible rules instead of covering the 3D view.

Defect 1: framing ignored the panels

The camera's centre of projection is moved to the middle of the clear rectangle (the dashed box) and the distance is fitted to the move disc, the turn knob, the size dots and a station's radius dot, not only to the item. The map controls and the rule chip are kept out of that box too. On 1280x800 no panel changes at all; on 1440x900 and 1920x1080 the box is simply wider. Press Today to see the handles land under the details panel instead.

Defect 2: the phone held sideways ran out of room

Two rules, in order: (1) while the details panel is open, the shelf folds to its icon rail (its progress rings stay visible); (2) if the 3D view is still narrower than the target (400 px), the list shrinks to a narrow column. Closing the details panel opens the shelf again; on 844x390 the list stays narrow even then, because the open shelf alone would leave 372 px. On 844x390 the 104 px strip becomes 410 x 326 px with the details panel open. At 700 px wide or less the panels are one bottom sheet, as today, and selections are framed in the part above it. The map controls always sit inside the clear rectangle, so they cannot overlap a panel.

    What it costs (honest weaknesses)

    • The 3D view is only the gap between the panels. On 1280x800 that gap is about 440 px wide, so a selection is shown smaller than it would be with panels floating over a full-width map, and the 3D drawn under the panels is wasted.
    • On a narrow window the layout moves when you select something: the shelf folds and the list narrows. The row you clicked keeps its place, but its name gets cut short. Moving things under the pointer is the price of the rules.
    • In the narrow column, names are cut after about ten characters and nesting is shallower, so finding an item by name is harder (two items called "Guide Hanna" look the same).
    • The picture's centre is not the window's centre. Orbiting turns around a point left or right of the middle, which can feel unfamiliar at first.
    • The phone held sideways still has only about 326 px of height. With the bottom panel open it falls to about 216 px, under the 250 px goal: no rule here handles height.
    • While folded, a shelf file's name shows only on hover or after a tap on the rail; the shelf's details are one tap away while you edit.
    • The target width is a choice. At 460 px rule 1 already fires on 1280x800; above 410 px rule 2 is not enough on 844x390 and the chip turns red. Try it with the slider in the chip.

    Try this

    • Click items in 3D or rows in the list: the camera frames each in the dashed box.
    • Use the size switch (top right) to compare desktop, phone sideways and phone upright, or all three; a selection in one is mirrored in the others.
    • On a narrow window close the details panel (the cross): the shelf and the list come back, and the camera reframes.
    • Drag the move disc, the turn knob, a size dot or a station's radius dot (teal).
    • Open the chip at the top of the 3D view to see why each rule fires.
