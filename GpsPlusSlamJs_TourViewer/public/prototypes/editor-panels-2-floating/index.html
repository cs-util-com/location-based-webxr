Problems 2

Floating cards over a full-window 3D view

The 3D view always fills the whole window; the list, the details and the asset shelf float over it as translucent cards you can fold away, and a selection is framed in the largest area the open cards leave free (the dashed box). On a phone only one card is open at a time, as a sheet from the side that suits how the phone is held, and the map controls move to the side that stays free.

Handles under the details panel

The camera's centre moves into the free area and the selection is sized to sit well inside it (a station fills about 80 %, an item about 40 %, to keep some context), so the move disc, the turn knob, the size dots and the radius dot land beside the cards, never under them. The details sit above the shelf in one right-hand column, so opening them costs height, not width. Fold or open a card and the selection glides into the space that is left.

A phone held sideways

One card at a time, as a side sheet of 340 px: the list from the left, the details, the assets and the problems from the right (where they sit on a desktop). The 3D view keeps the other side, about 440 x 390 px after the map controls, which sit on the edge away from the sheet. Held upright, every card is a bottom sheet of 46 % of the height and the controls go to the top right. A tap on a list row selects and frames the thing while the list stays open; the tabs in a sheet's head switch to the other cards.

What it costs

  • Only the selection is guaranteed in view: its neighbours can sit under a card, and the cards hide the world behind them.
  • The camera moves whenever a card opens or folds. On a sideways phone the list and the details open on opposite sides, so switching between them swings the selection across the screen.
  • On a phone, the list and the details are never seen together.
  • At 1280 x 800 with every card open the free area is about 670 px wide; under about 1100 px wide the cards shrink and it gets narrow.
  • Translucent cards read worse over a bright scene, and the blur costs GPU time on weak phones (it falls back to near-solid cards).
  • The map controls change place between layouts and states.
