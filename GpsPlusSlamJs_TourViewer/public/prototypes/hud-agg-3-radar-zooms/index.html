H-C - The radar zooms into the station. No new layer: the radar the walk already has becomes the one instrument for everything here. Outside a station it shows stations around you. When you arrive it zooms in, and the station's things become small marks at their true bearing and distance. Off-screen things are found by a glance at the radar, never by screen edges. Tap a mark and it becomes the focus: one beacon in the world, one arrow, one line under the radar.

The marks

Ranking rule

  • The radar never ranks. Every thing of the station is a mark at once; nothing competes for a place on screen, so nothing pops when another thing changes.
  • The line under the radar names one thing at most: a weak position first, then the focus (the thing you tapped, or the place you are standing in), then "station done" for a moment.
  • The bottom slot holds one thing: a story step first (the story wins the screen), then a text you tapped open, then the newest thing playing. Everything else stays in the world.
  • Where the walk goes next appears only once the station is done: an orange notch on the radar's rim.

A 7th and an 8th thing

They are two more marks: nothing else changes. Marks that would touch push apart along the ring, so their bearing stays roughly true. A thing farther than the zoom sits on the rim with an outward notch. Ambient sound (the fountain) never claims the bottom slot. Past about 12 marks the instrument would be too dense at 148 px; the zoom then would need to step in (a range control), which this page does not build.

How this differs from the earlier audio "horizon"

The horizon was a second instrument, a strip across the top, for sounds only. This is the same radar the visitor already reads on the walk, for every kind of thing, and it never adds a second instrument: it changes scale.

The strongest case against it

  • It asks the visitor to read a map, at 148 px, in daylight, to learn that something is behind them. A person who never looks up to the corner misses everything off screen, because the screen edges are now silent by design.
  • Marks are small to tap one-handed; the tap area is larger than the drawing, but two marks close together are still fiddly.
  • A weak position makes the map's promise hollow: the marks freeze where they were, and the visitor must trust a picture that no longer moves with them.

What changes in today's walk layer

  • The radar stays, and inside a station it zooms in instead of showing the stations.
  • The walk sheet is not shown inside a station; its peek returns as the line under the radar ("Next: The tower") once the station is done or left.
  • The focus pill and the station arrows are gone inside a station; one arrow points to the one focus.
  • The precision chip becomes the size and colour of your dot on the radar, plus the line when the position is weak.
