    Fast-forward: 2 minutes later

    W2 - Guidance in the world

    The walk is guided by things that stand in the street, not by a panel: a trail on the ground from your feet to the station, a beacon of light over it with its name, a marker at the edge of the screen when it is off to the side or behind you. Arriving is felt in the scene: the beacon sinks into its ring, the ring pulses once, and the station's figure and sign rise out of the ground. Screen text is one subtitle that says the guide's line once and then gets out of the way, plus two small pills (how the guide knows where you are; how far you are through the tour).

    Honest degradation

    • Code: the trail starts at your feet, its chevrons are sharp, the arrival ring is a thin line. Distances in whole metres.
    • GPS, e.g. ±8 m: the trail is wider and blurred, and only starts where the guide is sure, about the accuracy ahead of you; the arrival ring is a soft band as wide as the doubt; distances read "~35 m".
    • GPS too weak (above 25 m): the trail goes. The beacon swells into a wide haze, so it says "roughly over there" and nothing more; its label says "distance unsure". No arrival is judged.
    • No trusted position: every cue goes out: no trail, no beacon, no edge marker, no ring, because the station's place on screen would be a guess. Only the subtitle remains, asking for a code. Station content that only the code placed goes too.
    • Before the direction is known (after a re-entry on GPS), nothing is drawn either, and the subtitle asks for a few steps.

    What it trades away

    • It only works while you look through the phone; held down at your side, nothing guides you (no panel, no map).
    • With no depth, the beacon and the trail are drawn over buildings, as on a real phone; a trail can look painted on a wall where a street bends behind a house.
    • A tour with many stations close together crowds the sky (five beacons at most, the nearest).
    • A visitor who reads rather than looks gets less: the subtitle fades after a few seconds (tap the screen to bring it back; screen readers still get every line).

    The skip question (any order, no trusted position)

    Skip is still offered - a visitor stuck indoors without a position is exactly who needs it, and it keeps every tour completable. But it names no station: "Can't get there?" opens a short list, "Which station can't you get to?", with every station still to find and no distances, and the visitor picks. The world has just stopped pointing at anything, so a skip that named "the nearest" would claim a knowledge the scene itself just took back. The same list is used when GPS is too weak and the two nearest are closer together than the accuracy. In a fixed-order tour the skip names the next station, because the order, not the position, decides which one it is.

    Texts changed or new (proposals)

    • The framework's coaching hints with plain hyphens: "Starting up - point your device at the ground and hold steady.", "Tracking lost - slowly look around to let it recover."
    • New world labels: "~35 m" under GPS, "distance unsure" when too weak, "behind you" on the edge marker.
    • New pills: "Code", "GPS ±8 m", "No position", "Locating…"; tapping one says in one line what it means.
    • New picker: "Which station can't you get to?", "The guide doesn't know where you are right now, so it won't guess.", rows "Skip <title>", "Never mind".
    • New out-of-AR line: "You left AR. Your progress is kept.", button "Back into AR".
    • Not shown: "Walk to a station to see its content." (the beacons say it), and "Find the poster's code to line it up." when the line already asks for a code.
    • Tour complete panel: "Look around", "Exit AR"; the line reads "Tour complete - 1 skipped."
