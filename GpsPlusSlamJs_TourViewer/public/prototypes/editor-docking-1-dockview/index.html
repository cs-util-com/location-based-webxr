One docking system, several arrangements

Every panel is a tab that can share a group, sit beside or below another group, float over the 3D view, or fold to a rail. The 3D view is a panel too: its canvas is exactly its panel, so a selection is framed in the canvas and never under a docked panel. A floating group over the view is avoided as well.

Try it: drag a tab onto the middle of another group to join it, onto an edge of a group to split, onto a tab strip to reorder. The Float button in a group's header lifts it out; Dock puts it back. Fold turns a side group into a rail of upright tabs; tap a tab on the rail to unfold. The layout is saved as you change it and comes back after a reload, separately for desktop, phone sideways and phone upright. Layout holds the arrangements of the three earlier panel prototypes and Reset layout.

On a phone: measured with touch in a headless Chromium

844 x 390 and 390 x 844, touch emulation on, real touch events (press, hold, move, lift) sent to the browser, and the layout read back from the library after each gesture. A phone in the hand may differ in feel; the numbers are what the library did.

  • Works: tap a tab. Tapped an inactive tab; it became the shown one.
  • Works with an adaptation: four panels in one group. Named tabs overflowed into a "2 more" list at 320 px; on a phone a tab that is not shown keeps only its icon (46 x 44 px) and the shown one its name.
  • Works: drag a tab beside or below the 3D view, or onto another group's tab strip. Dropped at the 3D view's edge: the layout split; dropped on a tab strip: the panel joined that group. Both a quick drag and a 300 ms hold before moving start the drag (the library arms a touch drag after 250 ms, or at once when the finger moves). The drop zones are as small as on a desktop, so aiming is the hard part.
  • Works: a drop on the middle of the 3D view is refused, on purpose: the 3D view's group takes no tabs.
  • Works with an adaptation: drag a splitter. The library's grab strip is 24 px wide; on a phone it is 40 px with a visible grip. A drag starting 15 px beside the line moved it the full 100 px the finger moved, sideways and upright.
  • Works with an adaptation: hold a tab for its menu (Float, Maximize). Opens after about half a second (650 ms opened it, 350 ms did not). Its items were 25 px tall; on a phone they are 44 px.
  • Works with an adaptation: float a group. The library floats with Shift and a drag, which a phone does not have, or from the menu above; here every group has a Float button (40 px) and a floating one a Dock button. The float's title bar (26 px on a phone) moves it with a quick drag or after a hold. Dragging a tab out of a float back into the layout worked in 5 of 6 tries; holding 550 ms opens the menu instead. A float covers much of a phone's 3D view (298 x 214 of 844 x 346), and the selection is framed beside it.
  • Works with an adaptation: fold. The library folds only its fixed side bars (see below); folding any group is this page's own: a rail of upright tabs 44 px wide, or a 44 px strip below the 3D view. A tap on a rail tab unfolds it.
  • Works: the library's own side bars (Layout, "Sidebars that fold"). A tap on the shown tab folds the bar to a 44 px strip and back; a tab dragged out of a bar lands in the layout. Upright, two side bars left the 3D view 100 px wide, so there it is one bar at the bottom.
  • Works: one finger in the 3D view turns the camera (55 degrees for 120 px); the panels do not take the gesture. Two-finger zoom is not part of this page.
  • Works: the layout is restored after a reload, sideways and upright, kept apart from the desktop's.
  • Phone default: the 3D view plus one group holding all four panels as tabs, and no tab strip over the 3D view (44 px more of it). Sideways the 3D view keeps 524 x 346 px, upright 390 x 432.

What a splitter drag costs the 3D view

While a splitter moves, the 3D view's canvas is resized once for every pointer move, which means up to once a frame: 88 of 90 frames when the pointer moved every frame (about 60 times a second on a 60 Hz screen), 30 of 101 frames for a finger on a phone. Here one resize, with the new framing and a frame drawn, took 3 to 4 ms at desktop size and 4 to 14 ms at a phone's size, but the whole drag ran at 47 to 69 ms a frame against 16 ms with the size held. That was measured with software rendering in a headless browser and this light scene; a real globe re-creates its render targets on every resize. Layout, Hold its size while a splitter is dragged keeps the canvas as it is during the drag (it is cut off or shows a margin) and fits it once on release: 0 resizes during the drag.

What the library cannot do, or only with a paid licence

  • Paid licence only: side bars that hide and slide in when touched (auto-hide), creating a side bar by dragging a panel to the screen's edge, docking and moving between groups with the keyboard, undo and redo of layout changes, pinned tabs, tabs on several rows, searching the tab list, the drop compass and snapping guides for floats. None of them is used here.
  • Folding: only the library's side bars fold (one per edge, set up in advance; they cannot float). Any other group has no fold; the rail here is made from size limits.
  • Floating with a finger: only through the hold menu; the library's own gesture needs Shift.
  • Touch timings are fixed: a drag arms after 250 ms (500 ms in a float) and the menu opens at 500 ms; no option changes them.
  • Sizes: a group's new size is taken from its neighbour, and a size set while the layout is still changing is lost. Exact sizes here are pinned for a moment (smallest = largest), so the 3D view gives or takes the space. A saved layout loads a folded rail at the library's 100 px minimum, so the sizes are saved alongside.
  • Package: the plain JavaScript package is dockview, which re-exports dockview-core and alone carries the stylesheet; both need mapping when loaded without a build. Core is 394 KB minified (91 KB gzipped) before tree shaking, the stylesheet 160 KB (13 KB gzipped).

Library features this page uses

  • dockview 8.7.1, its stylesheet, and its dark theme's variables set to the editor's colours on an own theme class.
  • The 3D view is a panel rendered always (renderer: "always"), so its canvas survives moves; its group is locked against tabs and has no tab strip on a phone.
  • Own tab and header-button renderers (icons, badges, Fold, Float, Dock); the hold menu offers Float and Maximize only, so no popout windows.
  • Floats are kept inside the window; the library's free side bars in the "Sidebars that fold" arrangement; toJSON and fromJSON for saving, in this browser only.
