Preparing cloud noise...
Notes

Technique. Raymarched cumulus in two passes (a cloud target, then a composite with the sea, the haze and the sun), with a fixed blue-noise start offset per pixel and no history, so a still camera gives a still picture. The cloud field is the living one: a 2048 km region map sets coverage, cloud size, base height and a lifecycle phase, and inside it a 46 km dome tile carries three generations of domes that grow, hold and dissolve over a 15-minute cycle. The march follows a continuous distance scale: its quality and the cloud target's resolution change smoothly with the distance to the cloud layer, with no shader swap and no jump.

What changed, and why.

  • The distance scale. Two numbers follow the distance to the cloud layer through a small table per tier: the march quality (step count, step length, light samples, erosion, cloud shadow) and the cloud pixels per CSS pixel. The loops run to fixed bounds and stop early, and the last step is weighted by the fraction of the limit left, so the picture changes smoothly with the limits. The cloud target is allocated once at the canvas size and drawn into a share of it. The field's filter width comes from a fixed reference (the table's coarsest setting), never from the step actually taken, so a change of quality does not change the cloud shapes.
  • A still empty-space skip (the flicker farther away). The march takes longer steps where a coarse map of the coverage is empty. That map was the live, changing field, so every growing or shrinking cloud changed the step lengths along the whole ray behind it: the sample positions slid along the rays from frame to frame and the cloud surfaces behind blinked. The skip now reads a bound of the shape change, the union of the three generations fully grown: a cloud at any moment of its life lies inside it, and it does not move in time. Test: holding the lifecycle still removed most of the pops, holding the noise rise did not, and the skip change alone brought the pops to the static field's level (numbers below).
  • A refined cloud entry (the layers). At the first sample inside a cloud the march steps back to the last empty sample and crosses the entry again in quarter steps, at most twice per ray. The stripes were the step shells: their strength follows the step count (below), not the shape texture's trilinear creases.
  • Erosion that fades to its mean. Far away, and at the scale's low end, the erosion detail is not sampled. The earlier pages faded it to nothing, so a small dome that erosion eats near the camera survived far away as a small round blob; here the far samples take the erosion's mean value instead.
  • The cycle mean far away. Where a dome texel is smaller than the filter footprint, a cloud's growth and decay is replaced by its mean over the cycle, so no young core is born smaller than a pixel. After the skip fix this is a small effect (below).
  • The frame meter. The display refresh is a sliding-window estimate (the lowest cluster of frame intervals over 1.5 s), shown in the readout with any change it sees, and used by Measure to say whether a setting runs at the display rate. A self-test feeds it 8.3 ms, then 16.7 ms, then 8.3 ms frames.

Toggles (buttons in the bar, or in the URL).

  • ?lod=1|0 - the distance scale (default) or the fixed tiers of the living-field page; ?refine=1|0 - the refined entry; ?refdiv=4|2 - its quarter or half steps; ?skip=static|live - the still skip or the live one.
  • ?field=random|tiled - the region field or the 46 km tile; ?evolve=1|0 - shape change on or off; ?timescale=1|10|60 - its speed (default x60, 0 holds it; the wind and the sea keep real time).
  • ?erosion=mean|fade, ?farlife=1|0, ?stepmul=1|2|4 (every step divided, for the stripe test), ?steps=N (fixed tiers only), ?sea=old, ?tier=low|high, ?view=10km|3km|30km.

Tiers. low: canvas at 0.75 of the CSS pixels; near the cloud layer about 73 primary steps, four light samples and cloud pixels at 0.5 of the CSS pixels, from 7 km on about 59 steps, three light samples and 0.6. high: canvas at device pixels (at most 1.5x), 96 steps, five light samples, erosion detail near the camera, cloud pixels at 0.75 to 1 of the CSS pixels.
