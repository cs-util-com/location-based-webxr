Notes on this prototype

Direction

Do, do not explain: the first screen is a small playground where the reader makes a three-stop tour of a drawn park and then watches it as a visitor, before reading anything else.

Why it should work for this audience

  • A guide, a teacher or a museum person decides whether a tool is "for them" by trying it. Placing a stop and seeing their own sentence appear at that spot is the whole idea of an AR tour, felt in half a minute, with no setup.
  • It answers "is it hard?" by experience: three taps and a button. The six real steps then read as the same action, done on site.
  • The look stays in the light paper family of the current setup page, so the try leads straight into it.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • Phone portrait 390 x 844: headline, the three facts, the Android line and the whole playground (map, editor, See it as a visitor) above the fold; the map is full card width.
  • One-line instruction only: the 1-2-3 rail is the instruction, and it advances (tap, write, see). The tap cue pulses on the map until the first stop.
  • Notes are pre-filled with a suggestion that matches the nearest place (the mill, the pond ...), selected on first focus, so typing is optional and replaces it.
  • Every action is undoable (Undo button, Ctrl+Z); Clear is undoable too. At three stops a tap says why nothing happens instead of failing silently.
  • The visitor preview never strands the reader: Back to editing, Replay, Next stop, and the "Make your real tour" block appears after the walk.
  • Reduced motion: the map starts with an example tour already placed, and the preview steps stop by stop on Next with no motion.
  • Spacing that a design system would reset: the rail gap 4px, editor min-height 128px, the after block 10px margin, paragraph margins 0 by design.

Measured cost (desktop machine, under load; not phone numbers)

  • JS: all inline, 24.3 kB raw, 8.1 kB gzip; the whole page is 66.5 kB raw, 19.7 kB gzip. No library, no external request, no canvas or WebGL.
  • Frames at 390 x 844 over 5 s each: idle, scroll and the visitor walk all at 16.8 ms worst (no throttling); with 4x CPU throttling the walk peaks at 21 to 24 ms, with 6x at 57 to 67 ms (one frame per run). A cold browser's very first frame once took 1.2 s; later loads showed no idle frame over 50 ms.
  • A first-time walk-through (Playwright, touch): 4 taps (three on the park, one on See it as a visitor) with the suggested lines; 6 taps when typing one line and picking a picture. Time to the end of the visitor walk: 18 s at a quick pace, 27 s at a slow pace (4 s reading, 2.5 s per decision), 39 s slow with typing one line.

Known weaknesses

  • A drawn park is not the camera: a reader may still not picture AR. The phone preview is a sketch, not a camera feed.
  • Typing on a phone keyboard hides the map; the suggestion makes typing optional, but a reader who does type loses sight of the pin for a moment.
  • Pins are small on a 360 px phone (touch target about 30 px); dragging needs a steady finger.

Parameters it rests on

  • Up to 3 stops; per stop 1.5 s walking and 2.6 s reading in the preview (try ?dwell=1800 or ?dwell=4000).
  • ?ar=1 / ?ar=0 fake the device's AR support; the toggle at the top does the same.
