About this prototype

Direction

A decision aid, not a landing page: the two candidate layouts for "how making a tour looks" one after the other, with the same drawn phone, the same pictures and the same words, so a reader can say which one they prefer. A is the numbered strip of the drawn-phone strip page, copied unchanged. B is the three-card layout of the earlier extended setup page, with each small icon replaced by the same animated drawn phone.

How the four moments fit into three cards

Card 1 is "Place a station", card 2 "Add text and a picture", and card 3 joins "Hang the code" and "A visitor sees it", because hanging the code is what lets a visitor see the tour. Card 3's phone plays both pictures in turn (5 s each), and its two small buttons show which one is on screen and switch between them. The words are A's captions, the last two joined.

What a reader should notice

  • On a phone held upright, A takes about one screen and is swiped sideways, one phone at a time; B is a column about two and a half screens tall that is read by scrolling, each card complete on its own.
  • On a desktop, A shows four smaller pictures in a row and plays them in a loop; B shows three cards with more room for words, each played once.
  • A carries progress bars, numbered tabs and a Pause button; B carries a dotted route between its numbered cards and a "Play again" button.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • The phones in A and B are the same drawing at the same size: 206 px wide on a phone, at most 232 px on a desktop. B goes to three columns only from 820 px, so its phones never shrink below A's; between 560 and 819 px a card puts its phone beside its words.
  • Text inside every phone has a 13 px base size: at 390 px wide the card text is 12.2 px and the card title 14 px; only the clock and the small upper-case tags are smaller (9.4 to 9.6 px).
  • One layout plays at a time: the one that fills more of the screen. A plays as on the strip page (5 s a frame, from a quarter on screen, stops on Pause, on a tap or a swipe). B plays each card once, in order, when 60 % of its phone is on screen; a card that leaves the screen plays again when it comes back; a tap plays that card alone.
  • The place and the AR marks are separate layers in every phone; the progress bars animate a transform.
  • Reduced motion: nothing plays, every phone shows its final picture, and card 3's buttons switch its two pictures without motion.

Technique

Layered SVG and HTML with CSS transforms; the place is drawn once and reused in all seven phones and their pale backdrops. No WebGL, no canvas, no library, so the page is its own no-WebGL floor.

Measured cost (desktop machine under other load, not a phone)

  • JS: inline only, about 18.5 kB raw, 6.1 kB gzip; no external script. The whole page is about 69 kB raw, 18 kB gzip.
  • Frames at 390x844 over 5 s each (A playing, B playing, a scroll through the page), two runs each with the CPU slowed 1, 4 and 6 times: no frame over 50 ms at 4x (worst 50 ms); at 6x A has 5 to 6 frames over 50 ms per 5 s (worst 100 ms) and B 0 to 1 (worst 50 ms). One stall of 0.8 s happened only in the first page of a freshly started browser (warm-up).

Known weaknesses

  • The two layouts play differently because their shapes ask for it (a looping row against a column of cards played once); a reader may judge the motion rather than the layout.
  • Card 3 carries two pictures and a pair of buttons, so it is busier than the other two cards and than any of A's frames.
  • On a desktop, B's cards 1 and 2 have empty room under their short text, because the phones line up at the bottom.
  • At 1000 px, A's photo slightly overlaps its text card in the "add" frame, as on the strip page it is copied from.
  • The fake codes are patterns, not real codes.

Parameters it rests on

  • Base text size inside the phones: 13 px (?fs=12 / 13 / 14). At 14 the photo overlaps the text card in both layouts at 390 px; 12 fits but is smaller.
  • Frame and scene length: 5 s (?frame=3.5 / 5 / 6.5), the same for A's frames and B's pictures.
  • How much of a card's phone must be on screen before it plays: 60 % (?cv=0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8).
