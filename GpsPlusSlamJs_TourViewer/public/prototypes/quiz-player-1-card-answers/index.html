The card answers

A quiz is one more step of the story, so it lives in the story card: the question as one line, the answers where Continue stood, the guide reacting in the same card. The answers may stand in the square, but you always answer on the card, so walking is never required.

    The rules this page keeps

    • Nothing counts until the one orange or green button: tap an answer again, or tap another, to take it back.
    • Standing somewhere never answers. A weak position changes nothing on the card; only the world's letters fade and the direction arrows go.
    • Typed answers are judged after folding case, white space, accents written out (ü = ue) and a decimal comma, against salted hashes. The page never holds the solution of a text or a code.
    • Every type is handled here: one answer, several answers, a word, a code and a number with its unit. Nothing is handed elsewhere.
    • While the question is on the card, free items (the narration plume, the video) only glow in the world.
    • Leaving with the question open folds the card to one line; coming back opens it with your pick kept.

    Strongest case against

    It is a form. The place carries the answer, but the screen carries the act: a visitor can answer the 1784 question from the gate without looking up once, and the card costs about a quarter of the screen while it is open, the most of any quiz direction. It needs no new format and no position, which is why it is the floor, not why it is the best.
