Edge halo

The middle of the screen belongs to the world. Everything that is not in view lives on the screen's border, as one coloured mark per item at its bearing. Turn toward a mark and it melts into its item; only then does one caption appear.

Ranking rule

  • The frame shows every item of this station that is out of view. When there are more than the symbol limit, the order is: playing, waiting for you, not yet seen, paused, seen; nearer before farther. The rest stay on the frame as plain dots, so nothing disappears.
  • The middle shows one caption: the item in view nearest the middle of the screen. It keeps the caption until another item is clearly nearer, so it does not flicker.
  • The foot shows one thing: the story if it speaks, else a card you opened, else what is playing. Nothing stacks.
  • One voice: starting a sound pauses the other one. The story pauses free sound and hands it back when it ends.

Items of this station, and everything else

  • Inside a station: one symbol mark per item, and a tinted frame says "you are here". Other stations are not shown at all.
  • Outside a station: no item marks. Stations are dots, the next one is an orange flag mark. Items of a station you are not at never show on the screen; they show only their look in the world.
  • The next station joins the frame inside a station only once the station is done (its story finished).

A seventh and an eighth item

They get marks like the others. The symbol limit keeps the frame readable: beyond it a mark is a dot, ranked as above. Turn on 8 items above to see it.

The strongest case against it

  • The edge of a phone is where thumbs and the eye's attention are weakest. In bright sun a 20 px mark at the border may simply not be seen, and the design puts everything there.
  • Bearing is only as good as the position. Near items (3 m) swing right round the frame when the position moves a few metres, and when the position is weak the marks have to widen into smears that say little.
  • Colour carries the item kind. Eight kinds are near the limit of colours told apart at a glance, and the symbol inside a 20 px mark is small.
  • Nothing on the frame is tappable, so a visitor cannot ask "what is that mark?" without turning toward it.
