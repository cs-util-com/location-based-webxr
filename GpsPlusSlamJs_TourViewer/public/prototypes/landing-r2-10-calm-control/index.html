What it is

A tour is a short walk with stations at a real place, such as a park, a museum garden or a school ground. At each station, the visitor's phone shows the notes and photos you put there, fixed to the spot as if they were part of it.

What you need

  • An Android phone with Chrome that supports AR To place your stations on site. Your visitors need one too, to walk the tour. Open this page on a phone and it tells you whether that phone can.
  • Any computer or phone To plan the tour, print its posters, and later fix its texts in the editor.
  • A printer For the posters with the code that visitors scan.
  • Storage you already have Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, GitHub or your own web server. Your tour is one small file there.

You do not need an account, a sign-up, an app to install, or any code.

How it works

Six steps. Steps 1 to 4 work on any device. Step 5 is the walk itself, on site.

  1. Choose where your tour will live

    Pick a storage you already use. This page gives you an empty starter tour to put there.

    Any device

  2. Paste its share link

    Copy the share link of that file and paste it here. Your printed codes point to this link.

    Any device

  3. Print your posters

    Each poster carries a code. Scanning it opens your tour, with nothing to install.

    Any device and a printer

  4. Hang a poster where the tour starts

    Flat on a wall or a post, at about chest height, where a phone can see it squarely from a step away.

    On site

  5. Walk the route and place your stations

    Stand at the poster and start. At each station, place a note or a photo where it belongs. The finished tour is saved on the phone.

    On site, Android phone with Chrome (not this device)

  6. Put the saved tour in place

    Replace the starter file in your storage with the saved tour. From then on, everyone who scans the poster gets your tour.

    Any device

Questions people ask

Does it work on iPhone?

Not for the AR part, today. AR in a web page works in Chrome on Android phones, and iPhones do not offer it to web pages yet.

On an iPhone or a computer you can still plan the tour and print the posters, and on a computer fix its texts in the editor. A visitor with an iPhone can open the tour page but cannot see the tour in AR.

How exact is it?

It is closest near a printed code, which the phone uses to line the tour up with the place. Further away, the phone follows its own movement, so on a long walk a note can sit a little off from where you put it.

How it feels depends on the place and the phone. Try a short route before you plan a big one.

What does it cost?

There is nothing to buy and no account to pay for. Your costs are your own: printing the posters, and storage space if your tour does not fit in a free plan.

Where is my data?

With you. Your tour is one file in the storage you chose, and you can delete it there at any time. Until you save it, everything you place stays on your phone.

There are no accounts, no cookies, no analytics and no advertising. The camera, your location and the motion sensors are used on the phone to place the tour and are not sent anywhere.

If your tour is on Google Drive, phones fetch it through this website's file service, because Drive does not let web pages load files directly. Read the privacy page.

Does it need mobile signal?

To open the tour, yes. Once the tour has loaded, it keeps working without signal while you walk.

Can I change the tour later?

Yes. Open it in the editor on a computer to fix its texts, move its pins or remove photos, then save it to your storage again. Visitors who open it after that see the changes.

About this prototype

Direction

The calm control: no 3D, no animation, no tricks. One quiet, readable page in the spirit of a good public-service or documentation page, to test whether plain clarity does the job the showier variants attempt.

Why it should work for this audience

  • A guide, a teacher or a tourism office decides by reading three questions: what is it, can I do it, what does it need. The page answers them in that order, in plain words, before asking for anything.
  • The honest limits (Android with Chrome, no numbers on exactness) sit in the first screen and in the FAQ, not in small print.
  • The first step of setup is on the page itself, as one plain question, so "landing" and "start" are the same page.

Load-bearing details (check a build against these)

  • Body text 18 px on phones, 19 px from 640 px wide, line height 1.55, measure 37 rem (about 70 characters a line at 19 px). Lede 20/22 px. H1 32 to 48 px, H2 24 to 32 px.
  • Every p, ul, ol and fieldset has its own bottom margin (1.25 rem); sections are 4 rem apart with a thin top rule. A design system that zeroes margins removes the page's whole rhythm.
  • The three facts with a black left bar; the AR status box under them; one green "Start now" with a hard bottom shadow; the visitor inset after it.
  • Focus is a yellow fill with a black underline on links and a yellow ring on controls. Buttons, radios and FAQ rows are at least 44 px tall.
  • Steps are numbered circles with a "where" tag; step 5's tag is the only dark-bordered one and says "(not this device)" without AR.
  • FAQ is native details/summary; the + and minus marks are CSS only.
  • No motion at all, so reduced motion needs nothing. No WebGL, no canvas, no web fonts.
  • Light and dark follow the system setting.

Measured cost

  • JS: about 1.1 kB inline (0.53 kB gzip), only for the AR toggle. No external scripts, styles or fonts. The whole page is 28 kB (8.9 kB gzip).
  • Frames at 390x844 on a desktop (not a phone, machine busy with other work): 5 s idle and 5 s scroll, worst frame 17 ms unthrottled and 33 ms at 4x CPU slowdown. Opening all six answers at once lays out in 13 to 18 ms at 4x. The only frame over 50 ms seen was the browser starting up, in the first tab of a fresh browser, whatever the throttle.

Known weaknesses

  • It shows nothing moving, so it explains AR with one still picture and a sentence. A reader who has never seen AR may still not picture it.
  • It looks like a form from an office, which may read as dull to a museum or a tourism office that wants to be excited.
  • Phone landscape shows the headline and the three facts on the first screen; the AR status and "Start now" are one scroll down. On a small phone (360x740) "Start now" is just below the first screen too.

Parameters it rests on

  • Body size 18 px on phones, tried 16, 18 and 20: the three facts stay on the first screen at every size and viewport; "Start now" stays on a 390x844 first screen at 16 and 18 px and drops below it at 20 px. Measure 37 rem, tried 34, 37 and 40 (58, about 70 and 77 characters a line). Section gap 4 rem.
  • The AR toggle is fake: ?ar=1 or ?ar=0 in the address, or the control at the top. Without either, the page asks the browser.
