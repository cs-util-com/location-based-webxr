Asset optimizer check

Runs the editor's own picture, sound and 3D model optimizers on a file you choose, or on one of the sample files, at the chosen setting and one step either side, so the results can be seen and heard next to the original. The chosen setting has a blue frame. Everything stays in this browser. The sample recordings and photo are in the public domain or under CC0; their sources are listed in playwright-tests/fixtures/asset-optimizer/SOURCES.md.

Pictures

Chosen (the photo setting): longest side 1600 px, WebP at quality 0.6. Its steps: quality 0.5, 0.75 and 0.9, and sides 1280 and 2048 px at 0.6. Next to them, the flat-colour candidates for cartoons and drawings: quality 0.9, and lossless WebP. Zoom into the pictures to compare detail, text and edges. Each result is compared with the original scaled to the same size: SSIM (1 is identical), PSNR (higher is closer; "exact" is lossless) and the near-worst pixel error (0 to 255, the error that only 1 pixel in 1000 exceeds, which shows fringes at edges and smeared text).

The original's card also says how flat it is: the share of its pixels in its 16 most common colours (a cartoon of flat colours is near 100 %, a photo far below).

Sounds

Chosen: speech (a mono sound) as Opus at 24 kbps, music (stereo) at 64 kbps. The steps go far down, to find where a difference becomes audible:

  • speech: 32, 24 (chosen), 16, 12, 8 and 6 kbps, about 234, 176, 117, 88, 59 and 44 KiB a minute (6 kbps is the lowest Opus takes);
  • music in stereo: 96, 64 (chosen), 48, 32, 24 and 16 kbps, about 703, 469, 352, 234, 176 and 117 KiB a minute;
  • music made mono: 32, 24, 16 and 12 kbps.

Each result shows its real size. Opus always codes at 48 kHz here: a lower sample rate saves nothing at the same bitrate (16 kbps from a 16 kHz and from a 48 kHz sound gave the same bytes), so the steps lower the bitrate, and at the lowest ones Opus narrows the sound's band itself. Listen with headphones; decoded, the length must stay the same to the sample (a player shows an Opus file about 7 ms longer: its pre-skip).

3D models

Chosen: textures at most 1024 px (colour as JPEG 0.85, data maps as JPEG 0.92 or kept PNG), 16-bit positions, 8-bit normals, 16-bit texture coordinates, and the default level of detail: triangles thinned as far as the shape stays within 0.25 % of the model's size. The texture and position steps (512 and 2048 px textures, 8-bit positions with a 1 % error budget) keep that thinned mesh, so they show the chosen triangle count; "every triangle" and "a quarter of the triangles" change only the mesh.

One large view: drag to turn, pinch or scroll to zoom. The buttons under it swap only the model; the camera stays where you put it, so a change shows as a jump at the same spot. "Wireframe and flat light" draws every triangle over plain grey with the textures off, so removed geometry is visible; it stays on while you switch. SSIM (1 is identical) compares a drawing of each result with the original's, from one fixed camera.
