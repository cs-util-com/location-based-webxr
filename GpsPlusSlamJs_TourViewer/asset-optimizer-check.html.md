# `asset-optimizer-check.html` - the editor's optimizers, seen and heard

## Purpose

A standalone check page that runs the editor's own picture, sound and 3D model optimizer rows (`ASSET_OPTIMIZERS`, through the real Worker) on a file the person chooses, or on a sample file, at the chosen setting and one step either side, and shows each result next to the original: pictures to look at (with size, share of the original, SSIM and time), sounds to listen to (with size, length in milliseconds and time), 3D models in one large view with a geometry slider and a texture slider that change only the model (with size, share of the original, texture and geometry bytes, triangles, largest texture side, the SSIM of fixed drawings and time).

It is a Vite build entry (`vite.config.ts`), reachable on a deployed branch preview at `/tour/asset-optimizer-check.html` and linked from the prototype gallery (`public/prototypes/index.html`) only; nothing in the Tour Viewer imports it. Its wiring is the page's own inline script; the generated test files and the measures are `src/checks/asset-optimizer-check.ts` (unit-tested).

## Outdated: the model part

The model part (the large view, its sliders and options) is being redesigned: three model import prototypes are in the prototype gallery (`public/prototypes/`, anchor `#2026-11-16-model-import`). The page says so in a note at the top of its model section, linked to that anchor. Until one is chosen the part stays as it is, a research tool and not the design; superseded parts are marked, not removed.

## What it shows

- **Pictures:** the photo setting, longest side 1600 px at WebP quality 0.6 (chosen, blue frame); its steps, quality 0.5 and 0.75 at 1600 px, and 1280 and 2048 px at 0.6; and the flat-colour candidates, quality 0.9 and lossless (quality 1, which Chromium writes as lossless WebP) at 1600 px. Each card names its setting, size, share of the original, SSIM, PSNR ("exact" when lossless), the near-worst pixel error and time; the original's card the share of its pixels in its 16 most common colours (how flat it is). Samples: a real street photo, 2400 x 1800, and two generated cartoons of 2400 x 1800 as PNG (`cartoonPicturePixels`): flat colours, and the same figure shaded.
- **Sounds:** steps far down, to find where a difference becomes audible. A mono sound as speech at 32, 24 (chosen), 16, 12, 8 and 6 kbps (6 is the lowest bitrate Opus takes); a stereo sound as music in stereo at 96, 64 (chosen), 48, 32, 24 and 16 kbps, and made mono at 32, 24, 16 and 12 kbps. The intro lists the KiB a minute of each; each card its real size. What the encoder path offers, measured in Chromium: `AudioEncoder` takes Opus at 6 to 510 kbps (not below 6), at sample rates 8 to 48 kHz, but a lower rate saves nothing at the same bitrate (16 kbps from 16 kHz and 48 kHz: the same bytes), so the steps lower the bitrate; the encoder options Chromium accepts (`application`, `signal`, `frameDuration`, `complexity`, DTX) are not set by the shelf's encoder (measured at 8 kbps on a generated tone: 60 ms frames about 9 % smaller, `application: "voip"` about 2 %; DTX not measured), so the page does not offer them. The shelf's own defaults are unchanged by these steps. The samples are real recordings: German speech (20 s, MP3 at 22.05 kHz), English speech (20 s, MP3 at 44.1 kHz), piano (15 s, FLAC at 44.1 kHz) and orchestra (15 s, FLAC at 48 kHz), so the page's resampling is exercised. Each result says whether, decoded at 48 kHz, it has the original's number of samples (a player's millisecond length of an Opus file is about 7 ms longer, its pre-skip, so it is not the measure).
- **3D models:** one large three.js view at full width (OrbitControls: drag to turn, pinch or scroll to zoom). A caption above it names the original: size, triangles, largest texture side and texture bytes. Under it the shown file's size, large, with its share of the original and its split into texture and geometry bytes (`glbByteShares`), next to a "Show the original" checkbox; then two sliders, one under the other, both "right means more":
  - **Geometry**: the triangles kept up close (the tour draws coarser levels farther away on its own). Its stops are the shelf's own (`detailStops` of `shelf-panel-model.ts`, from the default run's level-of-detail report), its readout the shelf's (`detailReadout`): share, "(default)" on the level the budget chose, triangles, largest deviation. Stops that keep the same number of triangles (the floor of a small model) are kept as the shelf has them.
  - **Texture**: 128, 256, 512, 1024 (default, the shelf's), 2048 px and the original size.
  - Two options, measured to save: WebP textures in place of JPEG (an `EXT_texture_webp` file), and 8-bit vertex positions with a 1 % budget, labelled as the mesh's points, not the textures. "Wireframe and flat light" stays as before. Palette or indexed textures and KTX2/Basis are not offered: no browser encoder writes either, and the tour refuses `KHR_texture_basisu` (`checkGlbInert`).
  - A slider or checkbox changes only the model: the camera is never touched. Each variant is made once, on release of the slider, and cached by its settings (`variantSettings`); while it is made the one before stays in view, the facts line says so and the view is `aria-busy`. A variant that failed is not cached: the facts line names the failure, and asking for it again retries it. A variant still being made when another model is loaded is dropped without a word: it takes the original and its drawing before its first await, so it is never compared with the new model.
  - GPU memory: only the drawn model is on the GPU. A model swapped out is disposed (three uploads it again if it is shown again, its images kept on the CPU side), and each fixed SSIM drawing disposes its scene once read. The e2e pins it with `renderer.info.memory`.
  - Samples: the scanned rock (`public/prototypes/model-lod-1/`, 1024 px textures), a marble bust (a face) and a utility box (straight edges, lettering), both with 2048 px textures, the last two Poly Haven models under CC0. The SSIM compares 256 x 256 px drawings from one fixed camera (`framePose`), lit by an ambient and a directional light. The view's state (the sliders, the options, the camera pose, the loaded model's geometry stops, triangles and size, and which variant is drawn) is `src/checks/model-compare-view.ts`, a pure reducer; three.js objects and the cache of made variants stay in the page as resources.
  - Like every page with a slider it installs the framework's page-wide slider guard (`guardSlidersIn(document)` from `gps-plus-slam-app-framework/utils/slider-scroll-guard`), so a vertical swipe that starts on a slider scrolls the page on a phone; `tests/repo-config/slider-pages-load-the-guard.test.js` holds it to that.

## Slider order: geometry first, then texture

Measured with the shipped optimizer on the three samples (KiB, total = textures + geometry):

- Original: rock 664 (435 + 228), bust 1 613 (1 204 + 409), box 1 350 (1 209 + 141).
- The default (1024 px, the default level): rock 567 (435 + 132), bust 730 (624 + 106), box 592 (562 + 31). Textures are 77 to 95 % of the made file.
- Texture slider at the default level, 128 / 256 / 512 / 1024 / 2048 / original: rock 151 / 190 / 311 / 567 / 567 / 567 (its textures are 1024 px); bust 126 / 171 / 311 / 730 / 1 310 / 1 310; box 50 / 89 / 212 / 592 / 1 237 / 1 237. The 2048 px step is the original on every sample: a 4k texture set does not fit the repository's 2 MiB file ceiling (the bust's three 4k maps are 5.4 MB), and on its 4k download, measured before it was re-encoded, 2048 px made 2 270 KiB against 797 at 1024 and 5 291 at its own size.
- Geometry slider at 1024 px, share 0.05 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1: rock 447 / 480 / 520 / 595; bust 644 / 703 / 774 / 913; box 577 / 595 / 616 / 656.
- So the texture slider spans 4 to 25 times the bytes (10 to 100 times on 4k textures), the geometry slider 1.1 to 1.4 times. Geometry comes first because what a texture step looks like depends on the mesh it is drawn on, the wireframe judges geometry without textures, and the larger byte lever is the last choice, made on the final mesh. The reverse order would have the author pick a texture on a mesh still to change. What would reverse it: a model whose geometry is most of its bytes (an untextured scan of millions of triangles), where the geometry slider is the size lever; it would still come first.
- Options: 8-bit positions save 1 to 3 % of the file (rock 567 to 550, bust 730 to 716, box 592 to 587). WebP at the same quality numbers saves 11 to 26 % of the file (rock 507, bust 609, box 438).

## Samples

The sample files live next to the e2e in `playwright-tests/fixtures/asset-optimizer/`, with their sources and licences (public domain or CC0, each checked on its source page) in `SOURCES.md` there. The page imports each by URL (`?url`), so Vite serves them in development and copies them into the build; `sample(name)` fetches one as a Blob (`photo`, `speech-de`, `speech-en`, `piano`, `orchestra`) and rejects with words for an unknown name or a failed load; `sampleModel(name)` does the same for the models (`rock`, `bust`, `box`).

## For the e2e

`window.assetOptimizerCheck` carries `optimizePicture(blob, maxSidePx, quality)`, `optimizeSound(blob, bitsPerSecond, channels?)` (`channels` overrides the music setting's; a mono source is speech, one channel) (each resolves `{ blob, extension, width?, height?, ms }` or rejects with the row's words), `sample`, `testPicture`, `cartoonPicture(width, height, shaded)` (a PNG) and `testSound` (the generated files, which the e2e keeps for exact lengths over sample rates, the 3-minute memo and the 12 MP timing), `pictureSsim`, `comparePicture(original, optimized)` (`{ ssim, psnr, nearWorst }` against the original scaled to the result's size), `colourShare(blob, top?, bits?)` (the common-colour share on a copy of at most 256 px a side, nearest pixel), `decode`, `durationMs` and `bestLag`; for models `optimizeModel(blob, modelSettings)`, `modelFacts(blob)` (`{ min, max, triangles, textureSide, chainBytes, inert }` as three.js and the tour's check see the file; `chainBytes` the bytes of the coarser levels' index lists read from the file's JSON), `compareModel(blob, modelSettings)` (the result with both files' facts and the drawings' `ssim`) `testModel(name)` (a scanned model of the prototype by name), `sampleModel(name)`, and `modelView()` (the large view's state, three.js's camera position and target, the geometry stops, the asked and shown variant keys, `busy` while the asked variant is made, the shown file's triangles, largest texture side and bytes, the original's bytes, how many of the shown model's meshes have wire lines drawn and their own textured material on, and `gpu`: `view` and `draw`, the `renderer.info.memory` counts of the large view's renderer and of the fixed drawings' one). `playwright-tests/editor-asset-optimizers.spec.js` drives them.

## Invariants

- Settings are the shipped `OPTIMIZER_SETTINGS` with only the swept field changed; a sound's speech or music settings are chosen by the shipped row (by its channels), so both carry the variant's bitrate.
- While a picture, sound or model run is going, that section's buttons and file input are disabled and the section is `aria-busy`; the status line counts the results ("Making picture 3 of 7: ...") and ends with "Pictures done: 7 results." (a model: "Model ready.", and again after each variant a slider asks for) or "Failed: " and the reason, and the controls come back either way: only those the run disabled, after which the section's own render sets the ones it rules (a geometry slider with a single stop stays disabled).
- WebCodecs needs a secure context: the page works on https and on localhost, not on a plain-http address.
- The runner's judgement (10 % smaller, the caps) is not applied here: the page shows every result.
