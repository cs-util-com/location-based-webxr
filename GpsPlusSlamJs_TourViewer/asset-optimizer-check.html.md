# `asset-optimizer-check.html` - the editor's optimizers, seen and heard

## Purpose

A standalone check page that runs the editor's own picture, sound and 3D model optimizer rows (`ASSET_OPTIMIZERS`, through the real Worker) on a file the person chooses, or on a sample file, at the chosen setting and one step either side, and shows each result next to the original: pictures to look at (with size, share of the original, SSIM and time), sounds to listen to (with size, length in milliseconds and time), 3D models drawn by three.js from one camera next to the original's drawing (with size, share of the original, triangles, largest texture side, the SSIM of the two drawings and time).

It is a Vite build entry (`vite.config.ts`), reachable on a deployed branch preview at `/tour/asset-optimizer-check.html` and linked from the prototype gallery (`public/prototypes/index.html`) only; nothing in the Tour Viewer imports it. Its wiring is the page's own inline script; the generated test files and the measures are `src/checks/asset-optimizer-check.ts` (unit-tested).

## What it shows

- **Pictures:** the photo setting, longest side 1600 px at WebP quality 0.6 (chosen, blue frame); its steps, quality 0.5 and 0.75 at 1600 px, and 1280 and 2048 px at 0.6; and the flat-colour candidates, quality 0.9 and lossless (quality 1, which Chromium writes as lossless WebP) at 1600 px. Each card names its setting, size, share of the original, SSIM, PSNR ("exact" when lossless), the near-worst pixel error and time; the original's card the share of its pixels in its 16 most common colours (how flat it is). Samples: a real street photo, 2400 x 1800, and two generated cartoons of 2400 x 1800 as PNG (`cartoonPicturePixels`): flat colours, and the same figure shaded.
- **Sounds:** steps far down, to find where a difference becomes audible. A mono sound as speech at 32, 24 (chosen), 16, 12, 8 and 6 kbps (6 is the lowest bitrate Opus takes); a stereo sound as music in stereo at 96, 64 (chosen), 48, 32, 24 and 16 kbps, and made mono at 32, 24, 16 and 12 kbps. The intro lists the KiB a minute of each; each card its real size. What the encoder path offers, measured in Chromium: `AudioEncoder` takes Opus at 6 to 510 kbps (not below 6), at sample rates 8 to 48 kHz, but a lower rate saves nothing at the same bitrate (16 kbps from 16 kHz and 48 kHz: the same bytes), so the steps lower the bitrate; the encoder options Chromium accepts (`application`, `signal`, `frameDuration`, `complexity`, DTX) are not set by the shelf's encoder (measured at 8 kbps on a generated tone: 60 ms frames about 9 % smaller, `application: "voip"` about 2 %; DTX not measured), so the page does not offer them. The shelf's own defaults are unchanged by these steps. The samples are real recordings: German speech (20 s, MP3 at 22.05 kHz), English speech (20 s, MP3 at 44.1 kHz), piano (15 s, FLAC at 44.1 kHz) and orchestra (15 s, FLAC at 48 kHz), so the page's resampling is exercised. Each result says whether, decoded at 48 kHz, it has the original's number of samples (a player's millisecond length of an Opus file is about 7 ms longer, its pre-skip, so it is not the measure).
- **3D models:** textures at most 1024 px with 16-bit positions at the default level of detail (chosen: the fewest triangles within the deviation budget, `model-lod.ts`), every triangle kept, a quarter of the triangles, 512 and 2048 px textures, and 8-bit positions with a 1 % error budget. Each card names its triangle count. The test model is the scanned rock in `public/prototypes/model-lod-1/`. A drawing is 256 x 256 px, lit by an ambient and a directional light, the camera framed on the original's box.

## Samples

The sample files live next to the e2e in `playwright-tests/fixtures/asset-optimizer/`, with their sources and licences (public domain or CC0, each checked on its source page) in `SOURCES.md` there. The page imports each by URL (`?url`), so Vite serves them in development and copies them into the build; `sample(name)` fetches one as a Blob (`photo`, `speech-de`, `speech-en`, `piano`, `orchestra`) and rejects with words for an unknown name or a failed load.

## For the e2e

`window.assetOptimizerCheck` carries `optimizePicture(blob, maxSidePx, quality)`, `optimizeSound(blob, bitsPerSecond, channels?)` (`channels` overrides the music setting's; a mono source is speech, one channel) (each resolves `{ blob, extension, width?, height?, ms }` or rejects with the row's words), `sample`, `testPicture`, `cartoonPicture(width, height, shaded)` (a PNG) and `testSound` (the generated files, which the e2e keeps for exact lengths over sample rates, the 3-minute memo and the 12 MP timing), `pictureSsim`, `comparePicture(original, optimized)` (`{ ssim, psnr, nearWorst }` against the original scaled to the result's size), `colourShare(blob, top?, bits?)` (the common-colour share on a copy of at most 256 px a side, nearest pixel), `decode`, `durationMs` and `bestLag`; for models `optimizeModel(blob, modelSettings)`, `modelFacts(blob)` (`{ min, max, triangles, textureSide, chainBytes, inert }` as three.js and the tour's check see the file; `chainBytes` the bytes of the coarser levels' index lists read from the file's JSON), `compareModel(blob, modelSettings)` (the result with both files' facts and the drawings' `ssim`) and `testModel(name)` (a scanned model by name). `playwright-tests/editor-asset-optimizers.spec.js` drives them.

## Invariants

- Settings are the shipped `OPTIMIZER_SETTINGS` with only the swept field changed; a sound's speech or music settings are chosen by the shipped row (by its channels), so both carry the variant's bitrate.
- While a picture or sound run is going, that section's buttons and file input are disabled and the section is `aria-busy`; the status line counts the results ("Making picture 3 of 7: ...") and ends with "Pictures done: 7 results." or "Failed: " and the reason, and the controls come back either way.
- WebCodecs needs a secure context: the page works on https and on localhost, not on a plain-http address.
- The runner's judgement (10 % smaller, the caps) is not applied here: the page shows every result.
