# mesh-distance.ts

**Purpose:** the sampled symmetric surface distance between two triangle meshes, the deviation the level-of-detail pass (`model-lod.ts`) picks its default level by and stores per saved level.

## Public API

- `surfaceDistance(PA, IA, PB, IB, samples = 20000, gridA = null)` returns `{ mean, max }` in model units: `samples` points by area on each mesh, each measured to the nearest point of the other. `max` estimates the Hausdorff distance from below. `gridA` is a `SurfaceGrid` of mesh A reused by a caller comparing many levels against one full mesh. Two empty meshes read `{ 0, 0 }`; one empty against a non-empty one reads `Infinity`.
- `SurfaceGrid(P, I, maxCells = TRIANGLE_CELLS)` with `nearest(x, y, z)` and `entries`: a uniform grid of triangles, about two triangles a cell (each extent counted as at least 1 % of the largest, so a flat mesh gets finite cells; at most 256 cells an axis), searched in growing shells until no nearer triangle can lie outside the shell. A triangle is filed in every cell its bounding box covers, unless that is more than `maxCells` (`TRIANGLE_CELLS`, 32): such a large triangle goes to a list every query measures directly, before the shell search. `entries` counts the references held (cells plus the large list), at most `maxCells` a triangle.
- Internal to the module, `TRIANGLE_CELLS`: 32, the default cap above. Ordinary triangles, about two to a cell, cover a few cells each and never reach it.
- Internal to the module, `samplePoints(P, I, n, seed)`: `n` points uniform by area, xyz each.
- `pointTri2(px, py, pz, P, a, b, c)`: the squared distance from a point to a triangle (`a`, `b`, `c` offsets into `P`), by the closest-point regions. **Approximate below a height of 1e-5 of the longest edge:** such a triangle, a legitimate one in a decimated mesh included, is measured by its edges (an over-read of at most half its height), so a caller needing an exact interior distance there must not reuse it. In detail: a zero-area triangle, a sliver whose height is under 1e-5 of its longest edge (`SLIVER2` is the square, 1e-10; the region tests would read rounding noise as a region there; the segments over-read by at most half the height), and a sliver whose region products underflow (to 0/0, or to an area sum whose reciprocal overflows) although its squared area stays a positive subnormal, by its three segments, never NaN or Infinity. The threshold balances the bounds of the two over-reads (segments below it, region noise above it) near h/L = 6e-6; a 300 000-run corner property passed with the squared value at 1e-8 to 1e-16 (the shipped 1e-10 in two separate runs) and failed at 1e-20, which shows no over-read past a corner, not accuracy. The tests pin the threshold from above only (a thin real triangle at h/L = 1e-4 keeps its interior, so the squared value stays under 1e-8). Nothing pins it from below: a random search of slivers from h/L = 5e-6 down to 1e-10, measured by the region path, read within about 5e-11 of the longest edge of the honest band, so no committed case reads region noise just under the threshold.

## Invariants

- Seeded (mulberry32 with fixed seeds 1 and 2): the same meshes always give the same answer, so a dropped file always gets the same default level.
- `max` never exceeds the true largest distance, up to an over-read of half the height on a sliver under 1e-5 of its longest edge, and the region path's rounding noise on a sliver just above it (bounded by about eps * (L/h)^2 of the edge L, measured far smaller); with 20 000 samples a side it reads within a few percent of it on the scanned models.
- **Memory is linear in the triangles, whatever their shape.** Filing each triangle in its whole box used to cost O(T^2) for a mesh of long triangles: a flat mesh has about T/2 cells and a triangle spanning it covers nearly all of them. Measured: 1 000 diagonal triangles over a flat square get 37 x 37 x 1 = 1 369 cells and held 1 369 000 entries (about 1.4 million); at 10 000 they would get 80 x 80 x 1 = 6 400 cells and about 64 million entries, past a phone Worker's memory (the level-of-detail pass builds one grid per pose). With the cap they hold at most 32 a triangle (all 1 000 there are large: 1 000 entries).
- **Queries stay exact, up to a triangle's own rounding.** A large triangle is measured by every query, so none is missed; the shell search stops only when no filed triangle outside it can be nearer than the best found, large ones included. The price is time, not memory: a mesh made mostly of large triangles is searched nearly by brute force (T distances a query), slow for tens of thousands of them. A large triangle whose bounding box is already farther than the best distance found is skipped without measuring it (a triangle is never nearer than its box, so this stays exact up to the triangle's own rounding; on a tie it is measured, since at subnormal distances a triangle can read below its box). Measured on a flat mesh of 20 000 triangles, half of them small and half medium faces of 0.15 of its side (10 000 large), `surfaceDistance` with 5 000 samples a side: 7.4 to 27.5 s before the skip (median 20.0 s over five runs) and 3.9 to 17.3 s with it (median 6.8 s over four), on a loaded desktop, so the spread is the load's. The optimizer client's watchdog bounds how long such a run may stay silent, not how long it may take: the pass reports after each measurement.
- Positions and indices are any `ArrayLike<number>`; nothing is validated (an internal kernel: its caller passes indices it produced).

## Tests

- `mesh-distance.test.ts`: `pointTri2` in every region, on zero-area triangles, an underflowing sliver and a sliver whose region tests read rounding noise (all shrunk counterexamples of the property), a thin but real triangle (height 1e-4 of its edge) measured by its interior with each corner as `a`, which pins the sliver threshold from above (its squared value under 1e-8); as a property (100 unseeded cases), never farther than the nearest corner; zero for a mesh against itself and repeatable; a sphere lifted by h reads close to h and never above it; a reused grid gives the same answer; a flat mesh measured without the grid blowing up; an empty mesh reads infinitely far; a flat mesh of 1 000 long triangles holds one entry a triangle, a probe triangle covering 32 cells is filed in 32 and one covering 33 is a single large entry, and it answers as a search over every triangle does (the large-triangle box skip only skips a box strictly farther than the best, since at subnormal distances a triangle can read below its box on a tie).
- `mesh-distance.property.test.ts` (seed 7, 200 runs of up to 40 triangles): on random meshes mixing small and long triangles, flat or not, with the cap at 1, 2, 8 or its default, `nearest` equals the square root of the smallest `pointTri2` over every triangle, exactly.
