# `three/woods-governor.ts`

## Purpose

On a phone too slow for the invented woods, draw fewer of them: the owner's rule "above 50 ms a frame the library lowers the density on the phone automatically, the desktop stays full". Pure: no three.js, no clock of its own.

## Public API

- `WOODS_GOVERNOR`: the thresholds, frozen. `slowMs` 50, `fastMs` 36, `slowS` 2, `fastS` 12 (counted seconds), `worthwhile` 0.15, `restS` 300, `smoothing` 0.1, `gapMs` 250, `spikeLimit` 4, `shares` `[1, 0.5, 0.25]`.
- `createWoodsGovernor({ auto = true })` returns:
  - `frame(atMs, chained)`: one frame drawn at `atMs` (a monotonic clock in ms, e.g. the rAF timestamp); `chained` when it was requested from within the previous frame's callback. A non-finite time, or one not after the last, is ignored.
  - `state()`: `{ share, mode, averageMs }`. `share` is one of the ladder; `mode` is `off` (auto off), `full`, `thinned` or `resting` (full after a step that bought nothing); `averageMs` is the smoothed counted interval, undefined before the first.
- Types `WoodsGovernor`, `WoodsGovernorState`, `WoodsGovernorMode`.

The share is applied by `applyWoodsShare` (`city-objects.ts`).

## Invariants & assumptions

- **Only chained frames are measured.** A consumer that draws on demand (OsmDemo) would otherwise read every idle pause as a slow frame and strip the woods on every phone. The first frame of a burst sets the clock and measures nothing. A consumer with a continuous loop passes `chained: true` every frame.
- **One frame longer than `gapMs` says nothing** (a hitch, a tab coming back mid-burst); two in a row do, or a device at a quarter-second a frame would never step down. A counted interval credits the slow, fast and bought seconds at most `gapMs`, and enters the average at most `spikeLimit` (4) times the average: the second of two long hitches cannot fill the two slow seconds, neither by itself nor by the seconds an inflated average takes to decay on a device just under the line (swept over devices at 16.7, 33.3, 40, 45, 47 and 49 ms and second hitches of 0.4 s to an hour; a limit of 16 thins 5 of those 36 pairs, 2 and 8 thin none). **A fresh average is seeded unlimited, then smoothed**: the session's at the median of its first three counted frames, a rung's after a step at the smaller of its first two. While a seed is incomplete the average is the lower median of the frames so far (the first frame, then the smaller of two), and the verdicts credit their seconds against it as usual; no verdict can fire before a seed is complete, since three counted frames credit at most three `gapMs`, 0.75 s, under `slowS`. A device at 300-2000 ms a frame still reads as slow as it is from its first counted frames, so the worthwhile test sees what thinning saved. A single hitch among the first three counted frames, or one long frame after a step, is not the start the limit then defends; two outliers in the same direction among the first three are the seed themselves (the open limits below). Capping the average at `gapMs` instead made every step on a slow device look worthless.

- **The limit's margin is narrow**, and it is a trade in both directions. A higher limit lets more hitch pairs through: the cheap-woods test grid below first fails at 4.5 (4 of 96 end thinned) and at 5; with 0-10 % jitter, of 2016 pairs mid-session on devices at 16.7-49.5 ms, 16 thin at 3, 25 at 4, 35 at 4.5 and 41 at 5, against 44 on main. Those counts are one jitter sample per case: two further samples of the same grid give 8 and 14 at 3, 15 and 34 at 4, 19 and 39 at 4.5, 27 and 45 at 5, against 27 and 50 on main, so the order holds and the counts move by a factor of two. A lower limit hides real stutter: of 123 regular stutters with a mean over 52 ms (every 2nd-10th frame 60-245 ms on 8.3-40 ms devices), 16 never thin with no limit or at 5 and above (their smoothed average swings around the line), 22 at 4, 26 at 3.5 and 35 at 3, and at 3 a 16.7 ms device with every 4th frame at 200-245 ms (a mean of 63-74 ms) keeps its full woods for good. 4 is the value both tests hold at. The stutters 4 misses and main catches are an accepted limit, and wider than this grid shows: six here (every 4th-10th frame at 245 ms, means of 60-67 ms), and with 5-15 % jitter 34 of 480 (means over 55 ms, every 2nd-10th frame 60-245 ms on 8.3-40 ms devices, two phases, two seeds), all with long frames of 200-245 ms, 200 ms included; on that grid 3 misses 95, 5 misses 5, and main itself never thins 17. The seed rule does not move these counts.
- **The trade the limit makes**: a run of long frames on a fast device now climbs the average by at most four times a step, so a run of 260-3000 ms frames on a 16.7 ms device thins after 13 frames (main took 10), and a sudden steady slowdown from 16.7 ms to 260 ms thins after 3.4 s against 2.6 s, to 1000 ms after 13 s against 10 s; from 33.3 or 45 ms the delay is at most a second. A device that genuinely runs at seconds a frame from the start thins as before, because its fresh average starts at its own frames.
- The smoothed interval above `slowMs` for `slowS` of counted frames steps one rung down; under `fastMs` for `fastS` steps one rung up. The ladder ends at 0.25: never no woods.
- **Each rung is judged by its own frames**: at a step the average starts afresh at the next counted frames (the smaller of the first two). A device at a second a frame has eight counted frames a verdict, too few for the smoothing to forget the rung before. The interval right after a step is the step's own frame (the woods re-sort their draw order) and counts toward nothing: neither the fresh average nor a verdict starts at it (swept over full woods at 60-100 ms, a half at 60-84 % of that and a post-step frame of 80-250 ms: no verdict changes; counting it, a fresh average at a second a frame carries about 43 % of it). When the re-sort takes two frames, the second is the fresh average's first counted frame and the smaller of the two seeds it, so two post-step frames 1.1-5 times as long change no verdict either (seeding at the first frame alone, 79-90 of 500 flip; with 0-5 % jitter, full woods at 60-120 ms and halves at 60-90 %, 2-7 of 500 flip against 33-48 on main). A longer second frame after a step is dropped from the seed rather than smoothed in at `smoothing`; measured against smoothing it in, that moves the jitter counts by at most 4 of 800 and every other count above by at most 1. Three such frames are a regression against main, an accepted one: 95-101 of those 500 flip against 63-79. Ending the burst at a step instead (the next frame measuring nothing, as a burst's first) is not equivalent to skipping the step's frame: 159 of 3000 random fuzz sequences diverge, though every test here passes with either.
- **The worthwhile test**: from the second slow verdict on, if the average is above `(1 - worthwhile)` of the average when the thinning began (the baseline, taken only at the full woods), the woods were not the cost; the share returns to 1 and the step-down rests `restS`. A 30 Hz cap or a saving mode therefore costs one thinning try every five minutes. On a slow device the half rung saves half the woods' share of the frame, so woods at 30 % of the frame or more thin to a quarter and at 20 % come back and rest (swept 150-2000 ms). **A known limit, the baseline's age**: the baseline is the smoothed average when the first slow verdict fires, a few frames old, and on a device at a second a frame (eight counted frames a verdict) it still carries about 43 % of its seed (0.9 to the 8th). Runs of long frames under `gapMs` at a session's start therefore lift it and thin near-line devices, on main and here alike. A different baseline is a design question shared with the cloud governor, open in the follow-up `2026-11-14-2029-cloud-governor-climbing-average-baseline-followup.md`.
- **A step that bought its time**: a thinned share whose average stayed under `slowMs` for `fastS` of counted frames has bought it (one fast frame, a short pause in the scene, does not). A later slow verdict at the half is a new one: it steps on to a quarter without the test and remembers that average as a second baseline. The quarter is then worthwhile against either baseline, so a denser district (half at 70 ms, a quarter at 55, full once at 60) keeps its quarter, and a flat slowdown whatever the woods (full 70-150 ms, then 52-65 ms) stays at a quarter while the later frame is under 85 % of the full woods' and comes back with a rest above it, which is the old judgement against the full woods. A quarter that had bought its time and gets slow has no rung left and keeps that purchase until it steps up, even when the later frame is above 85 % of the full woods'. Chosen by measurement: in a heat sweep (full 30-150 ms, woods 30-80 % of the frame, the whole frame slowing 1.2-2.5 times over 60-600 s, 900 s, 252 cases) re-testing the quarter ended 56 cases at full woods with a mean 225 s slow at full; holding it ended 43 there with 138 s (main: 102 and 294 s). A wider grid (1980 cases, 30 min) gives 529 and 425 s against 452 and 283 s. The cost: a quarter slow for another reason is not re-tested before it steps up. **A rung re-entered from below counts as bought**: the rung under it paid, so a half that a fast quarter climbed back to steps down again to the quarter when it is slow, never to the full woods for a rest (swept over full 60-80 ms, a later half at 55-90 ms and quarter at 20-34 ms for 20 minutes).
- **No popping**: a rung left for being too slow is not returned to within `restS` of leaving it. After that it is tried again (a phone that cooled down gets its woods back).
- Rests run on the clock passed in, so idle time counts toward them; the verdicts run on counted frames only.
- `auto: false` (desktop, or `woodsauto=off`) never moves the share, and still measures (`averageMs`), so a readout shows the desktop's frame time too.
- **Its origin**: the rule is modelled on a cloud quality governor the globe package once had, removed with the cloud renderer it stepped, so this is now the only copy. From it come the smoothed interval, a lone frame over `gapMs` ignored and a pair counted, the baseline taken when the first step leaves the full set, and the worthwhile test against it with a `restS` rest. What this one added is listed above (chained frames only, the verdict and average limits on a long frame, a fresh average per rung that skips the step's own frame, and the purchase).
- **A pair inside the first verdict's window is an accepted limit**: a pair of long frames from about 1.25 s into a session, on a device over the line whose woods are cheap (5-10 % of a 55-70 ms frame), lands in the average the first verdict takes as the full-woods baseline, so the thinning looks worthwhile and stays. Main thins all 24 of the test grid's cases at 1.5-1.9 s and 8 at 1.25 s; this governor thins 24, and 4 at 1.25 s (70 ms with woods at 10 %). With 5 % jitter the window opens earlier: of 180 cases (full 52-80 ms, woods 5-12 %, three seeds) a pair at 1.0 s thins 13 against main's 0, at 1.25 s 77 against 48 and at 0.25-0.5 s 1 against 0. The fix would be a different baseline, not the limit: a limit of 2 holds there and misses 14 of 27 stutters.
- **What would reverse a verdict**: the parameters are `slowMs` 50 (the owner's number), `fastMs` 36, `slowS` 2, `fastS` 12, `worthwhile` 0.15, `restS` 300, `gapMs` 250, `spikeLimit` 4 (its margin above: 3 hides stutter, 4.5 lets the cheap-woods pairs through) and the seed's three frames (above: two rest slow devices, four rest more under jitter). Swept at `spikeLimit` 3, 3.5, 4.5 and 5, every seed row above is unchanged; only the pairs at the session's start (8-16, 12-29, 29-45 and 35-57 thin, against 18-36 at 4) and the stutters (44, 32, 20 and 18 never thin, against 28) move, which is the limit's own trade. The sweeps in the tests show that a steady 34-49 ms never steps down and 51-300 ms steps down at 2-3 s (the average starts at the first interval, so a steady frame above the line is slow from its first counted frame); after a thinning and its rest, 16.7-35 ms (a 30 Hz cap's 33.3 ms included) gives the woods back and 37-49 ms keeps the thinned share. `fastMs` 36 sits above a 30 Hz cap and well below the 50 ms rule, so a share is not given back on a device just under the line; a value under 33.3 would strand a capped phone at the thinned share for good. No phone has been measured; the readout (`?perf=1` in OsmDemo) is what the owner reads on his.
- **Where its state lives**: the per-frame state (the average, the slow and fast seconds, the baseline) stays module-local on purpose. It changes every frame, and dispatching it to the app's Redux store would cost the frame time the governor exists to save. Only its outward result (`share`, `mode`) would belong in the store, once a real HUD control shows it; that is deferred to the HUD redesign.

## Examples

```ts
import { applyWoodsShare, createWoodsGovernor } from "gps-plus-slam-osm/three";

const governor = createWoodsGovernor({
  auto: matchMedia("(pointer: coarse)").matches,
});
let share = 1;
// in the frame callback, before the render:
governor.frame(timestamp, chained);
const next = governor.state().share;
if (next !== share) applyWoodsShare(group.children, (share = next));
```
