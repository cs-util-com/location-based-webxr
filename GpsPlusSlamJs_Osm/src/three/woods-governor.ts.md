# `three/woods-governor.ts`

## Purpose

On a phone too slow for the invented woods, draw fewer of them: the owner's rule "above 50 ms a frame the library lowers the density on the phone automatically, the desktop stays full". Pure: no three.js, no clock of its own.

## Public API

- `WOODS_GOVERNOR`: the thresholds, frozen. `slowMs` 50, `fastMs` 36, `slowS` 2, `fastS` 12 (counted seconds), `worthwhile` 0.15, `restS` 300, `smoothing` 0.1, `gapMs` 250, `spikeLimit` 4, `shares` `[1, 0.5, 0.25]`.
- `createWoodsGovernor({ auto = true })` returns:
  - `frame(atMs, chained)`: one frame drawn at `atMs` (a monotonic clock in ms, e.g. the rAF timestamp); `chained` when it was requested from within the previous frame's callback. A non-finite time, or one not after the last, is ignored.
  - `state()`: `{ share, mode, averageMs }`. `share` is one of the ladder; `mode` is `off` (auto off), `full`, `thinned` or `resting` (full after a step that bought nothing); `averageMs` is the smoothed counted interval, undefined before the first.
- Types `WoodsGovernor`, `WoodsGovernorState`, `WoodsGovernorMode`.

The share is applied by `applyWoodsShare` (`city-objects.ts`).

## Invariants & assumptions

- **Only chained frames are measured.** A consumer that draws on demand (OsmDemo) would otherwise read every idle pause as a slow frame and strip the woods on every phone. The first frame of a burst sets the clock and measures nothing. A consumer with a continuous loop passes `chained: true` every frame.
- **One frame longer than `gapMs` says nothing** (a hitch, a tab coming back mid-burst); two in a row do, or a device at a quarter-second a frame would never step down. A counted interval credits the slow, fast and bought seconds at most `gapMs`, and enters the average at most `spikeLimit` (4) times the average: the second of two long hitches cannot fill the two slow seconds, neither by itself nor by the seconds an inflated average takes to decay on a device just under the line (swept over devices at 16.7, 33.3, 40, 45, 47 and 49 ms and second hitches of 0.4 s to an hour; a limit of 16 thins 5 of those 36 pairs, 2 and 8 thin none). **A fresh average** (the session's first counted frame, and the first after every step) **starts at the smaller of its first two counted frames**, unlimited: a device at 300-2000 ms a frame still reads as slow as it is from its first counted frames, so the worthwhile test sees what thinning saved, and a long frame among the first two cannot become the start the limit then defends. Starting at the first frame alone thinned 17 of 40 near-line devices hit by a pair in a session's first two chained intervals (45 ms from a second frame of 400 ms, 40 ms from 2 s, 33.3 ms from 5 s; the half sits above `fastMs` and never comes back), and sent 30 of 48 paying halves hit by a pair just after the step to full woods with a rest; starting at the smaller sends none (swept with the pair after none, one or two normal counted frames). Capping the average at `gapMs` instead made every step on a slow device look worthless.
- **The limit's margin is narrow**, and it is a trade in both directions. A higher limit lets more hitch pairs through: the cheap-woods test grid below first fails at 4.5 (4 of 48 end thinned) and at 5; with 0-10 % jitter, of 2016 pairs on devices at 16.7-49.5 ms, 16 thin at 3, 25 at 4, 35 at 4.5 and 41 at 5, against 44 on main. A lower limit hides real stutter: of 123 regular stutters with a mean over 52 ms (every 2nd-10th frame 60-245 ms on 8.3-40 ms devices), 16 never thin with no limit or at 5 and above (their smoothed average swings around the line), 22 at 4, 26 at 3.5 and 35 at 3, and at 3 a 16.7 ms device with every 4th frame at 200-245 ms (a mean of 63-74 ms) keeps its full woods for good. 4 is the value both tests hold at; the six stutters that 4 misses and main does not (every 4th-10th frame at 245 ms, means of 60-67 ms) are an accepted limit.
- **The trade the limit makes**: a run of long frames on a fast device now climbs the average by at most four times a step, so a run of 260-3000 ms frames on a 16.7 ms device thins after 13 frames (main took 10), and a sudden steady slowdown from 16.7 ms to 260 ms thins after 3.4 s against 2.6 s, to 1000 ms after 13 s against 10 s; from 33.3 or 45 ms the delay is at most a second. A device that genuinely runs at seconds a frame from the start thins as before, because its fresh average starts at its own frames.
- The smoothed interval above `slowMs` for `slowS` of counted frames steps one rung down; under `fastMs` for `fastS` steps one rung up. The ladder ends at 0.25: never no woods.
- **Each rung is judged by its own frames**: at a step the average starts afresh at the next counted frames (the smaller of the first two). A device at a second a frame has eight counted frames a verdict, too few for the smoothing to forget the rung before. The interval right after a step is the step's own frame (the woods re-sort their draw order) and counts toward nothing: neither the fresh average nor a verdict starts at it (swept over full woods at 60-100 ms, a half at 60-84 % of that and a post-step frame of 80-250 ms: no verdict changes; counting it, a fresh average at a second a frame carries about 43 % of it). When the re-sort takes two frames, the second is the fresh average's second frame and the smaller of the two is kept, so two post-step frames 1.1-5 times as long change no verdict either (without that, 118 of 300 cases flipped); three such frames are not covered.
- **The worthwhile test**: from the second slow verdict on, if the average is above `(1 - worthwhile)` of the average when the thinning began (the baseline, taken only at the full woods), the woods were not the cost; the share returns to 1 and the step-down rests `restS`. A 30 Hz cap or a saving mode therefore costs one thinning try every five minutes. On a slow device the half rung saves half the woods' share of the frame, so woods at 30 % of the frame or more thin to a quarter and at 20 % come back and rest (swept 150-2000 ms).
- **A step that bought its time**: a thinned share whose average stayed under `slowMs` for `fastS` of counted frames has bought it (one fast frame, a short pause in the scene, does not). A later slow verdict at the half is a new one: it steps on to a quarter without the test and remembers that average as a second baseline. The quarter is then worthwhile against either baseline, so a denser district (half at 70 ms, a quarter at 55, full once at 60) keeps its quarter, and a flat slowdown whatever the woods (full 70-150 ms, then 52-65 ms) stays at a quarter while the later frame is under 85 % of the full woods' and comes back with a rest above it, which is the old judgement against the full woods. A quarter that had bought its time and gets slow has no rung left and keeps that purchase until it steps up, even when the later frame is above 85 % of the full woods'. Chosen by measurement: in a heat sweep (full 30-150 ms, woods 30-80 % of the frame, the whole frame slowing 1.2-2.5 times over 60-600 s, 900 s, 252 cases) re-testing the quarter ended 56 cases at full woods with a mean 225 s slow at full; holding it ended 43 there with 138 s (main: 102 and 294 s). A wider grid (1980 cases, 30 min) gives 529 and 425 s against 452 and 283 s. The cost: a quarter slow for another reason is not re-tested before it steps up. **A rung re-entered from below counts as bought**: the rung under it paid, so a half that a fast quarter climbed back to steps down again to the quarter when it is slow, never to the full woods for a rest (swept over full 60-80 ms, a later half at 55-90 ms and quarter at 20-34 ms for 20 minutes).
- **No popping**: a rung left for being too slow is not returned to within `restS` of leaving it. After that it is tried again (a phone that cooled down gets its woods back).
- Rests run on the clock passed in, so idle time counts toward them; the verdicts run on counted frames only.
- `auto: false` (desktop, or `woodsauto=off`) never moves the share, and still measures (`averageMs`), so a readout shows the desktop's frame time too.
- **A deliberate copy**: the rule is modelled on the near clouds' governor, `GpsPlusSlamJs_Globe/src/globe-cloud-governor.ts`, which this package cannot import. They share the smoothed interval, a lone frame over `gapMs` ignored and a pair counted, the baseline taken when the first step leaves the full set, and the worthwhile test against it with a `restS` rest; they differ in what they measure (chained frames only), what they step and their thresholds. What this one adds to that rule is listed above (the verdict and average limits on a long frame, a fresh average per rung that skips the step's own frame, and the purchase); compare the two files for the cloud governor's current state rather than trusting a list here. A change to either is weighed for the other.
- **A pair inside the first verdict's window is an accepted limit**: a pair of long frames from about 1.25 s into a session, on a device over the line whose woods are cheap (5-10 % of a 55-70 ms frame), lands in the average the first verdict takes as the full-woods baseline, so the thinning looks worthwhile and stays. Main thins all 24 of the test grid's cases at 1.5-1.9 s and 8 at 1.25 s; this governor thins 24, and 4 at 1.25 s (70 ms with woods at 10 %). The fix would be a different baseline, not the limit: a limit of 2 holds there and misses 14 of 27 stutters.
- **What would reverse a verdict**: the parameters are `slowMs` 50 (the owner's number), `fastMs` 36, `slowS` 2, `fastS` 12, `worthwhile` 0.15, `restS` 300, `gapMs` 250 and `spikeLimit` 4 (its margin above: 3 hides stutter, 4.5 lets the cheap-woods pairs through). The sweeps in the tests show that a steady 34-49 ms never steps down and 51-300 ms steps down at 2-3 s (the average starts at the first interval, so a steady frame above the line is slow from its first counted frame); after a thinning and its rest, 16.7-35 ms (a 30 Hz cap's 33.3 ms included) gives the woods back and 37-49 ms keeps the thinned share. `fastMs` 36 sits above a 30 Hz cap and well below the 50 ms rule, so a share is not given back on a device just under the line; a value under 33.3 would strand a capped phone at the thinned share for good. No phone has been measured; the readout (`?perf=1` in OsmDemo) is what the owner reads on his.
- **Where its state lives**: the per-frame state (the average, the slow and fast seconds, the baseline) stays module-local on purpose. It changes every frame, and dispatching it to the app's Redux store would cost the frame time the governor exists to save. Only its outward result (`share`, `mode`) would belong in the store, once a real HUD control shows it; that is deferred to the HUD redesign.

## Examples

```ts
import { applyWoodsShare, createWoodsGovernor } from "gps-plus-slam-osm/three";

const governor = createWoodsGovernor({
  auto: matchMedia("(pointer: coarse)").matches,
});
let share = 1;
// in the frame callback, before the render:
governor.frame(timestamp, chained);
const next = governor.state().share;
if (next !== share) applyWoodsShare(group.children, (share = next));
```

## Tests

- `woods-governor.test.ts`: steady 60 ms steps down after about 2 s; on to a quarter while each step buys time; not worthwhile brings the woods back and rests; a half that had bought its time and gets slow later steps to a quarter, and a quarter in that case stays a quarter; a quarter that pays against the half's later slowness stays; a share has bought its time only after `fastS` under the line; two long frames credit at most `gapMs` each to the verdicts and at most `spikeLimit` times the average to the average; a 30 Hz phone gets the woods back after the rest; a single 250 ms frame and a single 3 s chained frame do nothing; idle gaps between 60 fps bursts never step down while a slow burst does; unchained frames measure nothing; no re-entry within the rest, re-entry after it; auto off never steps; a step back after 12 s of fast frames once the rest is over; non-finite and backwards times are ignored; the step-down swept over steady 34-300 ms frames (2-3 s above the line); the step-up after a rest swept over steady 16.7-49 ms frames; slow devices swept over 150-2000 ms frames with woods 20-80 % of the frame; the half rung's later slowdown swept over full-woods baselines 60-150 ms, later frames 52-65 ms and 30 or 120 s; two hitches in a row on devices at 16.7-49 ms swept from 0.4 s to an hour; a long-frame pair before the first verdict on cheap woods at 0.25-1 s; a long-frame pair at the start of a session and right after a step; a regular stutter over the line thins (pins `spikeLimit` against 3); the frame right after a step, and two long ones, change no verdict, and slow devices reach the quarter with longer post-step frames; a quarter climbing back to a slow half never returns to full woods; a quarter that bought its time keeps it when it gets slow later, above the worthwhile line too.
- `woods-governor.property.test.ts`: the share stays on the ladder and moves one rung or back to full; deterministic; auto off and unchained-only streams never move it.
