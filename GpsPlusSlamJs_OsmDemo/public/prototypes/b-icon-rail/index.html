About this variant

Direction. A rail of six labelled tools sits at the edge of the 3D view, on the seam to the map. Each tool opens one card, and cards always open over the map, never over the 3D view, so the view you are judging stays clear while you change it.

Visible by default: the rail (Place, Analysis, World, Look, Sun, Debug), a small "now" plate under it (category and score ramp, sun time, the quest distance once found, perf when on, and any work in progress), the map with its zoom, locate, AR and attribution, the legal link.

Folded: every card. One card at a time; the same rail button, Escape, the close button or a tap outside closes it. On desktop a pin keeps a card open while you click in the 3D view or the map. The light settings open as a card in the same place, with a back arrow to Sun.

Where things go: desktop - rail on the 3D view's left edge, cards over the map's right part, details at the 3D view's top right, toast and AR offer at the 3D view's bottom. Phone portrait - rail on the right edge of the 3D band, cards fill the map sheet (drag the grab bar to give a card more room), details use the same place. Phone landscape - the map moves to a right-side panel and the rail sits on its seam.

Work in progress stays visible: the control shows its own "Applying..." and is disabled; the rail button gets a pulsing dot; the now plate shows one line ("Applying colour scheme...", "Loading cells...", "Finding quests..."). All three survive a closed card.

Inapplicable controls are shown disabled with a reason ("below threshold - needs cells") instead of hidden, so nothing shifts.

Test helpers it would need: one openCard(page, name) for place, analysis, world, look, sun, debug, plus light. Every id and accessible name stays (site, category, layer-*, ground-mode, render-distance, sun-*, light-*, geo-event*, legend, status), except the colour scheme, which becomes a group of radio buttons named by the scheme. Text reads of #status work while its card is shut.

Weaknesses, plainly:

  • Cells, used every session, is two taps away (Analysis, then cells); the now plate only reports it.
  • Icons need their labels; the labels make the desktop rail 116 px wide.
  • While a card is open on the phone the map, locate and AR are hidden; one tap on the rail button brings them back.
  • Phone cards are only as tall as the map sheet: on a 360x640 phone the Look and Light cards scroll.
  • Six topics is the limit for the phone rail at 640 px tall; a seventh needs a second column or a merge.
  • Phone landscape moves the map to the right side, which is a layout change beyond the HUD.
